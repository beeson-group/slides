# Reference

Each element of the theme `beeson` is described here in full, with the formats of its files. The start of a talk is given in the README, and the vocabulary of the theme in `AGENTS.md`.

## Slides

Give a section slide `data-run` to show another text in the running head of its section, e.g. its heading without the math. Give a slide `data-visibility="hidden"` to leave it out of the show. Such a slide is removed from the page of the show, and it is kept in the file and in the editor. The next slide is faded in over 400 ms by default. With `data-transition="none"` on a slide, the next one is shown at once, and with `data-auto-animate` on both slides, the elements with the same `data-id` are moved between them. An element whose `translate` or `rotate` differs between the two slides is glided from its old place and angle to the new ones. With `data-transition="fade-in slide-out"` on a slide and `data-transition="slide-in fade-out"` on the next one, the slide is pushed out to the left by the next one, and a step back over it is a fade. Write the seconds planned for a slide in its `data-timing`, which is read in the pacing of the speaker view and in the times of the scrubber.

In the editor, the running head is written with the field Running Head of the Slide pane, and a hidden slide with Hide Slide in the Slide menu or with the box Hidden in the show. Choose the transition with None, Fade, Magic Move, or Push at the head of the Steps pane or in Slide > Transition. With Magic Move, the matched elements of the two slides are given one `data-id`. A line, a curve, or an elbow is matched to one of the same tone and arrowheads, hence a line with arrowheads is not matched to one without them. A shape is matched to a shape of the same classes, apart from the classes of its step and its effect, hence a shape is matched to its copy when one of the two has a step effect. At the end of Rehearse Timings, press Keep Timings to write the seconds spent on each slide into its `data-timing`.

## Steps and modes

Give each element its step once the slide is complete: `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. During the talk, a new element is faded in, and the earlier ones are dimmed to 45 percent. The last element is also shown alone at first, and all of them are restored to full opacity at one more step, which is added to every slide with a dimmed element. A slide whose fragments are empty, e.g. the steps of a plot, is not given this step.

Give a fragment the class `semi-fade-out` to dim it to 25 percent from its step on, as the emphasis Transparency of PowerPoint. Give any element the class `faint` to draw it at 25 percent throughout, also when it is a step and in the print view. In the editor, choose Dim as the effect of a step in the Steps pane to write `semi-fade-out`. A dimmed step is drawn at full opacity on the slide that you edit, and dimmed in Preview Steps, in Preview Transition, in the presenter display, and in the show. Turn on Ghosted in the Arrange pane to write `faint` on the selected elements, and Reset Slide keeps the class.

A step can be shown by itself after a delay, as a step that starts After Previous in PowerPoint. Give a fragment of the step before it `data-autoslide` with the delay in whole milliseconds. With `data-autoslide` on the last step, the deck is moved on to the next slide after the delay. Press a key during a delay to show the next step at once, and the later delays are still played. When you go back, the steps reached by a delay are skipped up to the last step reached by a key. Press A to pause and resume the timed steps. A slide with a delay on its last step is not given the step that restores the dimmed fragments, and the look of its last step is kept through the transition. Such a slide is marked with `data-moves-on` when the deck is started, and the attribute is not written in the file. In the Steps pane of the editor, choose After Previous as the start of a step and type the delay in seconds, at least 0.1, or step it by 0.1 s with the arrows of the field. The `data-autoslide` is then written on the first fragment of the step before.

You flip the mode with the key `d` or with the button at the upper right. The change is drawn as a circle at the button or at the point of the click, grown on a flip to light and shrunk on a flip to dark. During a flip, a backdrop that is being traced is drawn on in both modes. A viewer of tycho, a surface, a plot, or a video is held still in the old mode for up to 450 ms, until the new mode is shown over it. The mode is kept per browser in local storage. To fix it for a venue, add the URL parameter `?theme=light`. To draw every backdrop complete at once for a screenshot, add `?still`.

## The editor

You can change the words, the formulas, the speaker notes, the steps, and the places of the figures of a deck on the slides themselves, without opening `index.html` in a text editor. The editor is loaded only when the parameter `?edit` is in the address of the deck, and a deck without it does not load any of it. The page is laid out like the normal view of PowerPoint. The title bar at the top is made of the menus, the name of the file with its state after it in a muted color, and the Autosave switch. In a window too narrow for this row, the name of the file and its state are cut first, and Which? and Autosave are then drawn without their names. Under it is the toolbar, with the buttons View, Zoom, Undo, Redo, Add Slide, Table, Text, Formula, Shape, Media, Preview, Present, Save, Format, and Steps. In a window too narrow for the toolbar, its groups are folded from the right into the button More, apart from the first group and the group of Format, and their buttons are listed in the menu of More. A folded button with a list, i.e. Shape, Media, Preview, or View in a customized toolbar, is a submenu of More with the items of its arrow. Open the submenu with a hover or the right arrow, and close it with the left arrow or Esc. Navigator is the first item of the submenu of View, before a rule, and while a preview plays, Play Slide in the submenu of Preview is named Stop. Trace Backdrop and Play Steps are thus reached with the keyboard in a window 760 px wide as well. The list of the slides is at the left, the slide in the middle, a pane for the notes under it, and a pane for the steps at the right. The status bar at the bottom is made of the slide number, the buttons Slides, Steps, and Notes, and the zoom. Drag the zoom slider, pinch, or turn the wheel with Ctrl to scale the slide, and the backdrops are drawn sharp again once the gesture ends. The slide is zoomed by the whole movement of a pinch, also of a slow one, and the grabbed point of a drag is kept under the pointer through a pinch. In a window narrower than 760 px, the zoom slider of the status bar is hidden.

1. Open `index.html` in a browser, with a double click or from a web server, and press E, which opens the same slide in the editor, or add `?edit` to the end of the address, e.g. `file:///Users/you/talk/index.html?edit`. When the address ends in a slide such as `#/3`, put `?edit` before the `#`.
2. After a double click, a dialog asks you to choose the file of the deck, since a file on your disk is not readable by a page until you choose it. Choose the same `index.html`, whose folder is named on the dialog. From a web server, the file is read by the editor itself, and the dialog is not shown.
3. Click any text and type. Enter starts a new line in the same block, a paste is inserted as plain text with an item for each line pasted into a list item, and the arrow keys move within the text. One word copied with the space after it, as a double click takes it on Windows, is pasted with its spaces set as in PowerPoint, except inside a word and in code. The space after the word is then dropped before a space, a closing mark, or the end of the text, and a space is put before the word after a letter, a digit, or a closing mark. In a text outside a list and a table, Tab and Shift+Tab do not leave the text, and they stop only at the formulas of the block. Press Cmd+B or Cmd+I on a Mac, and Ctrl+B or Ctrl+I elsewhere, to make the selected words bold or italic, which are written as `<strong>` and `<em>`, and press the same keys again to take the format off. You can also type the marks of Markdown. A span closed by the mark that opened it, i.e. `**bold**` or `__bold__`, `*italic*` or `_italic_`, `` `code` ``, or `~~struck~~`, is written as `<strong>`, `<em>`, `<code>`, or `<s>` when you type its closing mark, and the text typed after it is outside the format. One undo gives back the marks as typed. An underscore inside a word, as in snake_case, is left as typed, and so are marks typed in code and in TeX. Markdown Formatting is switched off and on in Edit > Substitutions, next to Smart Quotes. On a Mac, Control with A, B, D, E, F, H, K, L, N, O, P, T, V, or Y is left to the text editing of macOS in a text and in the notes. Control-D thus deletes forward, and it is not taken as Cmd with the letter. Press Esc to leave the text. The block is then selected, as a text box in PowerPoint, the arrow keys nudge it, and a second Esc deselects it, after which the arrow keys and the buttons in the status bar move between the slides again. The keys of reveal.js for the overview, the black screen, fullscreen, and the speaker view are off in the editor. Every step of a slide is shown at once, and the backdrops are drawn still while you edit. On a slide with an element that is hidden at a step, e.g. with `fade-out`, `current-visible`, or `fade-in-then-out`, the steps are cut into layers at each step at which an element is hidden. The slide is then shown one layer at a time, as it is at the last step of the layer, and you choose the layer with the buttons after Shows in the status bar. A text that runs past the edge of the slide, or whose lines are drawn over a picture, a video, or a plot of its slide, e.g. after a change of the layout, is outlined in orange. The number of such texts is shown in the state in the title bar.
4. Press Cmd+Z on a Mac and Ctrl+Z elsewhere to undo the last change, whether it is typing, a formula, a step, or a move, and Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y to redo it. The Undo and Redo buttons in the toolbar do the same. An undo shows the slide of the change and puts the caret where the text changed, and once you have undone every change, the deck is marked as saved again. In a number field of the inspector with a typed value that is not entered yet, Cmd+Z or Ctrl+Z puts the shown value back and selects it.
5. Move between the slides with the arrow keys, the buttons in the status bar, or a click in the list at the left, where each slide is named by its title and its layout. Rest the pointer on a row to see a small copy of its slide beside the list, with every step shown. A backdrop is drawn in the copy once its slide has been shown. Press G and type the number of a slide to jump to it. Press the Slides button to hide the list, and the choice is kept between visits. Click the View button at the left of the toolbar to show or hide the list as well. In the menu of its arrow are Slide Sorter, Rulers, and Gridlines.
6. Click a formula, or reach it with Tab and press Enter, to open its TeX in a field under it, with its rendering under the TeX as you type. Apply, or Cmd+Enter on a Mac and Ctrl+Enter elsewhere, typesets it again, and Esc leaves it as it was. A TeX with an error is refused with the message of MathJax, an empty TeX is refused as well, and the slide is not touched.
7. Press Notes to show the speaker notes of the slide in the pane under it, and type into them. On a slide without notes, you can type into the empty pane, and the notes are saved once they have text. Drag the strip above the pane, or focus it with Tab and press the up and down arrows, to change its height, which is kept between visits.
8. Press Steps to open the pane at the right, where the elements of the slide are listed in reading order with the step at which each appears, as the animation pane of PowerPoint and the build order of Keynote. A formula is named in the list by its TeX. Choose the step of an element in its select, or choose Step 1, new, before the others to show it ahead of the other steps, which are each moved one step later. Choose an effect for an element shown with the slide, e.g. Dim, or Play for a video, to make it a new last step with this effect. Press One by one to show the elements one after the other and All at once to show them with the slide. Elements with the same step appear together, and the number of the step is shown at the upper left of each stepped element on the slide. A heading and the footnote can be given a step too, and One by one leaves them with the slide. A step is written into the file as a reveal.js fragment, i.e. `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. Each group of lines of a code box after the first is listed as a row at its step, and the row is moved with the other steps, while its lines are changed in the Code pane. Choose a row and press Play From, or choose Play From Step in the menu of a row, to play the steps of the slide in place from this step.
9. Drag a figure to move it, and drag a paragraph, a list, a formula, or a table by the handle ⠿ at its upper left, since a click into the text edits it. While you drag, the element snaps to the edges and the centers of the other elements of the slide, of the slide itself, and of its own place, and an orange guide shows the line it is aligned with. Hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to drag freely. Hold Option on a Mac, or Ctrl elsewhere, to drag a copy of the element, and hold Shift to keep the drag on the axis along which you moved farther. A copy made by such a drag, by Duplicate, or by a paste on the same slide leaves the other elements placed with `translate` where they are drawn. A duplicate or a paste of a block in the flow of the slide or of its text column is written right after its source in the file, e.g. for a paragraph, a list, or a table. The place of the copy is written in the `translate` of its `style`. A `translate` is then written on each block that the copy would push, so that the other blocks are drawn where they were. On a slide without the sources, a paste is put at the place of the sources. Where this place is taken by a copy of the same clipboard on this slide, the paste is put at the first free place in steps of 20 px down and to the right of it. On the slide of the sources, a paste that would be drawn on its source is put 20 px down and to the right of it, and each later paste one step of 20 px further. Such a paste is then moved on in the same steps past every source and every copy of the same clipboard on the slide. A duplicate is put 20 px down and to the right of its element, and moved on in the same steps past the element and its older duplicates. The place of a copy that is deleted or undone is free for a later paste or duplicate. The arrow keys nudge the selected element by one slide pixel, or by ten with Shift. Nudges less than a second apart are undone together with one Cmd+Z. While you move the element, its offset is shown in slide pixels at the lower right of its frame. A double click on the handle, or Enter on it, puts the element back. A figure is also reached with Tab, which selects it. With an object selected and no text edited, press Tab to select the next object in the order of the Object List, from back to front, and Shift+Tab to select the one before, around both ends. The offset is written into the tag of the element as the style `translate`, in slide pixels, and nothing else in the layout of the slide is changed. An element turned with its rotate handle or in the Arrange pane is written with the style `rotate`, in whole degrees from 1 to 359. A line with an attached end is the exception, as described under Lines. While you drag the rotate handle, the angle is held at a multiple of 45 degrees within 4 degrees of it, and an orange guide is drawn through the center of the turn while it is held. Hold Shift to turn in steps of 15 degrees, and Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to turn freely. The turn of a group is held at the angle of its first member, and the turn of a selection of several elements at the angle of the element whose frame has the handle. The other elements are turned by the same amount. Rotate Left 90° and Rotate Right 90° in the Arrange menu turn each selected element about its center, and a group about the center of its frame. A group is turned as one change of the history, also a group with a line. When you bring an element to the front or send it backward in the Arrange menu, an element out of the flow, e.g. a figure, is moved before or after the others in the file where this gives the order. Otherwise it is given the style `z-index`, as an element in the flow is, e.g. a paragraph, with `position: relative` where the theme does not position it, so that the layout of the slide is not changed. These styles are taken off again once the slide is drawn in the same order without them. A layer command does not put an element under a backdrop or a plate over the middle of the element, and it is dimmed where it would. A figure is turned at its picture, from the figure and from the picture selected by a second click. The `rotate` is then written on its image or video, on both images of a pair for the two modes, and the caption is left where it is.
10. Press Save, or Cmd+S on a Mac and Ctrl+S elsewhere. In Chrome and Edge, each edit is written into its place in `index.html`, and every other byte of the file is kept, hence a diff of the file shows only your changes. From a web server, the first save asks where to write the file. Open with unsaved changes asks whether to save them first, to open a file without them, or to go on editing. Save as, or Cmd+Shift+S and Ctrl+Shift+S, writes the deck into another file, and the next saves go into it. Firefox, Safari, and the browsers built on them do not have the file pickers of Chrome, hence a save there is a download of a copy of `index.html` with your edits, and you replace the original with it. To choose the place of the copy at each save, set the browser to ask where to save each download: in Safari under Settings, General, File download location, and in Firefox under Settings, General, Downloads. In the dialog, choose `index.html` in the folder of the deck and replace it.
11. Press Autosave to save after each change, as the AutoSave switch of PowerPoint. The file is written 1.5 s after the last change, once it has been chosen, and the switch is kept between visits. In every browser, the unsaved edits are also kept in the storage of the browser, and when the tab is closed or the browser crashes before a save, a dialog offers them the next time the deck is opened: Restore and save writes them into the file and shows the page again, Download a copy puts the file with them into your downloads, and Discard forgets them.
12. Press Present to play the saved deck in place, in a frame over the editor and in full screen where the browser grants it. Press Esc or `e`, or move on past the last step of the last slide, to return to the editor at the slide shown last. With unsaved changes, a dialog asks whether to save them first, to present the saved file without them, or to go on editing.
13. Edit the deck while an agent or a collaborator changes `index.html`, e.g. in a text editor or with a git pull. The editor reads the file once a second, through the file you chose or from the web server of the deck. Without unsaved edits, the page is loaded again on the same slide, and the changed slides are marked with a violet dot next to their thumbnails until you show them. With unsaved edits, the state names the changed slides, and the next save merges your edits into the file and loads the page again with both. When you edited a text that the file changed too, a dialog asks whether to keep your version or the file's, and Cancel saves nothing. After the page is loaded again, the editor writes into the same file without asking for it, while the browser lets the page write the file.

Press Preview, before Present in the toolbar, to play the current slide on the canvas without leaving the editor. Its backdrop is traced once, up to its last trajectory also where the trajectories start one after another, and then its steps are shown one per second, as the show draws them. In the menu of its arrow are Play Slide, Trace Backdrop, and Play Steps, to play the whole slide or one of its two parts, and Select Backdrop and Add Backdrop…. The first three are also in the Play menu. An entry is dimmed on a slide without what it plays, and Add Backdrop… on a slide that has a backdrop. Play Slide, Trace Backdrop, and Play Steps are dimmed while Slide Sorter is shown, and Trace Backdrop on a layer that hides the backdrop. With Play Slide on such a layer, only the steps are played. While a preview is played, the button is named Stop. Click it, press Esc, press outside the toolbar, change the slide, open Slide Sorter, or edit the deck to stop the preview. The file, the history, the selection, the caret, and the focus are kept as they were, also at Esc. While a preview is played, the frame, the grips, and the handles of the selection are not drawn, and neither are the bars of a table, the guides, the band of the selection on the rulers, and the caret. All of them are drawn again at its end. With the caret in a text, a key typed into the text stops the preview before it is written. A zoom key does not stop it, and the preview is played on at the new zoom.

Select two elements or more and choose Group in the Arrange menu, Cmd+Alt+G on a Mac and Ctrl+G elsewhere, to select and drag them together. Each member is then given a name that is not used elsewhere in the deck in its `data-group`, e.g. `data-group="g1"`. A group can be put into another group, and the names of an element are then listed from the outermost group to the innermost, e.g. `data-group="g2 g1"` on a member of g1 inside g2. A copy of a group is given new names at every level, and a copy made inside an entered group is a new group of the slide. A level with the same copies as the level inside it is dropped, and an element copied alone is not given a group. A click on a member selects its outermost group. A second click on a member of the selected group enters this group and selects the member, or the inner group in which it is, and the entered group is framed with a dimmed dashed line. Inside an entered group, click a member with Shift to add it to the selection. Align, Distribute, the layer commands, Delete, and a drag are then applied to the selected members. Press Esc, or Cmd+Shift+A on a Mac and Ctrl+Shift+A elsewhere, to leave one level and select the group that you left. With Ungroup, Cmd+Alt+Shift+G or Ctrl+Shift+G, the name of the selected group is taken off each of its members, and on a member selected inside an entered group, the entered group is taken apart, as in PowerPoint. With Regroup, Cmd+Alt+J or Ctrl+Shift+J, the groups of the last Ungroup are put back, as long as their members are still on the slide as Ungroup left them. Each member then gets back its whole list in `data-group`, with its outer groups. On Windows and Linux, the browser may take Ctrl+Shift+J for its console, and you then choose Regroup in the Arrange menu. Choose Lock, Cmd+L or Ctrl+L, to keep an element in its place. The element is then given `data-locked`, and it is not moved, resized, or rotated with the pointer, the keys, or the fields, while its text can still be edited. A locked element is framed in gray when it is selected, and the attribute is taken off with Unlock, Cmd+Alt+L or Ctrl+Shift+L. Both attributes are read only in the editor and do not have an effect in the show, hence you can also write them by hand.

Right-click an element to open its menu, with Bring to Front, Bring Forward, Send Backward, Send to Back, Rotate Left 90°, Rotate Right 90°, and the submenus Group (Group, Ungroup, and Regroup), Align, and Distribute. On Windows and Linux, press Ctrl+] for Bring Forward, Ctrl+[ for Send Backward, Ctrl+Shift+] for Bring to Front, and Ctrl+Shift+[ for Send to Back, besides the keys with the arrows. In a list item, Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[ still change the level of the item. Select All on a slide selects its lines with its other elements.

Choose Object List in the View menu, Cmd+Shift+L on a Mac and Alt+F10 elsewhere, to list the elements of the current slide under the thumbnails, from front to back, as the object list of Keynote. Each row is named by the kind of its element and the first words of its text, e.g. Text: The lead-in of a display. The members of a group are listed under a row of the group, which you fold with its arrow. An inner group is listed as a row under its group, at any depth, and it is folded and dragged as one row. The backdrop of the slide is the last row, named by its drawing, e.g. Backdrop: Lyapunov Families, and this row is not dragged. Click a row to select its element, and hold Shift or Cmd, or Ctrl elsewhere, to add the row to the selection or take it off. A selection on the slide is shown in the list as well. Press the padlock of a row to lock or unlock its element, and drag a row up or down to bring its element forward or send it backward, by the layers of Bring Forward and Send Backward. The row of a group is dragged as one element, as one change of the history, and its members keep their order. A row that these commands cannot move, e.g. a paragraph on a slide without a figure or a moved block, is not dragged. The line of a drag stops at the last place that Bring to Front or Send to Back reaches. While a row has the focus, the keys of the four layer commands, Group, Ungroup, Lock, and Unlock act on the rows chosen. Type into the field Search at the bottom of the list to show only the rows whose names contain the words, and press F6 to reach the list from the other panes. With several paragraphs, headings, or lists selected, the Text pane of the inspector formats all of them at once, as one change of the history, and a control whose value differs between them is shown as mixed. While you drag an element, two equal gaps between it and its neighbors are marked with a double arrow each, as in PowerPoint.

Choose View > Rulers, or Rulers in the menu of the View button, to show the rulers, and drag a guide out of a ruler onto the slide. The guides of a slide are shown only while the rulers are shown, and an element then snaps to them as it snaps to the other elements. Drag a guide on the slide to move it, and drag it back onto a ruler to remove it. The guides of a slide are written into `data-guides` on its `section`, e.g. `data-guides="x480 y270"` in slide pixels, and each change of them is undone with one Cmd+Z. Clear All Guides on Slide, Copy Guides, and Paste Guides are in the View and Slide menus, and they are enabled while the rulers are shown. Guides that an earlier version of the editor kept in the storage of the browser are shown as before, and they are written into the file at the next change of the guides of their slide. The attribute is read only in the editor, like `data-group` and `data-locked`.

To add a slide after the selected one, choose New Slide in the Slide menu, press Ctrl+M, or press Return in the list at the left. The new slide has the layout of the selected slide when it is a content, figure, or split slide, and it is a content slide after a title, section, or closing slide. Choose another layout with Insert > New Slide or with the button Add Slide of the toolbar. After the eight layouts, Title Only is offered in both, i.e. a content slide with only an empty `h2`, and such a slide is a Content slide in Change Layout. An empty paragraph or list item is one line high in the show, as in the editor, hence the empty placeholders of a new content slide are drawn at the same place in both. In the Slide menu, choose Duplicate Slide, or press Cmd+D or Ctrl+D in the list or with nothing selected on the slide, to copy the selected slides with each attribute `id` renamed and each `data-id` kept. Choose Delete Slide, or press Backspace in the list, to delete them, as long as one slide is left. The keys of Duplicate Slide and Delete Slide are printed in the Slide menu wherever the key runs the command. Choose Hide Slide, Cmd+Shift+H on a Mac, to write `data-visibility="hidden"`, and choose it again as Show Slide to take it off. Reorder the slides with a drag in the list, or with Move Slide Up and Move Slide Down, Alt+Up and Alt+Down. In the list, the selected slides are also cut, copied, and pasted with the keys of the clipboard. Choose a layout under Layout in the Slide menu to change the class of the slide in place. Its heading, words, figures, footnote, and notes are then moved into the wrappers of the new layout, with their order, their steps, and their places. The `plate` of a figure slide is written as a `figure-box` on any other layout. A shape keeps its place and size. With Reset Slide, the slide is written again in its layout, and the places are taken off its elements, apart from its shapes and lines.

Choose Play > Presenter View, or press Alt+Enter, to play the show in a tab of its own with the presenter display in front of you. A click or a key forward in the tab past the last step of the last slide ends the show and the display, as in Present. Press Esc or E in the tab to end Presenter View at the slide shown, and in the frame of the next slide of the speaker view to end it at the slide of the show. Each end of Presenter View closes the tab, the display, and a speaker view opened from the tab, and the editor shows the slide at which the show ended. Click the button Display Options at the right of the clock to choose which of the current slide, the next slide, the presenter notes, the clock, and the timer are shown, as in Keynote. With Play > Customize Presenter Display…, the display is opened without the show and with these options open. Click Customize Presenter Display at the foot of the options to frame each shown part, then drag a frame to move its part and drag its corner to resize it, with a slide kept at its aspect ratio. Use Auto Layout puts the parts back, and Done or Esc ends the customizing. The choice and the layout are kept in the storage of the browser for the deck, the layout as fractions of the display so that it holds at another window size, and neither is written into the file.

In the Insert menu, choose Text Box, Formula, Inline Formula, Table…, Image…, Video…, QR Code, Code Box, or Footnote to put the element at its place in the layout of the current slide. A shape and a line are drawn with a drag, as described under Shapes and Lines, and a backdrop is chosen in a gallery, as described under Backdrops. A new text box opens the Format tab of the inspector. An empty code box or footnote is written into the file only once you type into it, and it is taken off when you leave it empty. An empty text box is written once you type into it or leave it, and a text box left empty is written as an empty `p` in the entry of its insert. With the caret in a new empty text box or in the empty words of a shape, choose Formula, Cmd+Alt+E on a Mac and Alt+= elsewhere, or Inline Formula to write the formula into it. When the sheet of the formula is closed by a click away or replaced by the sheet of another formula, the empty words of the shape are taken off, and the empty text box is kept as an empty `p`. The newest object of a slide is in front. A new shape or line is brought to the front of its slide in the entry of its insert, as with Bring to Front. A new text box, formula, or figure on a slide with a shape is put in front of the shapes in the same way, and a text box or a formula is then given `position: relative` and a `z-index`. A new table is put into an empty figure box or plate of the slide, whose inner width its columns share, and else where a place of its size is free. Else it is put at the center of the slide when nothing is under the center. Otherwise it is put at the place nearest to the middle of the slide inside the margins, off the figures, the videos, the plots, the QR codes, and the other tables. Of such places, one off the words is taken first. A second such table is put off the first, with the room of its bars. On a title or closing slide, a text box is counted by its whole frame when a later text box or table is placed. An image or a video, chosen in the menu or dropped onto the slide, is put into an empty figure box or plate of the slide, and else into a new figure box. A file dropped onto a picture hidden in the mode of the slide, or chosen with its Choose Image for Light Mode… or Choose Image for Dark Mode…, is added as its twin for the other mode. With Caption in the Image pane, a picture gets a caption under it, which is written into the file once you type into it. A caption turned off and on again comes back with its text. At a save, its file is written into the folder `media` next to the deck in Chrome and Edge, and it is downloaded with the copy of the deck in the other browsers.

A plot of a deck is selected, moved, copied, deleted, given steps, grouped, locked, and ordered as a figure, and it is named Plot in the Object List and in the Steps pane. Its data, tabs, panels, and axes are not edited in the editor, hence change them in its JSON block in a text editor. The copy of a plot is pointed at the same JSON block, and the block and the step spans of a deleted plot are kept in the file. While you drag a surface to turn it, its azimuth and elevation are shown at the pointer, and the fields of the Surface pane are updated with it. These fields are drawn in the color of the selection until you choose Use Current View, and the view is then written into the file.

A text on the page that cannot be found in the file is skipped, e.g. after a change to a file that the editor does not read again. The number of skipped texts is shown in the state in the toolbar. Press Which? next to the state to see the list of these texts, with their slides and the reasons.

## Text and tables

Write bold as `strong`, italic as `em`, a strikethrough as `s`, a superscript as `sup`, a subscript as `sub`, and code as `code`. Inline code is drawn in the monospace of the system on a chip with rounded corners, tinted with the text color of the mode, and the code of a code box is drawn without the chip. To color words, put them in a `span` with one class out of `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, `tone-red`, `tone-subtle`, and `tone-muted`, which are drawn in `--accent`, `--violet`, `--blue`, `--red`, `--subtle`, and `--muted` in each mode. With `tone-text`, the words are drawn in `--text`, e.g. in a small paragraph. Such a color is shown from the start of the slide, and a color that is a step is set with `data-highlight`, as described under Stepped math. In the Format menu of the editor, Strikethrough is set with Cmd+Shift+X on a Mac and Alt+Shift+5 elsewhere, Superscript and Subscript are under Baseline, and the colors are under Text Color. There, choose Text to write `tone-text` on the words of a block drawn in another color, i.e. Small, Caption, and Footnote, or to take the span off in a block drawn in the text color. With Clear Formatting, every format is taken off, apart from a link, a formula, and a span of a colored symbol. With Paragraph Style, a block is written as `p` for Body, `p.small` for Small, `h3` for Subheading, `p.caption` for Caption, and `div.foot` for Footnote.

Give a heading, a paragraph, a list, a caption, or a `math` div the class `center` to center its words, or its display in a `math` div. The class is not written on a `section`, where reveal.js reads it in its print layout. In the editor, choose Center, Cmd+Shift+| on a Mac, or Align Left in Format > Alignment or in the section Alignment of the Text pane, which writes or takes off the class on the selected blocks. With the Format Painter, the class is copied as well.

A heading, a paragraph, a list, a `math` div, or a caption can be given a width in px in its `style`, e.g. `style="width: 720px"`, as a text box of PowerPoint. Its text is then wrapped at this width, even past the text column, and its height is the height of the wrapped text. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected block.

A numbered list is counted from 1, and the attribute `start` of its `ol` sets the number of its first item, e.g. `<ol start="4">` for a list continued after a paragraph. In the editor, type the number into the field Start at of the Text pane, where a 1 takes the attribute off. Return on an empty item in the middle of a numbered list cuts the list at a new paragraph, and the second list is given the `start` with which its count goes on, as in Keynote. The number of an item is drawn with its end 30 px before its words for any count of digits, hence a number of two digits or more is drawn partly in the margin at the left of the list.

The items of a `ul` are marked with an orange chevron. With the class `dots` on the `ul`, they are marked with an orange ring, and with the class `dashes`, with a short orange dash. A nested list without the class keeps the chevron. In a list with the class `center`, in its nested lists, and in a list of a shape not aligned to the left, each mark is drawn beside the centered words of its item. The mark is then in the line of the words, 54 px before them. An empty item is drawn without its mark in the show, in the print view, and in the PowerPoint file, while the mark is drawn as a placeholder in the editor. An item or a paragraph emptied in the editor is saved as `<li></li>` or `<p></p>`, without the line breaks that the browser leaves in it, while an emptied heading or cell keeps its `<br>`. In the editor, choose Paragraphs, Arrows, Dots, Dashes, or Numbers in Format > List or in the section Bullets & Lists of the Text pane. A choice changes the list of the caret at its level, a paragraph becomes a list of one item, and a numbered list becomes a `ul` without its `start`. A paragraph with a `translate` or a `width` in its `style`, e.g. a text box, gives them to its list, and the items are written without them. A list made paragraphs gives them back to each paragraph. On a block selected as an object, the kind is set on its top list and on every nested `ul`. Arrows and Numbers take the class off. Tab and Shift+Tab give the class to the list that they make. Dots and Dashes are not offered in the notes.

In a table, the width of a column is written in px in the `style` of its cell in the first row, e.g. `<th style="width: 320px">`. This width is the width of the content of the cell, without the padding of 40 px at its right. In the editor, it is written with a drag of the border between two columns, with Distribute Columns Evenly, and with Resize Column to Fit Content. With the last, the width of the widest words of a column is written, rounded up to a whole px. It is written for the columns of the selected cells or of the caret, or for every column of a table selected as an object. The height of a row is written in px in the `style` of its `tr`, e.g. `<tr style="height: 96px">`, and a row is drawn at least as high as its words. In the editor, choose Distribute Rows Evenly to give the rows of the selected cells, or every row of the table, the same height. This height is their mean height, or the height of the highest words among them where this is more. Choose Resize Row to Fit Content to take the written heights off the rows of the selected cells, the row of the caret, or every row of a selected table. The command is dimmed where none of these rows has a written height. Both commands are in Format > Table and in the menu of a row bar, and in the Rows section of the Table pane as the buttons Distribute Evenly and Fit to Content, beside the commands of the columns. In Format > Table and in the menu of a cell, Distribute Rows Evenly, Distribute Columns Evenly, Resize Row to Fit Content, and Resize Column to Fit Content are listed in this order. A cell set at the right with Numbers at the Right or Auto-Align is written with `text-align: right` in its `style`, and cells merged with Merge Cells are written with `colspan` and `rowspan`. A copy of a range of cells is written as text with tabs, as from a spreadsheet, and as a table. In the table, each cell is written with its formulas and their delimiters, its bold, italics, and strikethroughs, and its words colored with a class `tone-*`. A paste of such a table into the editor writes them back, and a table pasted from another app is written as plain text. With the caret in a cell, press Cmd+V on a Mac and Ctrl+V elsewhere, or choose Edit > Paste or Paste in the menu of the cell, to fill the cells from the cell of the caret.

To move rows or columns of a table in the editor, select them with a click on their bar, then drag the selection by its bar to its new place, which a line marks, as in Keynote. A press on a bar that is not selected still selects it, and Esc cancels a drag. Move Row Up, Move Row Down, Move Column Left, and Move Column Right are in the menu of a bar and in the context menu of a cell. The moved rows and columns are written in their new order in the file and stay selected. The widths of the columns stay on the first row, a header row stays above the body rows, and a row or a column is not moved across a merged cell.

## Lines

A line or an arrow on a slide is an inline `svg` with the class `line`, a child of the `section`, as large as the box of its line and moved to its place with `translate`. In the `svg`, one `line` is drawn from `x1`, `y1` to `x2`, `y2`, in pixels of the box. Its arrowheads are the `marker` of the `svg`, named in `marker-end` for an arrow and in `marker-start` as well for a double arrow. The line is stroked in `--text`, or in the color of one class `tone-muted`, `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, or `tone-red` on the `svg`, and its arrowheads are filled in the same color. It is 3 px wide, or 2, 6, or 8 px with one class `weight-2`, `weight-6`, or `weight-8` on the `svg`, and its arrowheads grow with its width. With the class `dashed`, it is drawn in dashes three widths long with gaps of two widths. An orange arrow from (200, 300) to (520, 300) on the slide is written as follows.

```html
<svg class="line tone-accent" width="320" height="1" style="translate: 200px 300px"><defs><marker id="line-1" orient="auto-start-reverse"><path d="M-4-2.5 1 0-4 2.5z"></path></marker></defs><line x1="0" y1="0" x2="320" y2="0" marker-end="url(#line-1)"></line></svg>
```

Give each marker an id that is not used elsewhere in the deck, and name it in the `url()` of its own `svg`. A curve or an elbow has one `path` in place of the `line`, with the same `marker-start` and `marker-end`, and it is not filled. Its kind is read from the `d` of the path. A curve is written as `M x1 y1 Q cx cy x2 y2`, a quadratic Bézier curve with its control point at (cx, cy). An elbow of horizontal and vertical segments is written as `M x1 y1 H m V y2 H x2` or `M x1 y1 V m H x2 V y2`, with its middle segment at m, or with one bend as `M x1 y1 H x2 V y2` or `M x1 y1 V y2 H x2`. The editor moves a path of another form whole, and it does not rewrite it.

In the editor, draw a line with Insert > Line or Insert > Arrow by a drag from one end to the other, with Shift for steps of 45 degrees, or click to insert a line 320 px long. With Insert > Curved Arrow and Insert > Elbow Arrow, you draw a curve or an elbow in the same way, with a bend that you change afterward. In the menu of the Shape button of the toolbar, the four lines are listed after the shapes. The line is written before the notes of the slide. Move it as any element, drag its ends to change them, and set its color and its arrowheads in Format > Line or in the Line pane. A selected curve or elbow has a filled third grip at its bend, which you drag to move the bend, and Esc puts the bend back during the drag. Hold Shift to keep the bend of a curve on the perpendicular bisector of its chord, for a symmetric arc. The middle segment of an elbow snaps to the line of an end within 8 pixels of the screen, unless you hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere. Change the kind with Straight, Curved, or Elbow, the width with 2 px, 3 px, 6 px, or 8 px, and the dash with Dashed, in Format > Line or in the sections Kind and Weight of the Line pane. At a change of kind, the ends, the arrowheads, the tone, and the marker of the line are kept, also on a turned line. The file is thus written as before after a change to a curve and back to straight. The marker of a copied line is given an id that is not used elsewhere in the deck, also in a slide copied with Duplicate Slide, Magic Move to Copy of Slide, or a paste. Its `url()` is then pointed at the new id. A line can be given a step in the Steps pane, where it is listed as Line.

In the editor, an end of a line that you draw or drag onto an element is attached to it, as with a connector of PowerPoint. An end can be attached to a shape, a picture, a figure, a figure box, a table, a formula, a QR code, a video, a tycho viewer, a text moved as a box, or a group as one object. It is not attached to a backdrop, a plate, the footnote, a line, or a text in the flow. While you drag the end, the element under it is outlined, with dots at the middles of its edges. The end is put at the middle of an edge within 16 slide px of it, and otherwise at the pointer. Hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to draw or drag an end without an attachment. The element is given a name unused on its slide in `data-anchor`, e.g. `data-anchor="a1"`. The end is written in `data-from` or `data-to` of the `svg` as this name and the place of the end in the box of the element, as two fractions from 0 to 1. Thus `data-to="a1 0 0.5"` is the middle of the left edge. The name of a group is written on each of its members, hence `data-anchor` is a list of names separated by spaces, as `data-group` is. An attached end is moved with each move, resize, and turn of its element, also when the element is moved by your typing in another text, in the same entry of the history as the change. A line moved as a whole is let go at both ends, unless its element is moved with it, and after a delete or a cut of the element the line is kept without an attachment. A copy of a line together with its elements is attached to the copies, and a copy of a line alone is not attached. In the section Connections of the Line pane, the element of each attached end is named, e.g. Start: Rounded Rectangle: Goal, and you press Detach to take the attachment off. A line with an attached end is not turned, as a connection line of Keynote is not. While only such lines are selected, the field Angle, Rotate Left 90°, Rotate Right 90°, and Reset Rotation are dimmed, and the title of the field reads Detach the line to turn it. The three attributes are read only in the editor, like `data-group` and `data-locked`.

## Shapes

A shape is a `div` with the class `shape`, a child of the `section`, placed with `translate` and sized with `width` and `height` in px in its `style`. Its words are in a `p` inside it, centered in its box, or aligned to the left with the class `left` on the `p`. An orange rounded rectangle of 480 by 240 px at (200, 300) on the slide is written as follows.

```html
<div class="shape rounded tone-accent" style="width: 480px; height: 240px; translate: 200px 300px"><p>Goal</p></div>
```

Without a geometry class, the shape is a rectangle. The classes `rounded` and `ellipse` give a rounded rectangle and an ellipse, which are drawn with CSS. The classes `triangle`, `arrow` (a block arrow), `arrow-both` (a double block arrow), `brace`, `plus`, and `equals` give the drawn geometries. The theme draws such a geometry from the box into an `svg` child when the deck is started, and again at a change of the size or the classes. This `svg` is not written into the file, and it is drawn in the print view as well. A brace is drawn as the center line of the extensible `}` of TeX, with round hooks at its tips. In a box of about 8 widths in height or more, its curves are drawn at the size that its width gives them, and only its straight stems are lengthened. In a shorter box, its curves are squeezed to half of the height, so that a short brace is the tall one drawn lower. The classes `flip-x` and `flip-y` mirror a drawn geometry, and they do not mirror its words.

The outline is drawn in `--text`, or in the color of one class `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, `tone-red`, or `tone-muted`, and the fill is a tint of the same color, i.e. 20 percent of it over `--bg`. The words keep the color of the text. With `fill-none`, the shape is not filled, with `fill-ground`, it is filled with `--bg`, and with `fill-translucent`, it is a translucent shape of `--bg` at 75 percent opacity, drawn without an outline unless it has `weight-6` or `weight-8`. The outline is 3 px wide, or 6 or 8 px with `weight-6` or `weight-8`, and it is left out with `no-outline`. The corners of a rounded rectangle are 24 px, or 12 px with `round-s` and 48 px with `round-l`.

In the editor, choose a shape in Insert > Shape or in the menu of the Shape button of the toolbar, whose main part draws a rounded rectangle, and drag on the slide for its box, with Shift for a square. The geometry of the shape is drawn in the box from the first move of the drag, with its size beside it. A click puts a shape of 240 by 240 px, and Esc leaves the drawing. The shape is written before the notes of the slide. It is selected, moved, resized by eight grips with a free aspect, turned, copied, aligned, ordered, grouped, and locked as a figure, and Shift on a corner grip keeps its aspect. Type with one shape selected to start its words, or press Return or double-click an empty shape to put the caret into it, and its `p` is written only once it has text. Drag the words of a selected shape to move the shape, or to drag a copy with Option on a Mac or Ctrl elsewhere, and click them without a move to put the caret at the point. Double-click a word of a shape to select the word, also when the shape is not selected, and drag over the words of a locked shape to select them. With a shape selected, use the Text pane to format all of its words, also as a list with nested items. Choose Align Left to write the class `left` on its `p`, and Center to take it off, and the alignment control is checked as the words are drawn. In the Shape pane, change the geometry in the pop-up Shape, which keeps the words, the box, and the other classes. Set Color, Fill (Tint, None, Background, or Translucent shape), Outline (None, 3 px, 6 px, or 8 px), and Corners (Small, Medium, or Large) in the same pane or in Format > Shape, for every selected shape. Corners is offered on a rounded rectangle, and an Outline of 3 px is disabled on a translucent shape. Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical toggle `flip-x` and `flip-y` on a drawn geometry. A shape without a fill is hit only on its words and on its outline, up to 6 px on either side of it, hence a marquee can be started inside it. In the Object List, a shape is named by its geometry and its words, e.g. Rounded Rectangle: Goal, and in the Steps pane it is listed as Shape. A change of the layout and the resets keep its place and size, while Reset Rotation still takes its turn off.

## Backdrops

Set the drawing of a `backdrop` with its attribute `data-backdrop`. Each drawing is computed in the browser by the code in `src/backdrops.ts`, and the demo deck shows each of them.

| Name | Drawing |
|---|---|
| `nest` | The Lyapunov families of L1 and L2 with the distant retrograde orbits about the Moon |
| `tubes` | The stable and unstable tubes of orbits at L1 and L2 |
| `wideRibbon` | The tubes of an L1 orbit toward the Earth, after Amlan Sinha's figures in the group deck and in [Phantom Pathways](https://amlan-sinha.github.io/art-of-science/art-1/) |
| a file in `data-trajectories` | The polylines of a file next to the deck, described below |

Set the window of the drawing with `data-cx` and `data-cy`, the point at the center of the box, and `data-half`, half of the height of the box, all in nondimensional units. The backdrop of a title, section, or closing slide is traced when its slide is shown, and the backdrop of another slide is drawn complete. Add `data-live` to trace it as well. To make a backdrop a step of its slide, e.g. to show the words of a title slide before the logo is traced, give it `class="fragment"` and a `data-fragment-index`. The backdrop is then hidden until its step, traced from this step on, and blanked again when the step is undone. While the pointer is on a backdrop, the trajectories within 20 slide pixels of it are tinted orange.

Label a point of a named drawing with a `label` span inside the backdrop, whose `data-at` is `L1`, `L2`, `moon`, or `earth`, and shift the label from the point by `data-dx` and `data-dy` pixels. A label is typeset with MathJax. Write a libration point as `\(\LL{1}\)`, i.e. a calligraphic L with its index, with the macro `\LL` of `theme/macros.js`, and write it in the same way in a heading, a caption, and the notes.

A backdrop can also be drawn from a file of polylines, e.g. the members of families computed elsewhere. Name the file in `data-trajectories` in place of `data-backdrop`, with its path relative to the deck. The demo deck draws `demo/broucke-sample.js`, eight members of each of Broucke's families 7 and 11 of the elliptic restricted three-body problem, which is also the example to start from.

The file is a JSON object with a list `families` of trajectories and an optional list `bodies`. Each trajectory is an object with a list `points` of `[x, y]` pairs, and optionally with a list `t` or `theta` of the time or the true anomaly at each point. The trajectory of a trajectory is traced on this clock, or at a uniform speed along its length without one. A trajectory with `"craft": true` is the path of a spacecraft, whose head is drawn without `data-heads`. The other keys of a trajectory are free, e.g. `family`, `branch`, `frame`, and `e` in the sample, and you select and color the trajectories by them. Eight keys set the look of a trajectory instead. The key `tone` is `orange`, `violet`, or `text`, for a color of the theme. The key `color` is a CSS color, or a pair of them for the dark and the light mode, drawn in place of the color of the tone. The key `alpha` is the opacity of the trajectory from 0 to 1. The key `weight` is its width in hairlines, and `head` is the radius of its head in hairlines, with 0 for none. The key `start` is the time on the clock at which the trajectory is begun, and with `glow` true it is drawn with a soft glow of its color. The key `fade` is the seconds of the clock of the trajectory, from its first point, after which its opacity falls to zero at its last point, as the square of the time left. Each entry of `bodies` has the positions of the primary and the secondary as `m1` and `m2` in an object per frame, and the mass ratio as `mu`, e.g. `{"family": 7, "mu": 0.012155, "pulsating": {"m1": [-0.01215, 0], "m2": [0.98784, 0]}}`. A body entry is matched to every trajectory with the same values for the numbers and strings of the entry. The bodies are drawn as dots only when all the drawn trajectories put them at the same positions, within 10⁻⁴ in the units of the file, hence none are drawn in the inertial frame, where they move, or for trajectories of two mass ratios.

Where the points of a trajectory are too far apart for its turns, e.g. at a close pass of a body, the trajectory is drawn as a smooth curve through the points. The curve is the centripetal Catmull–Rom spline, and it is drawn only where it departs from the straight segment between two points by more than 0.1 px.

A deck opened with a double click cannot read a JSON file, since the browser refuses the request. Wrap the JSON in `Beeson.trajectories(` and `);`, and name the `.js` file in `data-trajectories`. A plain JSON file can be named only in a deck served from a web server. With a missing or malformed file, the backdrop is left empty, and one warning is printed in the console.

- With `data-trajectories-select`, you choose the trajectories to draw, e.g. `family=7,frame=pulsating`. A term is `key=value`, `key=a|b` for either value, or `key<number`, `key<=number`, `key>number`, or `key>=number`. The terms of a group are separated by commas, and a trajectory is drawn when it passes every term of one of the groups, which are separated by semicolons.
- With `data-trajectories-color`, you give each value of a key, e.g. `family`, a pair of colors of the palette, in the order of the first trajectory of each value in the file. The palette is the eight colors `--trajectory-1` to `--trajectory-8` in `theme/beeson.css`, defined for each mode, and the pairs are `--trajectory-1` with `--trajectory-2`, `--trajectory-3` with `--trajectory-4`, and so on.
- With `data-trajectories-ramp`, you color each trajectory from the first color of its pair at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn trajectories of the pair, to the second color at the largest.
- With `data-trajectories-shade`, you draw each trajectory in a shade of the first color of its pair instead. The shade is lighter at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn trajectories of the pair, and darker at the largest. It is used in place of `data-trajectories-ramp` when both are given.
- With `data-trajectories-colormap`, you color the trajectories from a colormap of matplotlib in place of the palette, i.e. `viridis`, `plasma`, or `Blues_r`. A group of trajectories is a value of the key of `data-trajectories-color`, or the whole drawing without this attribute. In each group, the trajectory with the smallest value of the key of `data-trajectories-ramp` is drawn in the color of the lower end of the colormap, and the trajectory with the largest value in the color of the upper end. The ends are 0 and 0.85 for viridis and plasma and 0 and 0.683 for Blues_r, short of the yellow or white end, and you can give other ends after the name, e.g. `viridis 0.1 0.7`. In each mode, an end whose color has a contrast below 3:1 against `--bg` is moved toward the other end in steps of 0.01 until it reaches 3:1. Thus viridis is drawn from 0.29 to 0.85 in the dark mode, and from 0 to 0.57 in the light mode. A trajectory of a colormap is drawn opaque, hence its color on the slide is the color of a matplotlib figure, and each color between the ends keeps 3:1 against `--bg` as drawn. The colors are interpolated between 17 stops of each colormap, sampled from matplotlib and stored in `src/trajectories.ts`. With a colormap, `data-trajectories-shade` is ignored after a warning in the console. With a name other than these three, or with malformed ends, the trajectories are colored from the palette after a warning.
- With `data-stagger`, you start the trajectories one after another, in the order of the file, with the given number of seconds between two starts, e.g. `0.4`. It works on a named drawing too.
- With `data-loop`, you fly an orange head with a short trail around a trajectory, over and over at 9 s per round, while the slide is shown. On a named drawing, the head is flown on the first trajectory, and on a drawing of a file, a head is flown on each trajectory.
- With `data-heads`, you draw a moving head on every trajectory, at the size `full` or `small`, or on none with `none`. Without it, a head is drawn only on the path of a spacecraft, and on a trajectory of a file with a `head` of its own. Such a path is a trajectory of a file with `"craft": true`.
- With `data-duration`, you set the seconds in which the whole drawing is traced, from the first trajectory to the last, 12 by default. The clock of a file runs in these seconds, hence with `data-duration` equal to the last time on the clock, `start` and `t` are in seconds.

Without `data-half`, the window of a backdrop of a file is fitted to its drawing with the fill of `tubes` on the closing slide. In the window, 93 percent of the drawing is held, measured along each trajectory with every trajectory counted alike. The drawing is also kept 40 px clear of each side of the box that is inside the slide.

In the editor, click a backdrop to select it, and its frame is drawn at the edge of its box with eight grips. Drag from a backdrop that is not selected to draw a marquee, since a backdrop is often as large as the slide. ## Surfaces

A `surface` is the graph of a function $z = f(x, y)$ over a rectangle, drawn in 3D with three.js under a camera that turns slowly and can be turned with a drag. Set the function with `data-function`, and its parameters with further data attributes. Each function is defined in `src/surfaces.ts` with its states, i.e. what is drawn at each step of the slide. Give a fragment of the same slide the attribute `data-surface-state`, and the surface is moved to this state when the fragment is shown, and back when it is hidden. At a state, several sheets may be shown, i.e. layers of the function stacked at different heights, each in its own color ramp. A change of state is animated. The heights of each sheet are morphed, a sheet that is added is split from the first sheet or grown from a flat sheet, and a sheet that is removed is merged into the first sheet or flattened onto it. The points are grown and faded in place on the first sheet, drawn over the surface, and dimmed where the surface is in front of them, and a cutting plane is slid to its height. Label a point with a `label` span whose `data-at` is the name of the point. To pick out some of the points, give the fragment that sets the state the attribute `data-surface-points` with their names, e.g. `L1 L2 L3`. The other points and their labels are then dimmed to 18 percent. The points named in `data-surface-accent` are filled in orange, those named in `data-surface-violet` in violet, and their labels keep the color of the text. A fragment that changes only these attributes and repeats the state of the step before is drawn as a fade of the points with a time constant of 0.15 s, and the sheets are left as they are. The demo slide "Color the terms and the marks" is an example of each attribute. In a deck with a surface, load `theme/vendor/three/three.js` before `theme/beeson.js`.

| Function | Attributes | States |
|---|---|---|
| `effective-potential` | `data-mu`, the mass ratio $\mu$ of the circular restricted three-body problem, 0.05 by default, and `data-C` on the fragment of the cut, the Jacobi constant $C$, that of L1 by default | `wells` in Blues, `bowl` with the centrifugal dome in Reds above the wells, `sum` with the two merged in Magma_r (Magma reversed) without its darkest 30 %, `extrema` with L1 to L5 marked, and `cut` with the plane at $-C/2$ and the zero-velocity curve |

The camera is set with `data-azimuth` and `data-elevation` in degrees, `data-turn` in seconds per turn (0 for a still camera), and `data-zoom`, and the heights are scaled with `data-zscale`. A surface whose data attributes other than `data-surface-state` are those of a surface on the slide before is shown under the camera of that surface, and its turn is continued across the change of slide. With `?still`, a state is drawn at once and the camera is not turned. In a PDF and in PowerPoint, each step is printed with a still of its state.

## Figures

For a figure made with matplotlib, use the styles `beeson-dark.mplstyle` and `beeson-light.mplstyle` in `styles/`. The palette, the typeface, and a transparent background are set in these styles. Export one file per mode and mark the images `for-dark` and `for-light`, so that the right one is shown in each mode. Give a white raster figure the class `white-figure`, and it is framed. In a `plate` or a `figure-box`, the paper is cut to the size of the image, which is scaled down to fit and is not enlarged. Export such a figure at the aspect of its box and at least at its pixel size, e.g. 1680 by 630 px for the plate of a figure slide. Give a black-on-white line plot the class `invert`, and it is inverted in dark mode.

An image, a video, a QR code, or a tycho viewer can be given a width in its `style`. An image or a video with a width is drawn at this width, even past the box of its figure box or plate, and it keeps the aspect of its file. With a height in px as well, it is stretched to both. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected picture, and on both images of a figure with an image for each mode. A picture may be drawn inside a larger box with `object-fit: contain`. For such a picture, a Width or a Height typed into the Arrange pane scales the box at its ratio. The size is written as `width`, `height`, and `object-fit: contain`, which Reset Size takes off again. The old value typed back right after a typed Width or Height gives back the bytes from before. The height is written as well when the box Constrain Proportions of the Arrange pane is off, and you turn the box over for one drag by holding Shift on a corner. On a figure with a caption, a Height typed into the Arrange pane is kept as typed wherever a width of whole pixels gives this height, at any wrap of the caption. Otherwise the nearest height that a width gives is taken, at the smaller width where two widths are equally near. A width under the one at which the figure is lowest is not taken, since under it the caption grows by more lines than the picture loses in height. Crop an image with Edit Mask. The crop is written as `object-fit: cover` and `object-position` where the picture fills its box, and else as `scale`, a `clip-path` of the form `inset(N%)`, and `object-position`. Give an `img` the class `oval` to clip it to the ellipse of its box. The crop of an oval picture is written as `inset(N% round 50%)`, which clips it to the ellipse of the cropped box. In the editor, turn on Oval in Format > Image or in the Image pane, which writes the class on both images of a pair. On a picture drawn smaller than its box, e.g. one without a width in a figure box, the width of the drawn picture is written on both images as well. The figure is then moved with `translate`, hence the picture stays in place and is clipped to its ellipse. A flip with Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical in the Arrange menu is written as a negative `scale`, e.g. `scale: -1 1`.

Math is typeset in SVG with MathJax 4 and its TeX font, i.e. Computer Modern, and the text is set in Latin Modern Roman, all vendored. The macros of a deck are defined in `theme/macros.js`.

To show a video, put it in a `figure-box` or a `plate`, and add `data-autoplay` to play it while its slide is shown. Give every video a `poster`, since the poster is printed in the PDF and `npm run check` fails without one. The clip of the demo deck is the manifolds example of tycho, exported by `web/scripts/video.mjs` of tycho in the colors of each mode and re-encoded at 30 frames per second with ffmpeg, as described in CONTRIBUTING.md of tycho.

To start a video at a step, as a video set to start on a click in PowerPoint, give it `class="fragment play"` and a `data-fragment-index`, e.g. `<video class="for-dark fragment play" data-fragment-index="1" src="clip-dark.mp4" poster="clip-dark.png" muted></video>`, and the same for the clip of the other mode. Its poster is shown with its slide, and the video is played from its start once its step is shown, without `data-autoplay`. A video that is a plain fragment, or inside one such as a `figure-box` with `class="figure-box fragment"`, appears at its step instead, and with `data-autoplay` it is played from its start at this step. When its step is undone, a video is paused and rewound, to its poster when it is still in view. A video shown at a step is not dimmed at the later steps, since it is still played. In the PDF, each step is printed with the poster of each video shown at it. Give a video `muted` unless its sound is wanted, since a browser may refuse to play sound before a key is pressed, and in the speaker view only a muted video is played, as in reveal.js. In the editor, the effect Play is offered in the Steps pane for a video, and it is written as the class `play`, with `data-autoplay` taken off. With the checkbox Start on a click of the Video pane, the video is made the last step of its slide with this effect, or its step is given the effect. While this checkbox is checked, the checkbox Play while the slide is shown is dimmed and off, and once it is cleared, the video is left a step with the effect Fade In. In a preview of the steps, such a video is played from its start at its step, and it is rewound to its poster at the end of the preview. The demo slide "Starting a video on a click" is an example.

The videos shown at one step are started together from their first frame, once each of them can be played, and a video that can be played earlier is held at its first frame for up to 5 s. Thus two clips side by side are in step. To show them side by side, put them in a `plate` with the class `row` on a figure slide, e.g. `<div class="plate row">`, which gives each figure of the plate an equal share of its width, and give each clip `class="fragment play"` with the same `data-fragment-index`.

The videos of one `figure-box` or `plate` are stacked in one place, except in a plate with the class `row`, and a video is laid over the ones before it. A video under a later one that is shown, in the same mode, is hidden once the later one has faded in, and paused, and it is shown and started again from its start when the later step is undone. To show one clip and then another in its place, give the first `class="fragment play"` and a step, e.g. 1, and the second `class="fragment"`, `data-autoplay`, and the next step, e.g. 2. The poster of the first clip is then shown with the slide, the first clip is played at step 1, and the second clip is faded in over it and played at step 2. The test deck `test/pages/video-play.html` has a slide of each of these cases.

To embed a scene of tycho, the Typed Celestial HTML Observer, call `build_embed` of pytycho, the Python package of tycho, and paste the inline `tycho-viewer` element into a slide. The scene and its data are in two inline blocks of this element. The runtime is vendored once in `theme/vendor/tycho/`, copied by hand from a build of pytycho, since tycho is not on npm. It is built from the main branch of tycho at `34abdbe`, ahead of the next release of tycho, and it will be vendored again from that release. It reads scenes of format 0.4, which adds the timeline and the stages of tycho, and still those of formats 0.2 and 0.3, i.e. a scene written by pytycho 0.2.0 is played as before. To write a scene of format 0.4, run pytycho of the same commit. The viewer is set to the mode of the deck, the scene is played only while its slide is shown, and the keys used in the viewer are not passed to the deck. A viewer with `data-keep-theme` keeps the theme written in the file through a flip of the mode, for a scene whose marks are drawn for one background. In a PDF, and in PowerPoint with `--still`, the viewer is replaced by a still of the scene at its first frame, placed where the panels of the viewer are, and every step of the slide is printed with the same still. In PowerPoint without this option, the scene is a recording, as described under PowerPoint. Pass `kiosk=True` for a viewer without controls. Run `demo/tycho-scene.py` to write the viewer of the demo deck. This script is also the example to start from.

To start a scene at a step, as a video of the style `play`, give its viewer `class="fragment play"` and a `data-fragment-index`, which `build_embed` writes from its keywords, e.g. `build_embed(document, container, kiosk=True, class_="figure-box fragment play", data_fragment_index=2)`, or put the viewer in a fragment of the style `play`, e.g. a `figure-box` with `class="figure-box fragment play"`. The viewer is then shown with the first frame of its scene, paused, until its step, and its scene is played from its start once its step is shown. A kiosk viewer is started by tycho once its scene is loaded, and a viewer of the style `play` is held at its first frame again before its step. When its step is undone, or its slide is left, the viewer is paused at its first frame. The viewers shown at one step are started together, once the scene of each of them is loaded, e.g. two scenes side by side in a `plate` with the class `row`, each with the same `data-fragment-index`. A viewer shown at a step is not dimmed at the later steps, since it is still played. In a PDF, and in PowerPoint with `--still`, the still of its first frame is on every step of its slide. The viewers of a `figure-box` are not stacked in one place, as videos are, hence a second scene shown in the place of a first one is not one of these cases. A viewer without the style `play` is played by tycho while it is in view, and it is resumed from where it stopped when its slide is shown again. In the editor, the effect Play is offered in the Steps pane for a viewer as well, and it is written on the tag of the viewer. The checkbox Start on a click of the Video pane is in the Arrange pane of a selected scene too. Once this checkbox is cleared, the viewer is not left a step, as a video is, since tycho plays a kiosk viewer that is only a step while it is still hidden. The step is taken off the viewer, or off the figure box of the style `play` around it, and the scene is started with its slide again. In a preview of the steps, a viewer of the style `play` is held at its first frame until its step and played from its start at the step. The test deck `test/pages/viewer-play.html` has a slide of each case, written with pytycho of the same commit as the runtime.

## QR codes

A QR code is a `div` with the class `qr` and its text in `data-qr`, e.g. `<div class="qr" data-qr="https://example.com/slides.pdf"></div>`. The code is drawn into the div as an inline SVG when the deck is started. It is square at the width of the div, which is 320 px by default. On a closing slide, the code is 200 px wide in the lower right corner, 50 px from the right and the bottom of the slide, and the backdrop is drawn over it. Set another width in the `style` of the div, e.g. `style="width: 400px"`. The encoder is in `src/qr.ts` and is bundled into `theme/beeson.js`, hence nothing is loaded for a code.

The group logo, `brand/logo.png`, is drawn in the middle of the code, as a disc of 30 percent of the width of the modules with a light ring of 0.8 module around it. The modules under the logo and its ring are not drawn. The text is thus encoded as UTF-8 at error correction level H, at which about 30 percent of the code can be restored, up to 1273 bytes. The version is the smallest one with room for the text in which the codewords under the logo are at most two thirds of those that can be restored in a block, so that a third is left for the errors of a camera. An alignment pattern that the logo reaches is left out as a whole. With `class="no-logo"` on the `body`, which also switches off the logo at the top right of the slides, a code is drawn without the logo at level M, at which about 15 percent can be restored, in the smallest version with room for the text, up to 2331 bytes. A text too long for level H is drawn in the same way. Switch the logo off in a deck without the files of `brand/`.

The modules are drawn dark on a light tile in both modes, since an inverted code is not read in many scanner apps. Neighboring modules are joined, and a corner of a module without a dark neighbor is rounded. The finder and alignment patterns are rounded rings with an eye in the violet of the light mode, which is used in both modes for its contrast on the tile. The tile is the off-white of the text in dark mode and the paper color of the background in light mode, with rounded corners and a quiet zone of four modules. The colors are the custom properties `--qr-ink`, `--qr-eye`, and `--qr-tile` of `theme/beeson.css`, and the image of the logo is `--qr-logo`. The code is drawn the same in the slides, with `?still`, in the PDF, and in PowerPoint. In the editor, it is moved by a drag as a figure, and it can be given a step in the Steps pane. An empty or too long `data-qr` is shown as a message in place of the code, and `npm run check` fails on it. Keep the address short, since a shorter address is drawn with fewer and larger modules, which are read from farther back in the room. Draw a code that must be read from the back of a room wider than the 200 px of the closing slide, since the distance from which a code is read is proportional to its width. After changing the backdrop of a closing slide, read the code with a phone, since `npm test` reads only the code of the demo deck under its backdrop. The closing slide of the demo deck is an example.

In the editor, a QR code with a `translate` or a width is kept at its drawn place and size at a change between Closing and another layout. Its new `translate`, and its width where the default width of the new layout differs, are written in the same entry of the history as the change. A code without a `translate` and without a width takes the place and the size of the new layout. On a closing slide, the words of the block are drawn in front of the code. The code is thus moved past all of them at once with Bring to Front and Send to Back, and Bring Forward and Send Backward are dimmed for it. On a title slide, a code is in the flow, and Insert > QR Code puts it at the upper left corner of the slide. There, its layer is written as a `z-index`, which is also written on the backdrop once the code is sent to the back.

## Stepped math

A display is revealed in four steps: the lead-in sentence, the display, the sentence in which it is explained, and a step in which a symbol of the display is colored. Mark the symbol in the TeX with `\class{name}{...}`, and give a later fragment the attribute `data-highlight="name"`. Once this fragment is shown, the symbols of the class are drawn in orange, or in violet, blue, or red with `data-tone` set to `violet`, `blue`, or `red`. Any element of the slide with the class `name` is colored as well, e.g. a `span` around the words that name the symbol. To take the color off a class at a later step, give that fragment `data-unhighlight="name"`, and the class is drawn in the color of the text until a step after it names the class again in `data-highlight`. Use orange for motion and violet for structure, as in the rest of the theme, and blue and red for the terms drawn in Blues and Reds on a surface, and separate several classes with spaces.

An earlier fragment is dimmed to 45 percent once a later one is shown, and every fragment is restored at the step added after the last one. Give a fragment without an index the attribute `data-final`, e.g. a QR code, to show it at this step in place of the added one, i.e. once every fragment is restored. Give the display the attribute `data-keep` to hold it at full opacity through the next step, or `data-keep="4"` to hold it through the step of index 4. A display is also held at full opacity while a class in it is colored at the current step. The demo slide "Stepping through the equations" is an example of every step.

## Code

A code box is a `pre` with a `code` in it, and it is colored with highlight.js in the highlight plugin of reveal.js. Load `theme/vendor/reveal/highlight.js` after `notes.js`, and give the `code` the class of its language, e.g. `language-python`, since the language is guessed in a box without one. With the class `nohighlight`, a box is left uncolored. The tokens are colored from the palette of the theme in each mode, and the code is set in the system monospace at 22 px. At this size, about 24 lines can be shown under a heading, and about 29 on a slide without one. A line can be up to about 65 characters long in the text column, and up to about 115 on a slide with the class `wide`. For a longer box, set a smaller `font-size` in its `style`. In the editor, choose Smaller in the Format menu to write `font-size: 18px` on the `pre`, and Bigger to take it off, or set and clear the box Small in the Code pane.

To step through a box, number its lines with `data-line-numbers` on the `code`, and write one group of lines per step, separated by bars, e.g. `data-line-numbers="1-3|4|5-7"`. The first group is lit when the slide is shown, and each later group is lit at its step, while the other lines are dimmed to 35 percent. The whole box is dimmed at a step outside it after its last group, and it is restored at the step added at the end of the slide. In the print view, each group is printed on a separate page. While the deck is edited, the box is shown plain at the same size, since the text of the page is paired with the file. The demo slide "Code, one group of lines per step" is an example, cut from `demo/tycho-scene.py`.

## Plots

A plot is an animated 2D figure, drawn as SVG from a block of JSON in the deck. Put a `div` with the class `plot` in a `figure-box`, or with the classes `plot plate` on a `figure` slide, and give it the id of the block in `data-plot`. Write the block next to it as `<script type="application/json" id="...">`. The block is inline, since fetching a file is blocked for a deck opened with a double click. The demo deck does not have a plot, and the test decks `test/pages/plot-steps.html` and `test/pages/plot-reveals.html` are examples to start from. In the editor, a plot is moved as a figure, and its block is written only in a text editor.

The plot is played in a loop while its slide is shown, and it is paused when the slide is left. Under the plot, a button to play and pause, a scrubber with a tick at each event, and the value of the parameter are shown, and the plot is paused when you drag the scrubber. In the PDF and in PowerPoint, the plot is printed at its last frame. Set another frame with `data-plot-frame`, where a negative frame is counted from the end, and set `data-plot-paused` to show the still in the slides as well. To start the plot at a step, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-play`. The plot is then held at its still frame until the fragment is shown and played from its first frame afterward, and it is printed at its last frame from this step on. Under reduced motion, the plot is not played.

To reveal a plot in steps, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-show` with the ids of panels, marks, or elements, separated by spaces. Each of these is hidden until its fragment is shown. With `data-plot-tab` on a fragment, the plot is switched to the named tab, hence each tab is given a page of its own in the PDF. An empty `span` is enough for a fragment without text. To show one block on several slides, give each slide an element of its own, with the ids of its panels in `data-plot-panels` and the names of the tabs it offers in `data-plot-tabs`. A tab named in a `data-plot-show` is offered once its fragment is shown, hence give the panels, marks, elements, and tabs distinct names.

These keys are read from the block.

| Key | Value |
|---|---|
| `panels` | A list of panels, each with an `id`, the axes `x` and `y`, and an optional `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels`. An axis is `{ "range": [a, b], "ticks": [...], "title": "..." }`, and a tick is a number or `{ "at": v, "text": "..." }` with a label in the markup. Set `"scale": "asinh"` on an axis to draw a value v at asinh(v/u), where u is the `unit` of the axis, 1 by default. Set `"equal": true` for one scale on both axes, and `"axes": false` to leave the axes out. The `size` of a panel is its share of the plot, 1 by default, and its `margin` is `[top, right, bottom, left]` in pixels. |
| `marks` | A list of the animated marks, each with an `id`, a `panel`, and a `kind`. The data of a mark are given in each tab. |
| `tabs` | A list of named data sets, each with a `name`, a `label` for its button, the `values` of the parameter at the frames, the `data` of the marks by their ids, an optional list of `times`, an optional `active` list, and optional `panels`. In `panels`, the `x`, `y`, `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels` given for a panel id replace those of the panel while the tab is shown. |
| `parameter` | `{ "label": "$A$", "digits": 3 }`, the name of the parameter and the digits of its value. |
| `duration`, `hold`, `fade` | The seconds of a pass over the frames of a tab without `times`, 8 by default, the seconds for which the last frame is held, 1 by default, and the seconds of the crossfade at the start of the next pass, 0.5 by default and at most a pass. In the crossfade, the moving marks of the next pass are faded in while the held last frame is faded out. A text of the held frame is faded out in the first half of the crossfade and a text of the next pass in the second half, so that two texts are not drawn over each other. |
| `layout` | `row` or `column`, the direction of the panels. By default, the panels are in a row in a wide box and in a column in a tall one. |

The data of a mark are given with one entry per value of the tab, or with one entry for a mark that does not move. Between two frames, the positions are interpolated, and a text is taken from the nearest frame.

Without `times`, the frames of a tab are shown at even steps over `duration`. With `times`, each frame is shown at its time in seconds into the pass, from 0 and increasing, hence a tab can be slowed near an event, and a frame given twice with a later time is held still. The scrubber and its ticks are placed by these times.

| Kind | Entry | Drawing |
|---|---|---|
| `points` | A flat list `[x1, y1, x2, y2, ...]` | A dot at each point, filled, or a ring with `"hollow": true` |
| `trace` | A point `[x, y]` | The whole path in a faint line, left out with `"ghost": false`, and the path up to the current frame in full with a dot at its end |
| `label` | `{ "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, with one position and one text, or a list of each | The text at the position |
| `events` | A list of `{ "frame": n, "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, not one per frame | A ring and its text from the frame on, and a tick on the scrubber in the tone of the mark |

The faint line of a trace runs over the whole path, unless the data of the trace are given as `{ "points": [...], "ghost": n }`, with the list of points in `points`. The faint line then ends at frame n, and the rest of the path is drawn only as it is traced.

A region is a polygon of `points`, filled at `opacity`. A curve is a polyline of `points` or a `circle` `[cx, cy, r]`, with a `width` and a `dash`. A label is a `text` at `at`, with an `anchor` (`start`, `middle`, or `end`) and an offset `dx`, `dy` in pixels. A mark is drawn with a `size`, a `width`, an `anchor`, `dx`, and `dy` as well. Give each element and mark a `tone`, i.e. a color of the theme without its dashes (`accent`, `violet`, `text`, `subtle`, `muted`, or `data-1` to `data-8`), and the plot is drawn right in both modes. A `class` on an element or a mark is a target of `data-highlight`, as in stepped math, and the element keeps its color when a tab is chosen. In `active`, list the ids of the elements emphasized at each frame. The other ids named in the active list of any tab are dimmed at the same frame.

Text in the JSON is set in a small markup rather than with MathJax, so that a plot is drawn the same in the slides, the PDF, and PowerPoint. Math is written between `$` signs, with italic letters, `_` and `^`, braces, `\mathrm{}`, `\text{}`, `\mathcal{}` for the script capitals of Unicode, Greek letters, and a few symbols such as `\to`, and a hyphen is set as a minus sign there. A fault in the block is shown in place of the plot with its path, e.g. `tabs[0].data.m: 3 frames are given, and the tab has 2 values`, and `npm run check` fails on it.

## Browsers

You can show a deck in the current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, and in the browsers built on their engines, on a computer, a phone, or a tablet. This holds for both modes, the steps, the speaker view, and the viewer of tycho, whether you open the file with a double click or from a web server. All browsers on iOS are built on the engine of Safari. These are the limits.

- In Safari on a Mac, the paper size of the deck is ignored, and the PDF is printed on the paper chosen in the print dialog. Choose a paper of 16:9, or export with `npm run pdf`. The backdrops are also printed fainter than in the other browsers.
- On an iPhone, fullscreen is not available, since the fullscreen API is missing there.
- On a phone or a tablet, the speaker view is opened with the key `s`, hence you need a keyboard. A deck is opened from a web server, such as the site of the repository, since a double click on a file does not apply there. Printing is not tested.
- In every browser, `\require{bbm}`, `\require{bboldx}`, and `\require{dsfont}` show the error "Extension failed to load", since the fonts of these three extensions of MathJax are not vendored.

## PowerPoint

At some conferences, a speaker is given a laptop that shows only PowerPoint and PDF files. For such a room, run `npm run pptx`, which writes `slides.pptx` next to `slides.pdf`. Each step of the deck is a full-slide image, and a click in PowerPoint is a step of the deck. The text cannot be edited in this file.

A backdrop that is traced or looped in the deck is recorded from the first step of its slide at which it is traced, and the recording is placed on the PowerPoint slide of this step. The recording is over a still of the slide, i.e. its last frame, and under a picture of the words of each step, and it is started with the slide. Each later step of the same slide of the deck is one more picture of the words, shown on a click, while the same videos are shown at the steps. What is drawn under the backdrop, e.g. the QR code of a closing slide, is part of the recording, as it is drawn at the first recorded step. Hence an element under a recorded backdrop is kept as at the first recorded step, also where it is hidden or dimmed at a later step. A backdrop of the same slide that is traced only from a later step is not recorded, and it is drawn complete in the picture of the words of its step and of the steps after it. A recording of loops alone is one period long, i.e. 9 s, and it is looped in PowerPoint. A recording with a trace is as long as the trace, and it is not looped. A backdrop that is drawn complete is left in the image of its step, and so are the surfaces and the plots. A tycho scene is recorded once and placed in the box of its viewer as a video, with the first frame of the scene as the poster of the video. A viewer of the fragment style `play` is started on the click of its step, as a video of this style is.

With `npm run pptx -- --still`, the file is written without a recording. The backdrops are then drawn complete, each scene is left as the still of its first frame, and an H.264 encoder is not needed. The recordings are encoded with the H.264 encoder of WebCodecs, which is built into Google Chrome. In a browser without one, the export fails, and the reason is printed together with the option `--still`. A deck without a scene and without a traced backdrop is exported by such a browser as well.

Each video is placed over its image as a PowerPoint video, and it is started when its step is shown, together with the other videos of the step. The steps with a video are placed on one PowerPoint slide, hence the video goes on playing through them. A video of the fragment style `play` is part of the images of the steps before its own, with its poster, and a video under a later one of its place is left out from the step of the later one on. PowerPoint needs an MP4 file, and the export fails on a video without one. The speaker notes are copied to the PowerPoint notes. Keynote opens a `.pptx`, hence a separate Keynote export is not needed.

## Tooling

For `npm test`, `npm run check`, `npm run pdf`, and `npm run pptx`, Chrome is looked up at its default location on macOS and Windows, and on the `PATH` on Linux. Edge is used on Windows when Chrome is missing. To use another browser build, set `CHROME_PATH` to its executable. For the recordings of `npm run pptx`, an H.264 encoder is needed, which is built into Google Chrome and not into Chromium, and with `--still` you can export from a browser without one.
To run the check in another engine, use `npm run check -- --browser firefox` or `npm run check -- --browser safari`. Firefox is driven with `geckodriver`, which is looked up in `GECKOWEBDRIVER` and on the `PATH`, and `FIREFOX_PATH` selects another Firefox build. Safari is driven with `safaridriver`, which you enable once with `sudo safaridriver --enable`. Quit Safari before a run, since a session cannot be started in a Safari that is already open. A page from `file://` is refused under automation, hence the deck is served from a local port in Safari, and a double click is not covered by this check. The problems in the page are reported in all three browsers, but the console is read only in Chrome, and the phone layout, a pixel ratio of 1.25, and a save of the editor are checked only in Chrome. On Linux, run Firefox under `xvfb-run`, since WebGL in software is available there only in a window.

If you copied `theme/` and `brand/` by hand, copy `.agents/`, `.claude/`, `AGENTS.md`, `scripts/`, and `package.json` as well. You can then run `npm run check`, `npm run pdf`, and `npm run pptx` without `npm ci`.
