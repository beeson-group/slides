# Reference

Each element of the theme `beeson` is described here in full, with the formats of its files. The start of a talk is given in the README, and the vocabulary of the theme in `GLOSSARY.md`.

## Slides

Give a section slide `data-run` to show another text in the running head of its section, e.g. its heading without the math. The same text is the name of the section on the band of the scrubber. Give a slide `data-visibility="hidden"` to leave it out of the show. Such a slide is removed from the page of the show, and it is kept in the file and in the editor. Give a slide `data-draft` to make it a draft, i.e. a slide that is not finished yet. A draft is presented, printed, and exported as any other slide, and `npm run check` prints a line with the number and the heading of each draft without failing. The next slide is faded in over 400 ms by default. With `data-transition="none"` on a slide, the next one is shown at once, and with `data-auto-animate` on both slides, the elements with the same `data-id` are moved between them. An element whose `translate` or `rotate` differs between the two slides is glided from its old place and angle to the new ones. With `data-transition="fade-in slide-out"` on a slide and `data-transition="slide-in fade-out"` on the next one, the slide is pushed out to the left by the next one, and a step back over it is a fade. Write the seconds planned for a slide in its `data-timing`, which is read in the pacing of the speaker view and in the times of the scrubber.

A section slide holds its heading and, under it, an outline list of the sections of the talk, `<ol class="outline" data-auto></ol>` in its `div.sec`. The list is kept empty in the file. When the deck is shown, it is given an item with the heading of each section slide, also of one with `data-run`, and the item of its own section is drawn in `--text` and the others muted. In an `ol`, the item of its own section is numbered in `--text`, and in a `ul`, it is marked with a violet bullet, a ring with `dots` or a dash with `dashes`. The numbers and bullets of the other items are muted with their words. An outline list of an older talk, `ul.outline`, is thus drawn with bullets until it is made an `ol`.

The slides after the closing slide are the appendix. They are numbered A1, A2, and so on, and they are named on the band of the scrubber rather than given a tick. A section slide in the appendix opens a section of the appendix. The sections of the appendix are lettered A, B, C in the order of the deck, and a hidden section slide is not lettered. The label of the section, e.g. Appendix A, is drawn by the theme in violet at 64 px above the heading of its section slide. The label is not text of the file, hence the letters follow when a section slide is moved, added, removed, or hidden, and a section slide moved before the closing slide is an ordinary section slide again. The running head of a section of the appendix is its label and its name, e.g. Appendix A: Convergence proofs, and the running head of the slides before the first section slide of the appendix is Appendix slides. On the band of the scrubber, the name of a section of the appendix is its letter and its name, e.g. A: Convergence proofs. In an outline list, the item of a section of the appendix is its label and its heading, without a number in an `ol`. The text of `data-run` replaces the name in the running head and on the band, not the label. The label is drawn in the editor, in the PDF, and in the PowerPoint file as in the show, and it cannot be selected or typed into.

In the editor, the running head is written with the field Running Head of the Slide pane, and a hidden slide with Hide Slide in the Slide menu or with the box Hidden in the show. Make a slide a draft with Set as Draft in the Slide menu or in the menu of a right click on its thumbnail, with Cmd+Alt+Shift+D on a Mac, or with the box Draft of the Slide pane. The command is checked on a draft. On several selected slides, it makes all of them drafts, or takes `data-draft` off when all of them are drafts, as one change of the history. The chip Draft is then drawn over the upper right corner of the slide, on its thumbnail, and on its tile in Slide Sorter, in the orange of the mode of the deck. The chip is drawn only in the editor, and a duplicated or pasted draft stays a draft. While you type into the heading of a section slide, the outline lists follow the heading at each key, and so do the running heads of its section when it has no `data-run`. Numbers or Bullets in the Text pane or in Format > List, chosen on a selected outline list, is set on every outline list of the deck, and Paragraphs is dimmed for it. Click an item of a selected outline list to rename its section in a field over the item. Enter or a click elsewhere writes the name into the heading of the section slide, as plain text, and Esc leaves the field without a change. Choose the transition with None, Fade, Magic Move, or Push at the head of the Steps pane or in Slide > Transition. With Magic Move, the matched elements of the two slides are given one `data-id`. A line, a curve, or an elbow is matched to one of the same tone and arrowheads, hence a line with arrowheads is not matched to one without them. A shape is matched to a shape of the same classes, apart from the classes of its step and its effect, hence a shape is matched to its copy when one of the two has a step effect. At the end of Rehearse Timings, press Keep Timings to write the seconds spent on each slide into its `data-timing`.

## Steps and modes

Give each element its step once the slide is complete: `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. During the talk, a new element is faded in, and the earlier ones are dimmed to 45 percent. The last element is also shown alone at first, and all of them are restored to full opacity at one more step, which is added to every slide with a dimmed element. A slide whose fragments are empty, e.g. the steps of a plot, is not given this step. A step nested in another step, e.g. a `span` with the class `fragment` in an item of a list that is a step, is drawn at full opacity while it is the current step, together with the step that holds it.

Give a fragment the class `semi-fade-out` to dim it to 25 percent from its step on, as the emphasis Transparency of PowerPoint. Give any element the class `faint` to draw it at 25 percent throughout, also when it is a step and in the print view. In the editor, choose Dim as the effect of a step in the Steps pane to write `semi-fade-out`. A dimmed step is drawn at full opacity on the slide that you edit, and dimmed in Preview Steps, in Preview Transition, in Presenter View, and in the show. Turn on Faint in the Arrange pane to write `faint` on the selected elements, and Reset Slide keeps the class.

Give a fragment the class `late` and the custom property `--delay` in its `style` to fade it in this many seconds after the click of its step, e.g. `<p class="fragment late" style="--delay: 1.2s">`, as an animation of PowerPoint with a delay. A late step thus waits for a plot or a surface of its step to move first. A late fragment inside a shown fragment is delayed as well, and it is faded out without the delay when its step is undone. The editor keeps the class and the property, and it does not set them, hence write them in a text editor.

Give a fragment `data-fly-from` with a CSS selector to fly it in at its step from the box of the elements of its slide that the selector matches. The element is shown at once in place of its fade, moved from the center of this box to the center of its own box, and scaled from the width of this box to its own width, over the seconds of `data-fly-duration`, 1.2 by default. With `data-fly-to` and a selector of elements inside the element, the box of these elements is matched to the box of the sources in place of the whole element, e.g. a ring drawn both in a small plot and in the element. A fly is played only going forward. In the print view, with `?still`, under reduced motion, and in the editor, the element is shown in its place. The test deck `test/pages/fly.html` is an example. The editor keeps the three attributes, and Preview does not play the fly, hence present the deck to see it.

A step can be shown by itself after a delay, as a step that starts After Previous in PowerPoint. Give a fragment of the step before it `data-autoslide` with the delay in whole milliseconds. With `data-autoslide` on the last step, the deck is moved on to the next slide after the delay. Press a key during a delay to show the next step at once, and the later delays are still played. When you go back, the steps reached by a delay are skipped up to the last step reached by a key. Press A to pause and resume the timed steps. A slide with a delay on its last step is not given the step that restores the dimmed fragments, and the look of its last step is kept through the transition. Such a slide is marked with `data-moves-on` when the deck is started, and the attribute is not written in the file. In the Steps pane of the editor, choose After Previous as the start of a step and type the delay in seconds, at least 0.1, or step it by 0.1 s with the arrows of the field. The `data-autoslide` is then written on the first fragment of the step before.

You flip the mode with the key `d` or with the button at the upper right. The change is drawn as a circle at the button or at the point of the click, grown on a flip to light and shrunk on a flip to dark. During a flip, a backdrop that is being traced is drawn on in both modes. A viewer of tycho, a surface, a plot, or a video is held still in the old mode for up to 450 ms, until the new mode is shown over it. The mode is kept per browser in local storage. To fix it for a venue, add the URL parameter `?theme=light`. Selected words are drawn on a tint of the tone blue of the mode, `#d3dced` in the light mode and `#1b334d` in the dark mode, whatever the mode of the system, and every tone can be read on it. A formula is not drawn as selected, since its glyphs are drawn as SVG paths. To draw every backdrop complete at once for a screenshot, add `?still`.

## Stacks

Write content that replaces other content in the same place, e.g. the next form of an equation, as a stack: a `div` with the class `r-stack` of reveal.js, with one child `div` for each version. Give each child but the first the class `fragment` and a `data-fragment-index`, in increasing steps. A child is shown from its step until the step of the next child, and a first child without `fragment` is shown from the start. Keep the elements shown with every child, e.g. the heading and a closing remark, outside the stack.

```html
<section class="wide">
  <h2>CR3BP dynamics</h2>
  <div class="r-stack">
    <div><p>…</p><div class="math">…</div></div>
    <div class="fragment" data-fragment-index="2"><p>…</p><div class="math">…</div></div>
    <div class="fragment" data-fragment-index="3"><p>…</p><div class="math">…</div></div>
  </div>
  <p class="small fragment" data-fragment-index="2">…</p>
</section>
```

A stack is laid out in the flow of its slide, with every child at the upper left of the same place. The stack is as tall as its tallest child, and an element after it is laid out below it. Each child is as wide as the content of the slide, and its elements are laid out as on a slide of the same layout, e.g. `wide` or `narrow`. A child with a later child shown is hidden, and a child is not dimmed when a later step is shown, although the steps inside it are. In the print view and in the PowerPoint file, each step is drawn with the child shown at this step. Do not write `current-visible`, `fade-out`, or `fade-in-then-out` on elements that are replaced in place. A stack can be put in the text column of a split slide as well.

In the editor, each child of a stack is shown alone under its own tab at the slide, as if it were a separate slide. A slide with steps is shown in the editor at its last tab, as it is after its last step. It is thus shown so when you move to it, in its thumbnail, in the copy of a hovered thumbnail, and in Slide Sorter. A tab that you choose stays shown while you stay on the slide, and it is not written into the file. On a slide without a stack, it is shown again when you come back to the slide. Play Steps, Preview, and Present start before the first step. The children of the other tabs are hidden, and they cannot be clicked, selected with Tab or a marquee, or snapped to. The elements outside the stack and those of the tab shown are listed in the Object List. A text box or a paste is put into the child shown, and the elements of the other tabs stay where they are. The tab `+` after the tabs adds a copy of the child shown, with a step just before the step of the next child, and shows it. Delete Tab, in the menu of a tab, takes off its child. Open the menu with a right click or a long press on the tab, or press Delete on the focused tab. When one child is left, its elements are put in place of the stack, at the places at which they are drawn. In the Steps pane, a child is a row of the kind Stack child, which is not dragged and cannot be given a step, a start, or an effect, since its step is set by the tabs. A click on it shows its step tab, and the menu of the row only plays the steps from it. Arrange > Show on Every Tab moves the selected element out of the stack, so that it is shown with every child, and once more moves it into the child shown. Insert > Stack puts the selected elements into a new stack with one child. The steps of the slide are renumbered by each of these commands, and the elements keep the places at which they are drawn.

On a slide whose elements are replaced in place with `current-visible`, `fade-out`, or `fade-in-then-out`, Make Stack in the Slide menu makes these elements a stack with a child for each tab. The command is dimmed on other slides. Each such element is put into the child of the tab in which it is shown. An element shown at the first step of its tab loses its step, and the others become plain fragments. The other fragments and the elements without a step stay outside the stack, and every element keeps the place at which it is drawn. Make Stack refuses an element that is shown in more than one tab, or one inside a column, and names it in a message. It also refuses an element that would be drawn at another size inside the stack, e.g. one placed by a style of the deck written for the children of the slide, such as `section#id > .math`. The slide is then left as it was.

## The editor

You can change the words, the formulas, the speaker notes, the steps, and the places of the figures of a deck on the slides themselves, without opening `index.html` in a text editor. The editor is loaded only when the parameter `?edit` is in the address of the deck, and a deck without it does not load any of it. The page is laid out like the normal view of PowerPoint. The title bar at the top is made of the menus, the name of the file with its state after it in a muted color, and the Autosave switch. In a window too narrow for this row, the name of the file and its state are cut first, and Which? and Autosave are then drawn without their names. Under it is the toolbar, with the buttons View, Zoom, Undo, Redo, Delete, Add Slide, Table, Text, Formula, Shape, Media, Preview, Present, Save, Format, and Steps. In a window too narrow for the toolbar, its groups are folded from the right into the button More, apart from the first group and the group of Format, and their buttons are listed in the menu of More. A folded button with a list, i.e. Shape, Media, Preview, or View in a customized toolbar, is a submenu of More with the items of its arrow. Open the submenu with a hover or the right arrow, and close it with the left arrow or Esc. Navigator is the first item of the submenu of View, before a rule, and while a preview plays, Play Slide in the submenu of Preview is named Stop. Trace Backdrop and Play Steps are thus reached with the keyboard in a window 760 px wide as well. The list of the slides is at the left, the slide in the middle, a pane for the notes under it, and a pane for the steps at the right. The status bar at the bottom is made of the slide number, the buttons Slides, Steps, and Notes, and the zoom. Drag the zoom slider, pinch, or turn the wheel with Ctrl to scale the slide, and the backdrops are drawn sharp again once the gesture ends. The slide is zoomed by the whole movement of a pinch, also of a slow one, and the grabbed point of a drag is kept under the pointer through a pinch. In a window narrower than 760 px, the zoom slider of the status bar is hidden.

1. Open `index.html` in a browser, with a double click or from a web server, and press E, which opens the same slide in the editor, or add `?edit` to the end of the address, e.g. `file:///Users/you/talk/index.html?edit`. When the address ends in a slide such as `#/3`, put `?edit` before the `#`.
2. After a double click, a dialog asks you to choose the file of the deck, since a file on your disk is not readable by a page until you choose it. Choose the same `index.html`, whose folder is named on the dialog. From a web server, the file is read by the editor itself, and the dialog is not shown.
3. Click a text element to select it as a box, as in Keynote, and click it again to put the caret at the point of the click and type. A double click puts the caret in at once with the word under the pointer selected, and Return on a selected text element puts the caret at the end of its text. A click on a cell of a table selects the cell, as in Excel, as described under [Tables](#tables). A click on a text element that is never selected as a box, e.g. a `span` of words in a `div` of a talk, puts the caret in at once. Enter starts a new line in the same text element, a paste is inserted as plain text with an item for each line pasted into a list item, and the arrow keys move within the text. Words copied or cut out of a text are put on the clipboard with the TeX of each formula between its delimiters, e.g. `\(a^2\)`, and TeX between `\(` and `\)` or `\[` and `\]` pasted into a text is typeset. The caret goes past a formula at either end of a text with Home and End, or with a click beside the formula. The left and right arrows move the caret into the TeX of an inline formula, and a click on the formula selects its TeX. The TeX is then shown in the text between its delimiters, e.g. `\(a^2\)`, as in Obsidian, and it is edited as words. Delete or Backspace on the selected TeX thus takes off the formula alone. The formula is typeset again once the caret has left its TeX, and the file holds the same text whether the TeX is shown or not. A formula inside selected words is drawn in the color of the selection. One word copied with the space after it, as a double click takes it on Windows, is pasted with its spaces set as in PowerPoint, except inside a word and in code. The space after the word is then dropped before a space, a closing sign, or the end of the text, and a space is put before the word after a letter, a digit, or a closing sign. In a text outside a list and a table, Tab and Shift+Tab do not leave the text, and they stop only at the formulas of the text element. Press Cmd+B or Cmd+I on a Mac, and Ctrl+B or Ctrl+I elsewhere, to make the selected words bold or italic, which are written as `<strong>` and `<em>`, and press the same keys again to take the format off. You can also type Markdown syntax. A span closed by the sign that opened it, i.e. `**bold**` or `__bold__`, `*italic*` or `_italic_`, `` `code` ``, or `~~struck~~`, is written as `<strong>`, `<em>`, `<code>`, or `<s>` when you type its closing sign, and the text typed after it is outside the format. One undo gives back the signs as typed. An underscore inside a word, as in snake_case, is left as typed, and so is Markdown syntax typed in code and in TeX. Markdown Formatting is switched off and on in Edit > Substitutions, next to Smart Quotes. You can also type a formula in TeX. A formula between dollars, `$\theta$`, or between `\(` and `\)` is typeset as an inline formula when you type its closing sign, and it is written as `\(\theta\)`. A paragraph typed as `$$E = mc^2$$` or `\[E = mc^2\]` alone is made a display, `<div class="math">\[E = mc^2\]</div>`, as Insert > Formula writes it. One undo gives back the signs as typed. A dollar before a space or a digit opens nothing, hence "$5 and $10" is left as typed, and so are TeX that MathJax cannot typeset, TeX typed in code and in the speaker notes, and the text of an input method that is still being composed. TeX Formulas is switched off and on in Edit > Substitutions, next to Markdown Formatting. On a Mac, Control with A, B, D, E, F, H, K, L, N, O, P, T, V, or Y is left to the text editing of macOS in a text and in the notes. Control-D thus deletes forward, and it is not taken as Cmd with the letter. Press Esc to leave the text. The text element is then selected, as a text box in PowerPoint, the arrow keys nudge it, and a second Esc deselects it, after which the arrow keys and the buttons in the status bar move between the slides again. The keys of reveal.js for the overview, the black screen, fullscreen, and the speaker view are off in the editor. Every step of a slide is shown at once, and the backdrops are drawn still while you edit. On a slide with an element that is hidden at a step, e.g. with `fade-out`, `current-visible`, or `fade-in-then-out`, the steps are cut into ranges at each step at which an element is hidden. The slide is then shown one range of steps at a time, as it is at the last step of the range. You choose the range with the tabs under the slide. On a slide with a stack, each tab shows one child of the stack, as described under [Stacks](#stacks). The tabs are drawn above the slide when the canvas does not have room under it, and they are reached with Tab and pressed with Enter or Space. When you choose a row in the Steps pane or in the Object List whose element is hidden in the range shown, the editor shows a range in which the element is seen. A text that runs past the edge of the slide, or whose lines are drawn over an image, a video, or a plot of its slide, e.g. after a change of the layout, is outlined in orange. The number of such texts is shown in the state in the title bar.
4. Press Cmd+Z on a Mac and Ctrl+Z elsewhere to undo the last change, whether it is typing, a formula, a step, or a move, and Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y to redo it. The Undo and Redo buttons in the toolbar do the same. An undo shows the slide of the change and puts the caret where the text changed, and once you have undone every change, the deck is marked as saved again. In a number field of the inspector with a typed value that is not entered yet, Cmd+Z or Ctrl+Z puts the shown value back and selects it.
5. Move between the slides with the arrow keys, the buttons in the status bar, or a click in the list at the left, where each slide is named by its title and its layout. Rest the pointer on a row to see a small copy of its slide beside the list, with every step shown. Press G and type the number of a slide to jump to it. Press the Slides button to hide the list, and the choice is kept between visits. Click the View button at the left of the toolbar to show or hide the list as well. In the menu of its arrow are Slide Sorter, Rulers, and Gridlines.
6. Click a display, or reach a formula with Tab and press Enter, to open its TeX in a field under it, with its rendering under the TeX as you type. Apply, or Cmd+Enter on a Mac and Ctrl+Enter elsewhere, typesets it again, and Esc leaves it as it was. An undo or a redo that takes the formula off the slide, e.g. the undo of Insert Formula, closes its field. A TeX with an error is refused with the message of MathJax, an empty TeX is refused as well, and the slide is not touched. While the whole TeX of a display is selected, as the field opens, Cmd+C on a Mac and Ctrl+C elsewhere copies the display as Copy does, and its TeX as plain text. A paste on a slide then adds the display, and a paste into a field or into another app writes the TeX.
7. Press Notes to show the speaker notes of the slide in the pane under it, and type into them. On a slide without notes, you can type into the empty pane, and the notes are saved once they have text. Drag the strip above the pane, or focus it with Tab and press the up and down arrows, to change its height, which is kept between visits.
8. Press Steps to open the pane at the right, where the elements of the slide are listed in reading order with the step at which each appears, as the animation pane of PowerPoint and the build order of Keynote. A formula is named in the list by its TeX. Choose the step of an element in its select, or choose Step 1, new, before the others to show it ahead of the other steps, which are each moved one step later. Choose an effect for an element shown with the slide, e.g. Dim, or Play for a video, to make it a new last step with this effect. Press One by one to show the elements one after the other and All at once to show them with the slide. Elements with the same step appear together, and the number of the step is shown at the upper left of each stepped element on the slide. A heading and the footnote can be given a step too, and One by one leaves them with the slide. A step is written into the file as a reveal.js fragment, i.e. `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. Each group of lines of a code box after the first is listed as a row at its step, and the row is moved with the other steps, while its lines are changed in the Code pane. Choose a row and press Play From, or choose Play From Step in the menu of a row, to play the steps of the slide in place from this step.
9. Drag an element to move it, whether it is selected or not. A drag in the text that has the caret selects words, and a table is dragged by the handle ⠿ at its upper left, or by the band of 6 px around its edge, since a press on a cell edits it. Press Delete or Backspace, or the Delete button after Redo in the toolbar, to delete the selected elements. One click and Delete thus remove a text box. Backspace in a text deletes characters only, and it does not delete an emptied text box. The Delete button is dimmed without a selection, and in a window too narrow for the toolbar it is listed in the menu of More. While the navigator has the focus, the button deletes the selected slides, as Delete in the Edit menu does there. Alt-click, i.e. Option-click on a Mac, selects the element under the pointer one layer behind the selected one, and the front one again after the last, as a person reaches an element hidden under another. Where none of the elements under the pointer is selected, Alt-click selects the second from the front, since a click selects the front one. On a phone, a swipe that starts on a text element that is not selected goes to another slide, and a tap selects it. A press with Alt that becomes a drag still drags freely or drags a copy, as described below. While you drag, the element snaps to the edges and the centers of the other elements of the slide, of the slide itself, and of its own place, and an orange guide shows the line it is aligned with. Hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to drag freely. Hold Option on a Mac, or Ctrl elsewhere, to drag a copy of the element, and hold Shift to keep the drag on the axis along which you moved farther. A copy made by such a drag, by Duplicate, or by a paste on the same slide leaves the other elements placed with `translate` where they are drawn. A duplicate or a paste of a text element in the flow of the slide or of its text column is written right after its source in the file, e.g. for a paragraph, a list, or a table. The place of the copy is written in the `translate` of its `style`. A `translate` is then written on each element that the copy would push, so that the other elements are drawn where they were. On a slide without the sources, a paste is put at the place of the sources. Where this place is taken by a copy of the same clipboard on this slide, the paste is put at the first free place in steps of 20 px down and to the right of it. On the slide of the sources, a paste that would be drawn on its source is put 20 px down and to the right of it, and each later paste one step of 20 px further. Such a paste is then moved on in the same steps past every source and every copy of the same clipboard on the slide. A duplicate is put 20 px down and to the right of its element, and moved on in the same steps past the element and its older duplicates. The place of a copy that is deleted or undone is free for a later paste or duplicate. The arrow keys nudge the selected element by one slide pixel, or by ten with Shift. Nudges less than a second apart are undone together with one Cmd+Z. While you move the element, its offset is shown in slide pixels at the lower right of its frame. A double click on the handle, or Enter on it, puts the element back. A figure is also reached with Tab, which selects it. In a plate or a figure box that holds several figures, e.g. the two videos or scenes of a plate with the class `row`, the first click selects the plate, and a second click on one of them selects it by itself. It is then dragged, nudged, and resized by itself, with its offset in its own `style`, and Esc, or Select Figure in the Edit menu, selects the plate again. A scene so selected is turned by a drag inside it, as everywhere, and moved by a drag on its edge. A figure of one image with its caption is still one element, whose image a second click selects, and a drag of the image moves the figure. With an element selected and no text edited, press Tab to select the next element in the order of the Object List, from back to front, and Shift+Tab to select the one before, around both ends. The offset is written into the tag of the element as the style `translate`, in slide pixels, and nothing else in the layout of the slide is changed. An element turned with its rotate handle or in the Arrange pane is written with the style `rotate`, in whole degrees from 1 to 359. A line with an attached end is the exception, as described under Lines. While you drag the rotate handle, the angle is held at a multiple of 45 degrees within 4 degrees of it, and an orange guide is drawn through the center of the turn while it is held. Hold Shift to turn in steps of 15 degrees, and Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to turn freely. The turn of a group is held at the angle of its first member, and the turn of a selection of several elements at the angle of the element whose frame has the handle. The other elements are turned by the same amount. Rotate Left 90° and Rotate Right 90° in the Arrange menu turn each selected element about its center, and a group about the center of its frame. A group is turned as one change of the history, also a group with a line. These styles are taken off again once the slide is drawn in the same order without them. A layer command does not put an element under a backdrop or a plate over the middle of the element, and it is dimmed where it would. A figure is turned at its image, from the figure and from the image selected by a second click. The `rotate` is then written on its image or video, on both images of a pair for the two modes, and the caption is left where it is.

In Chrome and Edge, each edit is written into its place in `index.html`, and every other byte of the file is kept, hence a diff of the file shows only your changes. From a web server, the first save asks where to write the file. Open with unsaved changes asks whether to save them first, to open a file without them, or to go on editing. Save as, or Cmd+Shift+S and Ctrl+Shift+S, writes the deck into another file, and the next saves go into it. Firefox, Safari, and the browsers built on them do not have the file pickers of Chrome, hence a save there is a download of a copy of `index.html` with your edits, and you replace the original with it. To choose the place of the copy at each save, set the browser to ask where to save each download: in Safari under Settings, General, File download location, and in Firefox under Settings, General, Downloads. In the dialog, choose `index.html` in the folder of the deck and replace it.

In every browser, the unsaved edits are also kept in the storage of the browser, and when the tab is closed or the browser crashes before a save, a dialog offers them the next time the deck is opened: Restore and save writes them into the file and shows the page again, Download a copy puts the file with them into your downloads, and Discard forgets them.

Press Esc or `e`, or move on past the last step of the last slide, to return to the editor at the slide shown last. With unsaved changes, a dialog asks whether to save them first, to discard them and present the saved file, or to go on editing. In Firefox, Safari, and the browsers built on them, a save is a download, and the file at the address of the deck is not the saved one. The show then plays the saved text at the address of the deck, so that `theme/` and the figures are found, and Save and present downloads the copy when the show ends, so that a save dialog of the browser is not opened over the show. Present from Start and Presenter View play the saved text in the same way, and Presenter View downloads the copy before it opens its tab. The speaker view of reveal.js, opened in such a show with `s`, loads its two frames from the address of the deck, hence it shows the file as it was opened until you replace the file with the downloaded copy.

The editor reads the file once a second, through the file you chose or from the web server of the deck. Without unsaved edits, the page is loaded again on the same slide, and the changed slides are marked with a violet dot next to their thumbnails until you show them. With unsaved edits, the state names the changed slides, and the next save merges your edits into the file and loads the page again with both. When you edited a text that the file changed too, a dialog asks whether to keep your version or the file's, and Cancel saves nothing. After the page is loaded again, the editor writes into the same file without asking for it, while the browser lets the page write the file.

## The synchronous editor

With the synchronous editor, several people edit one deck at the same time, each in a browser, and each change is shown to the others as it is made. A session is free, since neither an account nor a paid service is needed.

To host a session, run `npm run edit` in the talk folder, or double-click `Edit.command` on macOS, `Edit.cmd` on Windows, or `Edit.sh` on Linux, for which Node.js 20 or later is needed. The editor is then opened in your browser at an address such as `http://127.0.0.1:8765/<key>/index.html?edit&session`, which is also printed in the window. The port of the last start is used again, 8765 at the first start, and a free port when this one is in use. Choose a port with `npm run edit -- --port N`, keep the browser closed with `--no-open`, and share through a relay from the start with `--relay URL`. Close the window or press Ctrl+C to stop the session. The edits are written into `index.html` within a second, with every other byte kept, and a new image or video into `media/`. The rest of the session is kept in `.session/`, which is ignored by git. When `index.html` cannot be written at the stop, the text is kept in `.session/unwritten-<time>.html`, or in the temporary folder of the system when `.session/` cannot be written, and its path is printed in the window.

A guest joins through a relay, since the edit command can be reached only from the host machine. Press Share in the title bar, type the address of the relay of your group and its secret once, and press Connect. Both are kept in `~/.config/beeson-slides/relay.json`, under `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME` when this variable is set, or in `%APPDATA%\beeson-slides\relay.json` on Windows. The link of the session is then shown with Copy and a QR code. A guest opens the link in a current browser, on a computer or a phone, without installing anything, and types a name under Join the session. In the menu of your own avatar, change the name with Change Name…, and as a guest leave with Leave Session. With Leave Session, the waiting edits are sent first, and the deck can then be downloaded with Download a copy or joined again with Join again. A guest edits as the host does, apart from Open, Open Recent, Revert to Saved, Preamble…, and Bibliography…, which are off for everyone, and apart from TikZ figures, which only the host changes, as described under TikZ figures.

Each person is given a color of the plot palette (`--data-2`, `--data-3`, `--data-5`, or `--data-7`) and an avatar with initials in the title bar. An element that another person selected is framed in this color, with a name flag at its upper left corner, which is collapsed to the initials after 3 s. While a person types, the flag reads e.g. Ana is typing, and the caret of this person is drawn in the same color. Your own other tabs of the deck are not drawn. Several people can type in one text element at once, each at their own caret, and their texts are merged character by character. A caret or a key in a word that another person replaces goes to the end of the new word. When two people type over the same letters of one word at once, the word of one of them is kept whole. When one person takes a format off a word while another types in it with this format off, the text of one of them is kept, and the tags of the paragraph stay balanced. While your caret is in a text element and you typed or put the caret into it within the last 30 s, you hold it. The others can type in an element that you hold, and set bold, italic, code, strikethrough, superscript, subscript, a tone, a link, an alignment, and a case in it. They cannot move, resize, or delete it, split it or join it with the element beside it, or change its style, its list, its size, or its indent. They cannot delete, move, hide, or lay out its slide either, or change the table around it. They are shown why in a bubble, e.g. Ana is typing here. You can do this once they leave it or stop for 30 s. For an element on another slide, the bubble is drawn under the title bar and the toolbar, and it names the slide by its number in the navigator, e.g. Ana is typing on slide 4. For a hidden slide, to which the navigator does not give a number, it reads Ana is typing. The hold ends for everyone when your caret leaves the element, when you have neither typed nor put the caret into it for 30 s, or when your page leaves the session. A page that stops without leaving, e.g. on a computer that goes to sleep, is dropped by the edit command about 40 s after its last request, and its hold is shown until then at the latest. When two people type at the same point at the same moment, their letters may alternate. A word that one of them puts in at once, e.g. with a paste, stays whole beside a word that the other types key by key there, each with the space before it. A word composed with an input method, e.g. in Japanese or with a dead key, is put in at its point when it is committed, after what the others typed meanwhile. When a text element of a plate or a figure box is held, e.g. a caption, another person can still click or tap another figure of the plate. This figure is then selected and can be moved, and a second click selects its image. A drag of the plate itself is refused. Other edits are merged as they come, and where two edits other than typing change the same characters, the later one is kept without a question. When another person puts a format around a word in which you type at the same moment, e.g. italic or a link, both are kept, and your keys are inside the new element. When one person moves slides or paragraphs, also several at once, at the moment at which another person types in them, the move is kept, and the typed text moves with them. This holds as well when the mover types in the moved text. When two people move the same slide at once, it is in the deck once. A move that another edit of the moved lines cuts across, e.g. the removal of a line in them, is undone, and the typed texts of both stay. When the edits of two people at once would leave an end tag that does not close the element opened last, e.g. a list split in two, the edits of the other person are kept, and of yours only the typed text outside them. Your text as it was before is then set aside, and you are told so in a toast: Your edit clashed with an edit of another person, and was dropped. It is kept under Set aside in Version History. When another person adds or removes a formula in the text element of your open Edit Formula sheet, or changes its formula, Apply is refused with the message Another person changed the formulas of this text. Copy your TeX, press Cancel, and open the formula again. The deck is then not changed, and your TeX is kept in the sheet. The same holds for Insert, and for a click away from the Insert Formula sheet of a new formula, which then stays open. When another person removes the whole text element of your open sheet of a formula, or of your open list of citations, the sheet or the list is closed.

In the navigator and the Slide Sorter, a slide is marked with a dot in the color of each person on it. In the navigator, a slide is also marked with a violet dot when someone else, or a change of `index.html` from outside, changed it since you last showed it. Click an avatar to follow its person. You then see the slide of this person at your zoom, inside a border in the color of this person, under the label Following Ana with Stop. Following stops when you press Stop, edit, select, change the slide, present, or open Version History, and it is kept through a zoom or a scroll. Right-click an avatar, or press and hold it, for its menu with Follow or Stop Following, and Go to Slide.

In place of Autosave, the button of the save state reads Saved, or Saved to index.html for the host. Each edit is sent as it is made, and with Save or Save As…, the waiting edits are sent at once, without a file. A new image or video is sent to the edit command before the edit that names it. While it is sent, your edits that do not name it are sent as well. When it cannot be sent, e.g. after a timeout, the save state reads e.g. Could not send media/plot.png. Try again, with the button Try again, and a file that failed on the network is sent again once the page is back online. A file may be refused for good, e.g. by the edit command when media/ leaves the talk folder, or by a proxy in front of the relay that refuses its size. The edit is then sent with the path of the file, which is missing from the talk folder. The file and the reason are then named in the save state of the page that added the file. On the host's page this line ends with Delete it from the deck, or add the file to media/ by hand and load the page again. On a guest's page it ends with Delete it from the deck, or ask the host to add the file to media/, then load the page again. The other pages are not told. This line is shown while the deck names the file, until the page is loaded again. The save state also shows a warning of the editor, e.g. why a dropped file is not added, until your next edit. While the save state shows a line that asks you to act, e.g. a file that is given up or an upload that failed, the warning follows this line. A line of the save state that is too long for the title bar is cut at its end, and its whole text is shown when you rest the pointer on it. In a narrow title bar, a warning takes a line of its own under the buttons, on which it is wrapped. It is at most five lines high there, and a longer warning is scrolled. To keep a copy of the deck, choose File > Export To > Editable HTML…. Undo and Redo are applied to your own changes. In a text element in which others typed meanwhile, Undo takes out only what you typed. It is refused when your letters cannot be told apart from theirs, or when another person changed them after you typed them. A bubble beside the text element then says why, e.g. Your typing cannot be undone without changing the letters of another person, and the next Undo takes the change before it. In a text element that another person changed meanwhile, you can undo your latest entry of typing and the eight entries before it, and an Undo of an earlier entry is refused in the same way. An entry that would write over a later change of someone else is dropped, and an Undo or Redo that would move, delete, split, or join an element that another person holds is refused, also when it comes from the Edit menu of the browser. A change of `index.html` from outside, e.g. by an agent or a git pull, is merged for everyone within a second, and the version of the file is kept where the session changed the same text. When the file is written back with a text that the session wrote after the last change from outside, e.g. by an editor that saved a copy that it did not reload, the newer edits are kept. An older text, e.g. from `git checkout -- index.html`, `git stash`, or `git reset --hard` after a pull, is merged as a change from outside, and takes back the edits of the session since this text for everyone. To go back, restore a version.

The host can use git in the talk folder during a session. `git add`, `git commit`, and `git push` leave the session as it is. `git commit -am` leaves out a new image or video of `media/`, which `git add media` adds. A `git pull` is refused while `index.html` holds edits that are not committed. Commit them, run `git pull --no-rebase`, and repeat both when the pull is refused again. When the pull stops at a conflict in `index.html`, the file holds the conflict markers of git, i.e. lines that start with `<<<<<<<`, `=======`, and `>>>>>>>`. The session then keeps its last text without them, the edits of the session are not written into the file, and the save state of every page reads The deck cannot be written on the host machine: index.html holds the conflict markers of git. Resolve the conflict and remove the markers. The file is then merged into the session as a change from outside, and the edits of the session are written into it. At a stop before then, these edits are not in `index.html`, and the edit command ends with code 1.

To present the deck from your own computer, choose File > Export To > Talk Folder…, as a guest or as the host. Your waiting edits are sent first. When they are not sent within 4 s, e.g. while you are offline, nothing is downloaded, and you are asked to try again once they are saved. A zip of the talk folder is then built by the edit command, and it is downloaded in pieces of 2 MB, four at a time. How much of it has arrived is shown in the dialog Talk Folder. The zip and the one folder in it are named after the talk folder, e.g. `my-talk.zip` with the folder `my-talk`. In this name, a character that Windows refuses in a file name, or that Chrome replaces in the name of a download, is written as `-`. A dot, a space, or a `~` at its start is left out, as is a dot or a space at its end. The name is cut to 64 characters, with an emoji counted as two. In a name of a device of Windows, a `-` is written after the device, e.g. `aux-` and `COM1-.txt`, and a name of which nothing is left becomes `talk`. In the zip are `index.html` as the session has it at this moment, and every other file that the edit command serves, as listed under Trust and limits. Among them are `theme/` with the editor, `brand/` with its notice, `media/`, and the launchers. A file below the talk folder is zipped under its name. One whose name Windows refuses, e.g. `aux.png`, is therefore not unzipped there. In a talk folder with `preamble.tex` or `references.bib`, the `tex.js` of the zip is the one of this `index.html`, also before the edit command has written it. The outputs `check/`, `slides.pdf`, and `slides.pptx` are left out. Unzip the file first, unless your browser has already unzipped it, as Safari does on a Mac by default. A deck is not shown from inside a zip, which Windows opens like a folder. Then open `index.html` in the unzipped folder with a double click. A JSON file named in `data-trajectories` cannot be read by a deck opened with a double click. For the zip, such a file of the talk folder is therefore wrapped in `Beeson.trajectories(` and `);` under its name with `.js`, e.g. `orbits.json.js`, or with a number when this name is taken, e.g. `orbits.json-2.js`. The wrapped file is named in the `index.html` of the zip. On a phone or a tablet, also an iPad with a mouse or a trackpad, you are first told that a deck is opened there from a web server, as described under Browsers. The zip is then downloaded with Download, to be moved to a computer. On a tablet of Windows without a keyboard or a mouse, the zip is downloaded at once, since a deck is opened from the disk there. An Android tablet that has a mouse and asks for the desktop site is not told apart from a computer. The same is therefore said at the end of a download on a computer. A talk folder whose files are over 4,095 MB in all, the most that a zip without the Zip64 extension holds, is refused with its size in the message. The same limit is set for guests and for the host. When a file of the talk folder is changed during the download, e.g. by `npm run tex`, nothing is downloaded, and the file is named in a message that asks you to try again. A zip of more than 65,535 files is refused as well. The `tex.js` of the session and each wrapped file are counted. Outside a session, Talk Folder… is not in the menu.

To give the talk to someone as one file, choose File > Export To > Single Web Page…, as a guest or as the host. It is named after the Single File Web Page of PowerPoint. Your waiting edits are sent first, as for Talk Folder…. The edit command then builds one HTML file named after the talk folder, e.g. `my-talk.html`, and it is downloaded in pieces of 2 MB, four at a time. In this file, each image, video, poster, script, icon, and style sheet that `index.html` names is written in place as a `data:` URL, and so are the fonts and images that a style sheet names. The files that the deck loads while it is shown, i.e. the data of the backdrops, the files of `data-trajectories`, the TikZ figures, and the extensions of MathJax, are listed in a script at the start of `head` in `window.beesonFiles`. A file of the talk folder that the deck does not name is left out, e.g. the editor, the launchers, and an image of `media/` that is on no slide. Open the file with a double click, without the talk folder and without a network, or from a web server. It shows every slide and step in both modes and on phones, with the speaker notes and the speaker view. A video or another file of over 8 MB of text is turned into a `blob:` URL when the page opens, since Safari does not play a video of over about 50 MB from a `data:` URL. Opened with `?edit`, the file says that it cannot be edited. Choose Editable HTML… to keep a copy of the deck that can be edited. A file that the deck names and that is not in the talk folder, or that is over 384 MB, the most that Chrome reads in one attribute, is left out and named in the message at the end of the download. When a file of the talk folder is changed during the download, nothing is downloaded, as for Talk Folder…. A talk folder of 220 MB gave a file of 179 MB, built in 0.1 s while the edit command held 12 MB. Its first slide was shown in 1.1 s in Chrome and in 1.9 s in Safari, against 0.5 s and 0.4 s from the talk folder. The speaker view loads the file again in each of its two frames.

In a session, File > Preamble… and File > Bibliography… edit `preamble.tex` and `references.bib` in the talk folder, for the host and for a guest. A source is saved about a second after your last key in its sheet, when you close the sheet, and at once for a BibTeX entry pasted into the list of citations. The edit command writes the save into the talk folder through a temporary file and a rename, and only over the text that your sheet read. When someone else saved the same source since, your edits are merged into their text, and the sheet tells you so. When your edits clash with theirs, the source is not saved, and you choose Keep My Text or Use Their Text in the sheet. Use Their Text is undone with the other edits. The others who have the same sheet open are shown under its heading, each in the tone of the person, and the label of their avatar reads e.g. editing references.bib. The host can also edit both files in the talk folder, e.g. in a text editor. The edit command writes `tex.js` with the text that `npm run tex` writes, after it writes an `index.html` that cites another set of papers. It also writes `tex.js` within about a second after a source, or a file that the preamble inputs, has stopped changing on disk. It does not write `tex.js` at its start, hence run `npm run tex` before the start when `tex.js` is not the one written from the sources and the citations of the deck. Nor does it write one in a talk folder that has neither `preamble.tex` nor `references.bib`. While one of these files, or a file that the preamble inputs, cannot be read, `tex.js` is not written either, and the reason is printed in the window, also at the start of the edit command. After each change of `tex.js` on disk, also by `npm run tex`, every page draws the deck again with it, without a reload. An open Edit Formula sheet draws its preview with the new `tex.js` at the next key that you type. A paper added to `references.bib` is found by the next search for a citation, and an open list of citations shows it once you type the next key. A BibTeX entry pasted into this list is added to `references.bib`, as outside a session. When the edit command cannot write `tex.js`, the save state of every page reads tex.js cannot be written on the host machine, followed by the reason, and the write is tried again until it succeeds.

When the connection is lost, you go on editing, and the save state reads e.g. Offline. Changes on slides 3 and 5 are kept in this browser. The waiting edits are kept in the browser for 30 days, also through a closed tab, and they are merged with those of the others at the reconnect or at the next join of the same address. The browser writes this copy within about 2 s of an edit, and at once when the page is hidden or closed. Until the copy is written, and unless the edit command has received the edit, you are asked before the page is closed or loaded again. Safari on an iPhone or an iPad does not show this question, and Chrome on Android may not show it. While you are online, the waiting edits are also sent when the page is hidden or closed. The copy written at this moment is kept in the local storage of the browser, which holds about 5 MB for each address and port. It holds the deck once, with the changed text. An edit of the last 2 s on such a phone is therefore lost only when the deck does not fit there, i.e. a deck of a few MB. When the host has started the edit command again in between and the deck has changed, you are asked first in the dialog Changes from an earlier session, with Download my version, Discard them, and Add my changes. After more than 5 s offline, the texts that someone else changed as well are listed in the dialog Others changed texts that you edited offline, with Keep mine, Keep theirs, and Download my version. When the host stops the edit command, each guest is told in the dialog Ana ended the session whether the host received the last changes, with Download a copy. A guest who was offline at this time is told the same at the reconnect, up to 24 h later. The other pages of the host are told that the session has ended, and the edit command waits at most 3 s for them before it exits. With End session in the Share dialog, the host ends the session for the guests and goes on editing.

A browser opens at most six connections to one address, shared by all its tabs. Three tabs of a session in one browser each keep one of them open to wait for the news of the others, and each further tab asks for the news once a second. A seventh tab, e.g. one opened after the presenter view, a few follow views, and spare tabs, thus joins at once, and the edits of the others reach it within about a second.

Open Version History in the File menu or in the menu of the save state. A version is a state of the deck. It is kept 60 s after the last edit or at the latest 5 min after its first edit, and when someone tags the current state, restores a version, or stops the session. The versions are listed newest first, with their times, authors, and changed slides, and a change from outside is listed as Changed in index.html outside the editor. Such a change is always a version by itself. When it replaces text that someone wrote in the session, everyone is told in a toast, and its button Show opens Version History, where this text is in the version before the change. The last version of the list is the deck as it was before the first edit, and it is listed as Start of the history. With Tag this version, you give a version a tag of at most 80 characters, in which characters that are not drawn are left out. To take the tag off, empty the field and press Remove tag. With Only show tagged versions, you hide the others. Click a version to see the deck at this version, with each changed element outlined in the color of its author under Show changes. In a changed text element, the characters that a person added are tinted in the color of this person as well. A text that a person removed from a text element is shown at its place, struck through in the color of this person, and its tooltip names the person. The text of a removed element is not shown there, since the bar of the preview names the removed elements, and neither is a removed text of more than 4096 characters. An element with a formula or a script keeps the outline alone, and every element in a browser without the CSS Custom Highlight API keeps the outline and the struck text. With Download This Version in the More menu, you download the deck as it was at this version. While the pane is open, the deck cannot be edited. The toolbar, the menus, the navigator, the notes, the Slide Sorter, the Object List, and the status bar are disabled and drawn dimmed, and you go back with Back to editor or Esc. A change of the deck for which the page has to be loaded again is held until then, and Current version reads The deck changed. It is loaded again when Version History is closed. On a phone, the pane is drawn over the lower half of the page, and the slide is fitted above it. A long tag of a version is cut in the bar of the preview there, and the selected row of the pane shows it in full. After you confirm Restore this version, the deck is made to read as the version for everyone, as a new version on top, and the others are told in a toast. An edit that someone sends during a restore is applied on top of the restored deck. Only `index.html` is kept in a version, hence the other files of the talk folder stay as they are at a restore, e.g. `preamble.tex`, `references.bib`, and the TikZ figures. The versions are kept in `.session/history.jsonl`. When the edit command is killed, e.g. by a power loss, the edits since the last version are listed at the next start as one version, under the people who made them. With Keep theirs in the dialog Others changed texts that you edited offline, your version of the deck is set aside. It is then listed under Set aside and kept in `.session/aside/`, and it is previewed and downloaded as a version is. Its preview is drawn without outlines, with a button for each slide that reads differently from the current deck, and it opens on the first of these slides.

To write a comment on the selected element, the text element of your caret, or else the slide, choose Comment in the Insert menu, the toolbar, or the context menu, which also takes an element that another person holds. The draft is opened at the top of the Comments pane of the inspector, under its place, e.g. On Heading: Low-thrust transfers or On Slide 4. Type the text and press Comment. Show or hide the pane with View > Comments or with the button Comments in the title bar. The count on this button is the number of the unread comments of others in the threads that you wrote, replied to, or are mentioned in. It counts the resolved ones as well, which are listed under Resolved and not under For You. Outside a session, Comment is not in the editor, and its key is left to the browser.

A comment and its replies are a thread. The threads are listed under the filters Open, For You, and Resolved, by slide in the order of the deck, with the threads of removed slides first under On the deck. Under For You, the open threads that you wrote, replied to, or are mentioned in are listed. A thread with a comment of someone else that you have not read is marked with a violet bar at its left. On the canvas, an element or a slide with open threads is marked at its upper right corner with a badge of their count, filled violet while one of them is unread. In the navigator, a slide is marked with the count of the open threads on it and on its elements, filled violet while one of them is unread. Hide the badges of the canvas with View > Comment Badges. Only its author can edit or delete a comment, with Edit or Delete in its menu. To delete the first comment of a thread is to delete the whole thread, after the question Delete this thread?. A reply that you type is kept when someone else resolves its thread, which is reopened when the reply is sent. When someone else deletes its thread, the reply becomes the draft of a new comment on the same element. A draft is kept on its element when you change the slide, and Comment chosen while a draft is open moves this draft with its text to the new place. Type @ at the start of a comment or after a space to list at most six of the other people who have joined a session of the talk. The list is narrowed by the letters typed after the @, and the people in the session come first, marked Here. Pick a name with a click, or with the arrow keys and Enter or Tab, to mention its person. A name typed by hand is text. When an edit or a delete takes the last @name of a person out of a thread, the thread is no longer listed under For You of this person, unless they wrote or replied to it. A mentioned person who is in the session is told in a toast, e.g. Ana mentioned you on slide 4, whose Show opens the thread. When more than three toasts wait, those of comments are joined into one, e.g. 4 new comments, whose Show opens For You. To take the comments to another computer, copy `.session/people.json` together with `.session/comments.json`, since the authors of the comments and the people mentioned in them are known by the names kept there. When its slide is deleted, the thread is listed as On a removed slide under On the deck, without a badge. It is attached again when the same element is put back on the same slide, e.g. by Undo or by a paste after a cut, or when the whole slide is put back. At a restore of a version, the threads are moved as at an edit. While Version History is open, the badges are hidden, the button Comments is dimmed and out of reach, and the toasts of mentions are held until it is closed. To run a relay for your group, start it on a computer that everyone can reach, behind a proxy that serves it over https, which a guest needs.

```sh
SLIDES_RELAY_SECRET=<a secret of 16 or more characters> npm run relay -- --port 8787 --host 127.0.0.1
```

With `--host 127.0.0.1`, the relay is reached only through the proxy. Caddy is one such proxy, and it gets the certificate of the address by itself.

```sh
caddy reverse-proxy --from relay.example.org --to 127.0.0.1:8787
```

Behind nginx, set `client_max_body_size 16m`, since the relay carries every file that a guest loads, and `proxy_read_timeout 90s`, since a guest waits up to 60 s for the edit command.

The secret can also be read from a file with `--secret-file PATH`, and the relay is bound to another address with `--host ADDRESS`. Everything is kept in memory, and the relay does not read or write a file apart from the secret file. The limits of a relay are 64 sessions, 256 waiting requests per session, and bodies of 256 MB in all, a quarter of them for one session. The body of a request of a guest is limited to 16 MB, and an answer of the edit command to 64 MB. A session is dropped after 90 s without its edit command. Until then, it is kept by the edit command that registered it, which keeps the token of the relay in `.session/relay.json`, also after it is killed and started again. Another edit command with the same key, e.g. in a copy of the talk folder, is refused, and so is anyone with the secret and the key while the network of the host is cut. The end of a session is kept for 24 h, for a guest who comes back in this time. At most 256 ends are kept, each of at most 1 kB.

### Trust and limits

- Everyone who has the link can edit the deck until the session ends, and can thus also write a script into it, which is then run in the browser of every person of the session. Give the link only to people whom you trust with the deck.
- Everyone who has the link can also read every file of the talk folder and of its subfolders, e.g. notes kept next to the deck. A file or a folder is not served when its name starts with a dot, ends with a dot or a space, holds a colon or a backslash, or is `node_modules`. A link that leaves the talk folder is not served either. Keep a file that the others are not to read outside the talk folder while a session runs.
- A name is not verified, hence anyone with the link can join under the name of someone else.
- Every session of one relay is served from one origin. With a script in the deck of one session, a person can read what another session of the same relay left in the same browser, e.g. its key, hence a relay is for one group whose members trust one another.
- A copy of slides or elements made in a page of a session names its deck by a hash of the key of the session in place of the key, also after the session ends and through a relay under a path, hence the key is not written into the clipboard. Every folder of the address whose name is of 22 to 128 letters, digits, `-`, or `_` is named by a hash in this way, since it may be a key. A page of the edit command opened without `session` in its address, e.g. `index.html?edit`, writes its address with the key into a copy. A page with `session` in its address that an edit command does not serve, e.g. from `file://` or from a web server, names its own address, as outside a session.
- A session edits `index.html` of the talk folder. Another page of the talk folder that is opened through the edit command with `session` in its address, also one in a subfolder, is edited without a session. The dialog This page is not the deck of the session then says so. In a copy made in such a page, the deck is named by the hash of the key as well.
- The copy of unsent edits kept in a browser is found by the origin and the path of the page. It is lost to the session when the edit command is started on another port or the address of the relay is changed.
- A session takes at most 64 pages, at most 48 of them through the relay, and at most 8 pages of one person. A page past these limits is not let in. The session is full is then shown in its save state, and the join is tried again until a place is free.
- A session takes images named `.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.gif`, `.webp`, `.avif`, `.svg`, or `.bmp`, videos named `.mp4`, `.m4v`, `.webm`, `.mov`, or `.ogv`, and the PDFs of PDF figures. An image or a PDF that is added in a session, and any file that a guest adds, is limited to 16 MB, and a video that the host adds to 64 MB. A run of the edit command takes at most 1000 new files and 2 GB of them in all, from every page together. A file over these limits is not sent, and the page that added it names the file and the reason in its save state, until the host starts the edit command again. A file with another extension or over its limit is not added, and the reason is shown in the save state. For another extension, the extension and the formats to save the file as are given with it, i.e. a PNG or a JPEG for an image, and an MP4 or a WebM for a video. A PDF figure is added in a session as without one, by the host and by a guest. Its renders are made in the page that adds it, and the PDF and its renders are sent into `media/` before the others see the figure. When the edit command gives the PDF another name, e.g. beside a file of the same name, `data-pdf` is written with this name, as `src` is for an image. The Page and Match the Mode of a PDF figure can be changed in every page of the session, and the new renders are sent as images. On the page of a guest, this is refused for a PDF of over 16 MB, the limit of a file sent through the relay, and the reason is shown in the title bar.
- A key of yours may be placed inside a text that another person types or pastes over a selected word of the same text element at the same moment. E.g. when you type Q into `beta` while the other person types `theta` over this word, the text reads `theQta`. Every page shows the same text afterward.
- A word that you compose with an input method, e.g. for Chinese or Japanese, while a command of yours removes the element after its paragraph may be lost together with this removal. This happens when another person types at the end of this paragraph at the same moment.
- A file of the talk folder over 16 MB is sent through the relay only in ranges, as they are requested for a video, hence a guest does not load an image of over 16 MB.
- A guest loads `index.html` whole at every load. A deck of over 64 MB is therefore not loaded through a relay, and the connection of the host has to send the deck within 60 s, e.g. 3 Mbit/s for a deck of 22 MB.
- While a page downloads the talk folder, its zip is kept by the edit command, until the download ends or 120 s after the last piece was requested. Only the compressed text files of a zip are kept in memory. Images, videos, PDFs, and other files over 16 MB are read from the disk for each piece, also through a relay, since a piece is answered within the limit of 64 MB of an answer of the relay. A zip is dropped after 30 s when a piece of it was not requested, e.g. after Cancel while it was built. The text files of the zips that are kept and built at one time are limited to 512 MB in memory. A page past this limit is told to try again in a minute, before a zip is built for it. Each piece of a zip has to be sent by the connection of the host within 60 s, i.e. at 0.3 Mbit/s or more for each page that downloads at the same time.
- A single web page is built in the same way as a zip, under the same limits of size and memory, and its images, videos, and PDFs are read from the disk for each piece. A file over 384 MB is left out of it.
- The edit command is bound to 127.0.0.1, the host machine.
- Of the files of the talk folder, a page writes only `preamble.tex` and `references.bib`. A preamble saved by a guest may input only files that the edit command serves, since the macros of any other file of the host would be written into `tex.js`, which everyone reads. A preamble saved by the host may input any file.
- Everyone who has the link can read every version of the deck and every text set aside, also of the time before this person joined.
- In the preview of a version, the scripts of this version are run in the page of the session.
- Nobody is told of a change from outside that replaces text of the session when the edit command was started again between the typing and the change. The text is still in the version before the change.
- A history of over 32 MB is shortened to the versions of the last 30 days. The tagged versions are kept as well, and each holds the changes of the versions left out before it.
- A history that cannot be read when the edit command is started is kept as `.session/history-unreadable-<time>.jsonl`, and a new history is begun. The path of the kept file is printed in the window of the edit command.
- A text set aside is limited to 8 MB. At most 50 of them are kept per person, and 256 MB of them in all, of which the oldest is removed first.
- Everyone who has the link can read every comment, also those written before this person joined. A comment is edited and deleted only by its author, who is known by the name alone, hence anyone who joins under your name can edit and delete your comments. A person who types a name that was used in the deck before is told so in a dialog, and joins under it with Yes, continue.
- A comment is limited to 4000 characters, in which a mention counts as 25, and to 50 mentions. A thread takes at most 200 replies, and a deck at most 2000 threads and 8 MB of comments in all. A comment over a limit is not sent, and the reason is shown under its field.
- Two elements, or two slides, are equal when their text in `index.html` is the same, e.g. two empty items of a list. A change made in the editor keeps the threads on their elements, also next to an equal element. When one of two equal elements is removed in the editor, the same one is taken off on the pages of the others. A change from outside the editor, e.g. an edit of `index.html` in another program or a restore of a version, does not tell which of two equal elements next to each other it removes or puts a copy before. When it removes the first of them, its threads stay on the element that is kept, and the threads of the kept element are listed as On a removed element. When a page reads the whole text of the session again, e.g. after the edit command was started again, a removal on this page and a removal by another person of two equal elements next to each other are taken as one, and one of the two elements is kept.
- A thread on a removed element or slide is attached again only when the element or slide comes back with every character as before. While an equal copy of a removed element is in the deck, the thread can be attached to this copy when the copy is moved to another slide. The thread of an element longer than 4096 characters stays listed as On a removed element when the edit that puts the element back also removes such a copy.
- Several slides may be changed in one change, e.g. by an edit of `index.html` outside the editor, by edits made offline, or at a restore of a version. A slide is then found again when at least half of the shorter of its two texts is kept at its start and its end. Any other slide is found again by its first element. This element has to keep its words, or its start tag when it is without words, e.g. an image or a backdrop. When the slide is without any words before or after the change, its first element has to keep its name. A slide that is not found again is taken as removed, although it is kept. It is listed as removed in the preview of a version, and its threads and those of its elements are listed as On a removed slide. In the preview alone, a slide is also taken as changed when this half is kept as one stretch of three or more whole words through its middle, at its place between two unchanged slides. An example is a slide whose heading and notes are changed. A slide replaced at its place by another slide with such a stretch is then listed as changed as well.
- When two slides are joined into one in such a change, the threads on the elements of one of them may be listed as On a removed slide, although these elements are kept.
- A `comments.json` that cannot be read when the edit command is started is kept as `.session/comments-unreadable-<time>.json`, whose path is printed in the window of the edit command, and the threads that can be read are kept. When the file can be neither moved nor copied, the comments cannot be changed until the edit command is started again. When `comments.json` cannot be written at the stop, its path and the reason are printed in the window.

## Text and tables

Write bold as `strong`, italic as `em`, an underline as `u`, a strikethrough as `s`, a superscript as `sup`, a subscript as `sub`, and code as `code`. Inline code is drawn in the monospace of the system on a tinted rectangle with rounded corners, tinted with the text color of the mode, and the code of a code box is drawn without this rectangle. To color words, put them in a `span` with one class out of `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, `tone-red`, `tone-subtle`, and `tone-muted`, which are drawn in `--accent`, `--violet`, `--blue`, `--red`, `--subtle`, and `--muted` in each mode. With `tone-text`, the words are drawn in `--text`, e.g. in a small paragraph. Such a color is shown from the start of the slide, and a color that is a step is set with `data-highlight`, as described under Stepped math. In the Text pane of the editor, the pop-up Step under the swatches is set to From the Start, which writes these classes. An underline is drawn in the color of its text, and the underline of a link in `--accent`. In the Format menu of the editor, Underline is set with Cmd+U on a Mac and Ctrl+U elsewhere, Strikethrough is set with Cmd+Shift+X on a Mac and Alt+Shift+5 elsewhere, Superscript and Subscript are under Baseline, and the colors are under Text Color. There, choose Text to write `tone-text` on the words of a text element drawn in another color, i.e. Small, Caption, and Footnote, or to take the span off in a text element drawn in the text color. With Clear Formatting, every format is taken off, apart from a link, a formula, and a span of a colored symbol. With Paragraph Style, a text element is written as `p` for Body, `p.small` for Small, `h3` for Subheading, `p.caption` for Caption, and `div.foot` for Footnote.

Give a heading, a paragraph, a list, an item, a cell of a table, a caption, or a `math` div the class `center` to center its words, or its display in a `math` div. The class is not written on a `section`, where reveal.js reads it in its print layout. In the editor, choose Center, Cmd+Shift+| on a Mac, or Align Left in Format > Alignment or in the section Alignment of the Text pane, which writes or takes off the class on the smallest text element that the selection means. With the caret or selected words in an item or a cell, this is the item or the cell, and with a range of cells, each cell of the range. A selected list is centered as one. A cell set at the right, i.e. with `text-align: right` in its `style`, loses this style with either choice. With the Format Painter, the class is copied as well.

A heading, a paragraph, a list, a `math` div, or a caption can be given a width in px in its `style`, e.g. `style="width: 720px"`, as a text box of PowerPoint. Its text is then wrapped at this width, even past the text column, and its height is the height of the wrapped text. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected text element.

A numbered list is counted from 1, and the attribute `start` of its `ol` sets the number of its first item, e.g. `<ol start="4">` for a list continued after a paragraph, or `<ol start="0">` for steps counted from 0. Any whole number is a start, 0 and negative numbers included. In the editor, type the number into the field Start at of the Text pane, or step it with the arrows of the field, where a 1 takes the attribute off. Return on an empty item in the middle of a numbered list cuts the list at a new paragraph, and the second list is given the `start` with which its count goes on, as in Keynote. The number of an item is drawn with its end 30 px before its words for any count of digits, hence a number of two digits or more is drawn partly in the margin at the left of the list.

The items of a `ul` are marked with an orange chevron. With the class `dots` on the `ul`, they are marked with an orange ring, and with the class `dashes`, with a short orange dash. A nested list without the class keeps the chevron. In a list with the class `center`, in its nested lists, in an item with the class `center`, and in a list of a shape not aligned to the left, each bullet is drawn beside the centered words of its item. The bullet is then in the line of the words, 54 px before them. An empty item is drawn without its bullet in the show, in the print view, and in the PowerPoint file, while the bullet is drawn as a placeholder in the editor. An item or a paragraph emptied in the editor is saved as `<li></li>` or `<p></p>`, without the line breaks that the browser leaves in it, while an emptied heading or cell keeps its `<br>`. In the editor, choose Paragraphs, Arrows, Dots, Dashes, or Numbers in Format > List. In the section Bullets & Lists of the Text pane, choose the kind of text, i.e. Paragraphs, Bullets, or Numbers, in the first selector, and the bullet, i.e. Arrows, Dots, or Dashes, in the second. Bullets keeps the bullet of a bulleted list and gives Arrows to any other text, and the second selector is dimmed for a text that is not a bulleted list. A choice changes the list of the caret at its level, a paragraph becomes a list of one item, and a numbered list becomes a `ul` without its `start`. A paragraph with a `translate` or a `width` in its `style`, e.g. a text box, gives them to its list, and the items are written without them. A list made paragraphs gives them back to each paragraph. On a text element selected as a whole, the kind is set on its top list and on every nested `ul`. Arrows and Numbers take the class off. Tab and Shift+Tab give the class to the list that they make. Dots and Dashes are not offered in the notes.

A table is set in type of 36 px between two heavy rules in `--text`, as in booktabs, without rules between its rows. When the rows of a table are steps, the closing rule is drawn under the last row shown so far, and each row is drawn in the same place at every step. A cell whose `rowspan` of 2 to 6 ends at the last row shown is drawn with the rule under it too. A cell that spans on into rows not shown yet leaves the rule open in its column, so that the rule does not cut through its words. To take the closing rule off a table, write `--closing-rule: transparent` in a style of the table, e.g. `table { --closing-rule: transparent }` in the style of the deck. In the editor, Show Table Row by Row in the context menu of a cell, or Table Row by Row in the Steps pane, makes each body row a step of its own, written as `<tr class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1">`. To give some rows a step, select them by their numbers and choose Add as Last Step, as for any element. Its header row, a first row of `th`, is drawn in italic in `--subtle` above a thin rule in `--text`. Numbers in a table are set in tabular figures, so that the digits of a column line up. A table is at most as wide as the text column until the widths of its columns are written. In the editor, a table is edited as in Excel. Click a cell to select it, and type to write over its text. Click the selected cell again, double-click a cell, or press F2 or Enter on a selected cell to put the caret in. With the caret in a cell, Enter selects the cell below, and Shift+Enter starts a new line. The arrow keys, Tab, and Shift+Tab move the selection to the next cell, and the arrow keys at the edge of the text of a cell move the caret to the next cell. Drag across cells or Shift-click a cell to select a range, and click a column letter or a row number to select the column or the row. Delete clears the selected cells. Press Esc to go from the caret to the cell, and press Esc again, click Select Table at the corner of the bars, or click the move handle to select the table as a whole. The bars of the column letters and the row numbers are drawn in the tones of the slide, and selected cells are framed in white, as a selected element. Drag a table selected as a whole anywhere on it to move it, with a mouse or a finger, as in Keynote. A click on it selects the cell under the pointer. Its eight grips resize it as a whole, as in PowerPoint and Keynote. A grip at its left or right edge scales the widths of its columns, a grip at its top or bottom edge scales the heights of its rows, and a grip at a corner scales both. Hold Shift to keep its proportions. The size of the type is kept, and a row is not made lower than its words.

In a table, the width of a column is written in px in the `style` of its cell in the first row, e.g. `<th style="width: 320px">`. This width is the width of the content of the cell, without its padding of 12 px at the left and 28 px at the right. In the editor, it is written with a drag of the line between two columns, on the column letters or inside the table, with Distribute Columns Evenly, and with Resize Column to Fit Content. With the last, the width of the widest words of a column is written, rounded up to a whole px. It is written for the columns of the selected cells or of the caret, or for every column of a table selected as a whole. The height of a row is written in px in the `style` of its `tr`, e.g. `<tr style="height: 96px">`, and a row is drawn at least as high as its words. In the editor, it is written with a drag of the line under the row, on the row numbers or inside the table. On a touch screen, the lines between columns and rows are dragged on the letters and numbers only. In the editor, choose Distribute Rows Evenly to give the rows of the selected cells, or every row of the table, the same height. This height is their mean height, or the height of the highest words among them where this is more. Choose Resize Row to Fit Content to take the written heights off the rows of the selected cells, the row of the caret, or every row of a selected table. The command is dimmed where none of these rows has a written height. Both commands are in Format > Table and in the menu of a row bar, and in the Rows section of the Table pane as the buttons Distribute Evenly and Fit to Content, beside the commands of the columns. In Format > Table and in the menu of a cell, Distribute Rows Evenly, Distribute Columns Evenly, Resize Row to Fit Content, and Resize Column to Fit Content are listed in this order. A cell set at the right with Numbers at the Right or Auto-Align is written with `text-align: right` in its `style`, and cells merged with Merge Cells are written with `colspan` and `rowspan`. Rule Below, in the context menu of a cell and in Format > Table, draws a thin rule under the selected cells, as under a header row, e.g. under a merged header cell over two columns. It writes the class `rule-below` on each cell, and it takes the rule off when every selected cell has it. A second line in small gray type under the words of a cell, e.g. the mass ratio under the name of a point, is written as `<span class="small">` in the cell, at 26 px in `--muted`. In the editor, press Shift+Enter in the cell, type the line, select it, and choose Smaller. Bigger takes it back, and a color of Text Color may be set on it as on other words. Boxed text, i.e. words framed by a thin rounded box in their color, e.g. "open problem" in a cell, is written as `<span class="boxed">`. In the editor, select the words and choose Format > Boxed, after Text Color when the box should take a color. A color set on the words alone is written outside the box, so that the box takes it. An image in a cell is written as `<img src="media/name.png">` after the words of the cell, and it is drawn as wide as its column, so that it is resized with the column. In the editor, select a cell, or put the caret in it, and choose Insert > Image. The width of its column is then written, when it was not, so that the image keeps its size. An image in a cell is copied, cut, pasted, and merged with the cell. A copy of a range of cells is written as text with tabs, as from a spreadsheet, and as a table. In the table, each cell is written with its formulas and their delimiters, its bold, italics, and strikethroughs, its words colored with a class `tone-*`, its small and boxed words, and its images. A paste of such a table into the editor writes them back, and a table pasted from another app is written as plain text. With the caret in a cell, press Cmd+V on a Mac and Ctrl+V elsewhere, or choose Edit > Paste or Paste in the menu of the cell, to fill the cells from the cell of the caret.

To move rows or columns of a table in the editor, select them with a click on their bar, then drag the selection by its bar to its new place, which a line shows, as in Keynote. A press on a bar that is not selected still selects it, and Esc cancels a drag. Move Row Up, Move Row Down, Move Column Left, and Move Column Right are in the menu of a bar and in the context menu of a cell. The moved rows and columns are written in their new order in the file and stay selected. The widths of the columns stay on the first row, a header row stays above the body rows, and a row or a column is not moved across a merged cell.

## Lines

A line or an arrow on a slide is an inline `svg` with the class `line`, a child of the `section`, as large as the box of its line and moved to its place with `translate`. In the `svg`, one `line` is drawn from `x1`, `y1` to `x2`, `y2`, in pixels of the box. Its arrowheads are the `marker` of the `svg`, named in `marker-end` for an arrow and in `marker-start` as well for a double arrow. The line is stroked in `--text`, or in the color of one class `tone-muted`, `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, or `tone-red` on the `svg`, and its arrowheads are filled in the same color. It is 3 px wide, or 2, 6, or 8 px with one class `weight-2`, `weight-6`, or `weight-8` on the `svg`, and its arrowheads grow with its width. With the class `dashed`, it is drawn in dashes three widths long with gaps of two widths. An orange arrow from (200, 300) to (520, 300) on the slide is written as follows.

```html
<svg class="line tone-accent" width="320" height="1" style="translate: 200px 300px"><defs><marker id="line-1" orient="auto-start-reverse"><path d="M-4-2.5 1 0-4 2.5z"></path></marker></defs><line x1="0" y1="0" x2="320" y2="0" marker-end="url(#line-1)"></line></svg>
```

Give each marker an id that is not used elsewhere in the deck, and name it in the `url()` of its own `svg`. A curve or an elbow has one `path` in place of the `line`, with the same `marker-start` and `marker-end`, and it is not filled. Its kind is read from the `d` of the path. A curve is written as `M x1 y1 Q cx cy x2 y2`, a quadratic Bézier curve with its control point at (cx, cy). An elbow of horizontal and vertical segments is written as `M x1 y1 H m V y2 H x2` or `M x1 y1 V m H x2 V y2`, with its middle segment at m, or with one bend as `M x1 y1 H x2 V y2` or `M x1 y1 V y2 H x2`. The editor moves a path of another form whole, and it does not rewrite it.

In the editor, draw a line with Insert > Line or Insert > Arrow by a drag from one end to the other, with Shift for steps of 45 degrees, or click to insert a line 320 px long. With Insert > Curved Arrow and Insert > Elbow Arrow, you draw a curve or an elbow in the same way, with a bend that you change afterward. In the menu of the Shape button of the toolbar, the four lines are listed after the shapes. The line is written before the notes of the slide. Move it as any element, drag its ends to change them, and set its color and its arrowheads in Format > Line or in the Line pane. A selected curve or elbow has a filled third grip at its bend, which you drag to move the bend, and Esc puts the bend back during the drag. Hold Shift to keep the bend of a curve on the perpendicular bisector of its chord, for a symmetric arc. The middle segment of an elbow snaps to the line of an end within 8 pixels of the screen, unless you hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere. Change the kind with Straight, Curved, or Elbow, the width with 2 px, 3 px, 6 px, or 8 px, and the dash with Dashed, in Format > Line or in the sections Kind and Weight of the Line pane. At a change of kind, the ends, the arrowheads, the tone, and the marker of the line are kept, also on a turned line. The file is thus written as before after a change to a curve and back to straight. The marker of a copied line is given an id that is not used elsewhere in the deck, also in a slide copied with Duplicate Slide, Magic Move to Copy of Slide, or a paste. Its `url()` is then pointed at the new id. A line can be given a step in the Steps pane, where it is listed as Line.

In the editor, an end of a line that you draw or drag onto an element is attached to it, as with a connector of PowerPoint. An end can be attached to a shape, an image, a figure, a figure box, a table, a formula, a QR code, a video, a tycho viewer, a text moved as a box, or a group as one element. It is not attached to a backdrop, a plate, the footnote, a line, or a text in the flow. While you drag the end, the element under it is outlined, with dots at the middles of its edges. The end is put at the middle of an edge within 16 slide px of it, and otherwise at the pointer. Hold Cmd on a Mac, or Alt elsewhere, to draw or drag an end without an attachment. The element is given a name unused on its slide in `data-anchor`, e.g. `data-anchor="a1"`. The end is written in `data-from` or `data-to` of the `svg` as this name and the place of the end in the box of the element, as two fractions from 0 to 1. Thus `data-to="a1 0 0.5"` is the middle of the left edge. The name of a group is written on each of its members, hence `data-anchor` is a list of names separated by spaces, as `data-group` is. An attached end is moved with each move, resize, and turn of its element, also when the element is moved by your typing in another text, in the same entry of the history as the change. A line moved as a whole is let go at both ends, unless its element is moved with it, and after a delete or a cut of the element the line is kept without an attachment. A copy of a line together with its elements is attached to the copies, and a copy of a line alone is not attached. In the section Connections of the Line pane, the element of each attached end is named, e.g. Start: Rounded Rectangle: Goal, and you press Detach to take the attachment off. A line with an attached end is not turned, as a connection line of Keynote is not. While only such lines are selected, the field Angle, Rotate Left 90°, Rotate Right 90°, and Reset Rotation are dimmed, and the title of the field reads Detach the line to turn it. The three attributes are read only in the editor, like `data-group` and `data-locked`.

## Shapes

A shape is a `div` with the class `shape`, a child of the `section`, placed with `translate` and sized with `width` and `height` in px in its `style`. Its words are in a `p` inside it, centered in its box, or aligned to the left with the class `left` on the `p`. Its words are kept 24 px from its sides and 12 px from its top and bottom, hence a shape with words is at least 48 by 24 px. A shape without words, e.g. a thin brace, has exactly the size of its `style`. An orange rounded rectangle of 480 by 240 px at (200, 300) on the slide is written as follows.

```html
<div class="shape rounded tone-accent" style="width: 480px; height: 240px; translate: 200px 300px"><p>Goal</p></div>
```

Without a geometry class, the shape is a rectangle. The classes `rounded` and `ellipse` give a rounded rectangle and an ellipse, which are drawn with CSS. The classes `triangle`, `arrow` (a block arrow), `arrow-both` (a double block arrow), `brace`, `plus`, and `equals` give the drawn geometries. The theme draws such a geometry from the box into an `svg` child when the deck is started, and again at a change of the size or the classes. This `svg` is not written into the file, and it is drawn in the print view as well. A brace is drawn as the center line of the extensible `}` of TeX, with round hooks at its tips. In a box of about 8 widths in height or more, its curves are drawn at the size that its width gives them, and only its straight stems are lengthened. In a shorter box, its curves are squeezed to half of the height, so that a short brace is the tall one drawn lower. The classes `flip-x` and `flip-y` mirror a drawn geometry, and they do not mirror its words.

The outline is drawn in `--text`, or in the color of one class `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, `tone-red`, or `tone-muted`, and the fill is a tint of the same color, i.e. 20 percent of it over `--bg`. The words keep the color of the text. With `fill-none`, the shape is not filled, with `fill-ground`, it is filled with `--bg`, and with `fill-translucent`, it is a translucent shape of `--bg` at 75 percent opacity, drawn without an outline unless it has `weight-6` or `weight-8`. The outline is 3 px wide, or 6 or 8 px with `weight-6` or `weight-8`, and it is left out with `no-outline`. The corners of a rounded rectangle are 24 px, or 12 px with `round-s` and 48 px with `round-l`.

In the editor, choose a shape in Insert > Shape or in the menu of the Shape button of the toolbar, whose main part draws a rounded rectangle, and drag on the slide for its box, with Shift for a square. The geometry of the shape is drawn in the box from the first move of the drag, with its size beside it. A click puts a shape of 240 by 240 px, and Esc leaves without a shape. The shape is written before the notes of the slide. It is selected, moved, resized by eight grips with a free aspect, turned, copied, aligned, ordered, grouped, and locked as a figure, and Shift on a corner grip keeps its aspect. Type with one shape selected to start its words, or press Return or double-click an empty shape to put the caret into it, and its `p` is written only once it has text. Drag the words of a selected shape to move the shape, or to drag a copy with Option on a Mac or Ctrl elsewhere, and click them without a move to put the caret at the point. Double-click a word of a shape to select the word, also when the shape is not selected, and drag over the words of a locked shape to select them. With a shape selected, use the Text pane to format all of its words, also as a list with nested items. The text colors are the exception. A color chosen in the Text pane or in Format > Text Color while a shape is selected is written as the tone of the shape, as with Color in the Shape pane, and the color of its words is left as it was. Subtle is not a tone of a shape, and its swatch is disabled then. To color the words, select them or put the caret into them first. With shapes and text elements selected together, the color is written on the shapes as their tone and on the words of the text elements. Choose Align Left to write the class `left` on its `p`, and Center to take it off, and the alignment control is checked as the words are drawn. In the Shape pane, change the geometry in the pop-up Shape, which keeps the words, the box, and the other classes. Set Color, Fill (Tint, None, Background, or Translucent shape), Outline (None, 3 px, 6 px, or 8 px), and Corners (Small, Medium, or Large) in the same pane or in Format > Shape, for every selected shape. Corners is offered on a rounded rectangle, and an Outline of 3 px is disabled on a translucent shape. Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical toggle `flip-x` and `flip-y` on a drawn geometry. A shape without a fill is hit only on its words and on its outline, up to 6 px on either side of it, hence a marquee can be started inside it. In the Object List, a shape is named by its geometry and its words, e.g. Rounded Rectangle: Goal, and in the Steps pane it is listed as Shape. A change of the layout and the resets keep its place and size, while Reset Rotation still takes its turn off.

## Backdrops

Choose a built-in backdrop with the attribute `data-backdrop` of a `backdrop`. `tubes` and `wideRibbon` are computed in the browser by the code in `src/backdrops.ts`, `normalForm` by the code in `src/normal-form.ts`, and `poincare`, `tori`, and `saddle` are drawn from the files of `theme/backdrops/`. The demo deck shows each of them but `saddle` and `normalForm`.

| Name | Backdrop |
|---|---|
| `tubes` | The stable and unstable tubes of orbits at L1 and L2 |
| `wideRibbon` | The tubes of an L1 orbit toward the Earth, after Amlan Sinha's figures in the group deck and in [Phantom Pathways](https://amlan-sinha.github.io/art-of-science/art-1/) |
| `poincare` | A Poincaré section of the center manifold of a libration point, described below |
| `tori` | Ten invariant tori near the Earth–Moon L5 in the bicircular problem, described below |
| `saddle` | The flow near a collinear libration point in the normal form, described below |
| `normalForm` | The orbits near a saddle, carried into the coordinates of its normal form and back, described below |
| a file in `data-trajectories` | The polylines of a file next to the deck, described below |

A backdrop is drawn under the other elements of its slide, e.g. its headings, text, figures, and QR codes, wherever it is written in the slide. On a closing slide, it is drawn over the QR code and under the title text. The lockup of a title slide is drawn under the backdrop, as on a closing slide.

Set the window of the backdrop with `data-cx` and `data-cy`, the point at the center of the box, and `data-half`, half of the height of the box, all in nondimensional units. The backdrop of a title, section, or closing slide is traced when its slide is shown, and the backdrop of another slide is drawn complete. Add `data-live` to trace it as well. To make a backdrop a step of its slide, e.g. to show the words of a title slide before the logo is traced, give it `class="fragment"` and a `data-fragment-index`. The backdrop is then hidden until its step, traced from this step on, and blanked again when the step is undone. To keep a backdrop on the slide and start its trace at a step instead, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-backdrop-play`, as `data-plot-play` starts a plot. Each backdrop of the slide is then kept at the start of its trace until this fragment is shown, i.e. its labels are drawn and its trajectories are not, and it is traced from this step on, also outside a title, section, or closing slide. When the step is undone, the backdrop is set back to the start of its trace. On a slide opened at a later step, e.g. from the slide after it, the backdrop is traced when the slide is shown. Under reduced motion and in the PDF, the backdrop is at the start of its trace before the step and complete from the step on. In PowerPoint, it is recorded from the slide of the step. In the editor, the backdrop is drawn complete. While the pointer is on a backdrop, the trajectories within 20 slide pixels of it are tinted orange.

Label a point of a backdrop with a `label` span inside the backdrop. Its `data-at` is `L1`, `L2`, `moon`, or `earth` on `tubes` or `wideRibbon`, or two numbers `x y`, the point in the units of the window, on any backdrop but `poincare` and `tori`. Shift the label from its point by `data-dx` and `data-dy` pixels. The theme typesets the text of a label with MathJax as one formula, in the font of the formulas of the slide: its words are set in `\text`, and its math is written between `\(` and `\)`. A label written with markup, e.g. `L<sub>1</sub>` in an older talk, is left as written, and so is a label in a deck without MathJax. Write a libration point as `\(\mathcal{L}_{1}\)`, i.e. a calligraphic L with its index, and write it in the same way in a heading, a caption, and the notes.
`poincare` is a Poincaré section of the center manifold of a libration point. Choose the system with `data-system`, which is `earth-moon-l2` by default, `sun-earth-l1`, `bicircular-l1-horizontal`, or `bicircular-l1-vertical`. The first two are collinear points of the circular restricted three-body problem, after Jorba and Masdemont (1999), in the section $q_3 = 0$ with $p_3 > 0$ and the coordinates $(q_2, p_2)$ of the center manifold. The last two are the periodic orbit that replaces the Earth–Moon $\mathcal{L}_{1}$ in the bicircular problem, after Jorba et al. (2020). `bicircular-l1-horizontal` is the section $p_2 = 0$ in the coordinates $(q_1, p_1)$, and `bicircular-l1-vertical` is the section $q_1 = 0$ in the axes $(p_2, -q_2)$. Each orbit is drawn as dots on its closed curve, with enough dots to close the curve, and it is colored by its rotation number, in plasma for the restricted problem and in Blues_r for the bicircular problem. The orbit at the center is marked with a disk and the two halo orbits with diamonds, all in `--data-7` for the restricted problem and in `--data-5` for the bicircular problem. In `bicircular-l1-vertical`, the orbit at the center turns hyperbolic where the halo orbits are born from it, and its disk then turns into a cross. When the backdrop is traced, the energy is raised from 0.2 to its last value in the seconds of `data-duration`, 13 by default, and the halo orbits are faded in at the energy of their bifurcation. The last energy is 1 for the restricted problem and 0.9 for the bicircular problem. The section is then left still.

`tori` is ten unstable invariant tori near the Earth–Moon $\mathcal{L}_{5}$ in the bicircular problem, after Simó et al. (1995), with the vertical amplitude α from 0.02 to 0.20 in steps of 0.02. Choose the plane with `data-plane`, which is `xy` by default, or `xz`, in which the x axis is drawn 5.5 times wider. Choose the tori with `data-tori`, a list of their α separated by spaces, e.g. `data-tori="0.02 0.10 0.18"`, or `none` for the periodic orbit alone. Without the attribute, all ten are drawn. Each torus is drawn as trains of dots that run along it, 0.44 periods of the Sun long, in the color of its α in magma from 0.18 to 0.88. A lone torus is drawn with twice the trains at twice the length. The periodic orbit at the center of the tori and a cross at $\mathcal{L}_{5}$ are drawn faint. When the backdrop is traced, the periodic orbit is traced first, and the tori are then started one after another. The last of ten is started after the seconds of `data-duration`, 11.1 by default, and the trains run without end while the slide is shown. A backdrop that is drawn complete is the still of 12 s after the last start.

`saddle` is the flow near the Earth–Moon $\mathcal{L}_{1}$ in the coordinates of the quadratic part of its normal form, i.e. the tubes of `tubes` made straight. The periodic orbit is drawn as a ring, and its stable and unstable manifolds as two cylinders that cross at the ring, the stable one in violet and the unstable one in orange. Three transit orbits pass inside the cylinders and two non-transit orbits outside them, in the color of the text. Each orbit fades out with its distance from the ring. When the backdrop is traced, the ring is traced first, then the stable manifold toward the ring, the unstable manifold away from it, and the passing orbits one after another. The clock of the file is in seconds, hence the trace takes 10.1 s without `data-duration`. The trajectories are read from `theme/backdrops/saddle.js`, which is written by `demo/saddle.py`. The backdrop is therefore drawn, fitted, labeled, and tinted under the pointer as a backdrop of a file in `data-trajectories`, and the coordinates and the window are described in the `readme` of the file. The `data-trajectories` attributes that choose and color the trajectories are not read on `saddle`.

`normalForm` is the flow near a saddle of the plane, from the families of the normal-forms talk of the group, where `poincare` and `tori` are from as well. Sixteen orbits are drawn in each of the four sectors of the saddle, in viridis from its upper end near the saddle to its lower end far from it, over a faint square grid in the color of the text. The stable manifold is drawn in `--violet` and the unstable one in `--accent`, each with a glow, and every line is faded out toward the edge of the box. In each mode, the ends of viridis are moved toward each other until each has a contrast of 3:1 against `--bg`. The orbits are drawn in standard coordinates. They are carried into the coordinates of the normal form, in which the orbits are hyperbolas and the manifolds are straight, by the flow of a generating function over a time of 1. This generating function is a polynomial of degree 3 to 6 damped by a Gaussian, chosen for the look and not taken from a model. The grid is carried the other way, hence it is square in standard coordinates. It is traced or drawn complete by the rule of the other backdrops, as `poincare` and `tori` are. When the backdrop is traced, the orbits are traced from the saddle out in 3.4 s, together with the stable manifold toward the saddle and then the unstable one away from it. The backdrop is then held for 1.4 s, carried into the coordinates of the normal form in 4.5 s, held there for 3.5 s, and carried back in 4.5 s, over and over while the slide is shown. In PowerPoint, the trace and one change there and back are recorded, i.e. 17.3 s. Under reduced motion, in the editor, in the PDF, and in the tile of the gallery, the backdrop is drawn complete, i.e. traced in standard coordinates. The saddle is at the origin of the window. Without `data-half`, the window is fitted to the box from $-0.8$ to $0.8$ across and from $-1$ to $1$ up, without a margin. The fade is started at 72 percent of the way from the saddle to an ellipse around it, whose half-axes are half of the sides of the box, but at most 1 unit. Labels, `data-duration`, `data-stagger`, `data-loop`, and `data-heads` are not read on `normalForm`.

`poincare` and `tori` are traced or drawn complete by the rule of the other backdrops, hence add `data-live` to trace one outside a title, section, or closing slide. Without `data-half`, the window is fitted to the box with a margin, also after a resize of the box. The units of the window are those of $(q_2, p_2)$ for `poincare`, and 0.0001 of the Earth–Moon distance from $\mathcal{L}_{5}$ for `tori`. The data of these two backdrops is in `theme/backdrops/`, in one file `poincare-<system>.js` per system and in `tori.js`. They were computed with DyLAN, and their keys are described in the `readme` of each file. A file is loaded by the theme once, and only in a deck with a backdrop that draws it. With a missing file, the backdrop is left empty, and one warning is printed in the console. Labels, `data-stagger`, `data-loop`, and `data-heads` are not read by these two backdrops, and their dots are not tinted under the pointer.

A backdrop can also be drawn from a file of polylines, e.g. the members of families computed elsewhere. Name the file in `data-trajectories` in place of `data-backdrop`, with its path relative to the deck. The demo deck draws `demo/broucke-sample.js`, which is also the example to start from. The file holds members of Broucke's families 7 and 11 of the elliptic restricted three-body problem in the frames `pulsating`, `rotating`, `inertial`, and `inertial-m2`, and it is written by `demo/broucke.py`. Its `readme` describes each frame and the members of each family.

The file is a JSON object with a list `families` of trajectories and an optional list `bodies`. Each trajectory is a JSON object with a list `points` of `[x, y]` pairs, and optionally with a list `t` or `theta` of the time or the true anomaly at each point. The trajectory of a trajectory is traced on this clock, or at a uniform speed along its length without one. A trajectory with `"craft": true` is the path of a spacecraft, whose head is drawn without `data-heads`. The other keys of a trajectory are free, e.g. `family`, `branch`, `frame`, and `e` in the sample, and you select and color the trajectories by them. Eight keys set the look of a trajectory instead. The key `tone` is `orange`, `violet`, or `text`, for a color of the theme. The key `color` is a CSS color, or a pair of them for the dark and the light mode, drawn in place of the color of the tone. The key `alpha` is the opacity of the trajectory from 0 to 1. The key `weight` is its width in hairlines, and `head` is the radius of its head in hairlines, with 0 for none. The key `start` is the time on the clock at which the trajectory is begun, and with `glow` true it is drawn with a soft glow of its color. The key `fade` is the seconds of the clock of the trajectory, from its first point, after which its opacity falls to zero at its last point, as the square of the time left. Each entry of `bodies` has the positions of the primary and the secondary as `m1` and `m2` in a JSON object per frame, and the mass ratio as `mu`, e.g. `{"family": 7, "mu": 0.012155, "pulsating": {"m1": [-0.01215, 0], "m2": [0.98784, 0]}}`. A body entry is matched to every trajectory with the same values for the numbers and strings of the entry. The bodies are drawn as dots only when all the drawn trajectories put them at the same positions, within 10⁻⁴ in the units of the file, hence none are drawn in the inertial frame, where they move, or for trajectories of two mass ratios.

Where the points of a trajectory are too far apart for its turns, e.g. at a close pass of a body, the trajectory is drawn as a smooth curve through the points. The curve is the centripetal Catmull–Rom spline, and it is drawn only where it departs from the straight segment between two points by more than 0.1 px.

A deck opened with a double click cannot read a JSON file, since the browser refuses the request. Wrap the JSON in `Beeson.trajectories(` and `);`, and name the `.js` file in `data-trajectories`. A plain JSON file can be named only in a deck served from a web server. With a missing or malformed file, the backdrop is left empty, and one warning is printed in the console.

- With `data-trajectories-select`, you choose the trajectories to draw, e.g. `family=7,frame=pulsating`. A term is `key=value`, `key=a|b` for either value, or `key<number`, `key<=number`, `key>number`, or `key>=number`. The terms of a group are separated by commas, and a trajectory is drawn when it passes every term of one of the groups, which are separated by semicolons.
- With `data-trajectories-color`, you give each value of a key, e.g. `family`, a pair of colors of the palette, in the order of the first trajectory of each value in the file. The palette is the eight colors `--trajectory-1` to `--trajectory-8` in `theme/beeson.css`, defined for each mode, and the pairs are `--trajectory-1` with `--trajectory-2`, `--trajectory-3` with `--trajectory-4`, and so on.
- With `data-trajectories-ramp`, you color each trajectory from the first color of its pair at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn trajectories of the pair, to the second color at the largest.
- With `data-trajectories-shade`, you draw each trajectory in a shade of the first color of its pair instead. The shade is lighter at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn trajectories of the pair, and darker at the largest. It is used in place of `data-trajectories-ramp` when both are given.
- With `data-trajectories-colormap`, you color the trajectories from a colormap of matplotlib in place of the palette, i.e. `viridis`, `plasma`, `magma`, or `Blues_r`. A group of trajectories is a value of the key of `data-trajectories-color`, or all the trajectories without this attribute. In each group, the trajectory with the smallest value of the key of `data-trajectories-ramp` is drawn in the color of the lower end of the colormap, and the trajectory with the largest value in the color of the upper end. The ends are 0 and 0.85 for viridis, plasma, and magma, and 0 and 0.683 for Blues_r, short of the yellow or white end, and you can give other ends after the name, e.g. `viridis 0.1 0.7`. In each mode, an end whose color has a contrast below 3:1 against `--bg` is moved toward the other end in steps of 0.01 until it reaches 3:1. Thus viridis is drawn from 0.29 to 0.85 in the dark mode, and from 0 to 0.57 in the light mode. A trajectory of a colormap is drawn opaque, hence its color on the slide is the color of a matplotlib figure, and each color between the ends keeps 3:1 against `--bg` as drawn. With a colormap, `data-trajectories-shade` is ignored after a warning in the console. With a name other than these four, or with malformed ends, the trajectories are colored from the palette after a warning.
- With `data-stagger`, you start the trajectories one after another, in the order of the file, with the given number of seconds between two starts, e.g. `0.4`. It works on `tubes` and `wideRibbon` too.
- With `data-loop`, you fly an orange head with a short trail around a trajectory, over and over at 9 s per round, while the slide is shown. On `tubes` or `wideRibbon`, the head is flown on the first trajectory, and on a backdrop of a file, a head is flown on each trajectory.
- With `data-heads`, you draw a moving head on every trajectory, at the size `full` or `small`, or on none with `none`. Without it, a head is drawn only on the path of a spacecraft, and on a trajectory of a file with a `head` of its own. Such a path is a trajectory of a file with `"craft": true`.
- With `data-duration`, you set the seconds in which the whole backdrop is traced, from the first trajectory to the last, 12 by default. The clock of a file runs in these seconds, hence with `data-duration` equal to the last time on the clock, `start` and `t` are in seconds.

Without `data-half`, the window of a backdrop of a file is fitted to its trajectories with the fill of `tubes` on the closing slide. In the window, 93 percent of the trajectories is held, measured along each trajectory with every trajectory counted alike. The trajectories are also kept 40 px clear of each side of the box that is inside the slide.

The new label reads Label, selected for you to type over. The section is not shown for Poincaré Sections, Invariant Tori, and Normal Form.
- In Motion, set Live, Duration, and Start, and press Preview to trace the backdrop once and leave it complete. In the Start menu, choose With the Slide, or a step of the slide, e.g. Step 2, named as in the step menu of the Steps pane. A step writes `data-backdrop-play` on its first element and takes it off the other elements of the slide, and With the Slide takes it off every element. The menu is disabled on a slide without steps. The trace is ended when the backdrop is deselected, at an edit, at a change of slide, and at Esc, which keeps the selection. The frame and the grips of the backdrop are not drawn during the trace, or during the seconds of one trace for a loop, and a press on the slide draws them again.

Click a label to select it, and click it again to edit its text, which is shown as you wrote it, e.g. `\(\mathcal{L}_{1}\)`. Return and Esc end the edit, and the label is typeset again. Drag a selected label to shift it, which writes its `translate`, and press Delete to take it off. Shapes, lines, and text boxes are drawn and dragged over a backdrop as anywhere else on the slide.

Edit Window, Fit Window, and Preview are also in Format > Backdrop. In the section Backdrop of the Slide pane, the backdrop of the slide is named next to the button Select, and the button Backdrop… is shown on a slide without a backdrop. It opens the gallery, as Insert > Backdrop… does.

The swings of the Earth leg that the logo does not show are cut out of the path. The window puts the transfer in the right part of the slide, beside the title text. In a talk made from the template, this backdrop is kept with the tag of its title slide, and the file is distributed only with the trademarks, see `brand/NOTICE.md`.

## Surfaces

A `surface` is the graph of a function $z = f(x, y)$ over a rectangle, drawn in 3D with three.js under a camera that turns slowly and can be turned with a drag. Set the function with `data-function`, and its parameters with further data attributes. Each function is defined in `src/surfaces.ts` with its states, i.e. what is drawn at each step of the slide. Give a fragment of the same slide the attribute `data-surface-state`, and the surface is moved to this state when the fragment is shown, and back when it is hidden. At a state, several graphs may be shown, i.e. the graphs of the parts of the function stacked at different heights, each in its own color ramp. A change of state is animated. The heights of each graph are morphed, a graph that is added is split from the first graph or grown from a flat one, and a graph that is removed is merged into the first graph or flattened onto it. The points are grown and faded in place on the first graph, drawn over the surface, and dimmed where the surface is in front of them, and a cutting plane is slid to its height. Label a point with a `label` span whose `data-at` is the name of the point. Its text is typeset as that of a label of a backdrop. To pick out some of the points, give the fragment that sets the state the attribute `data-surface-points` with their names, e.g. `L1 L2 L3`. The other points and their labels are then dimmed to 18 percent. The points named in `data-surface-accent` are filled in orange, those named in `data-surface-violet` in violet, and their labels keep the color of the text. A fragment that changes only these attributes and repeats the state of the step before is drawn as a fade of the points with a time constant of 0.15 s, and the graphs are left as they are. The demo slide "Color the terms and the points" is an example of each attribute. In a deck with a surface, load `theme/vendor/three/three.js` before `theme/beeson.js`.

| Function | Attributes | States |
|---|---|---|
| `effective-potential` | `data-mu`, the mass ratio $\mu$ of the circular restricted three-body problem, 0.05 by default, and `data-C` on the fragment of the cut, the Jacobi constant $C$, that of L1 by default | `zero`, the plane $z = 0$ at the start of the slide, `gravitational` with the gravitational wells in Blues, `centrifugal` with the centrifugal dome in Reds above the wells, `effective` with the two merged in Magma_r (Magma reversed) without its darkest 30 %, `extrema` with the points L1 to L5, and `cut` with the plane at $-C/2$ and the zero-velocity curve |

The camera is set with `data-azimuth` and `data-elevation` in degrees, `data-turn` in seconds per turn (0 for a still camera), and `data-zoom`, and the heights are scaled with `data-zscale`. A surface whose data attributes other than `data-surface-state` are those of a surface on the slide before is shown under the camera of that surface, and its turn is continued across the change of slide. With `?still`, a state is drawn at once and the camera is not turned. In a PDF, each step is printed with a still of its state, and in PowerPoint it is recorded, as described under PowerPoint. The surfaces and the strip bundles of a deck are drawn by one renderer of WebGL. When the browser loses its context, e.g. at a reset of the GPU, and restores it, every surface and strip bundle is drawn again.

A strip bundle is a `surface` with `data-bundle` in place of `data-function`, drawn from the JSON script with this id. In the script, `orbit` is a list of points `[x, y, z]`, closed from the last point to the first, and `strips` is a list of strips. A strip has an `id`, a `tone`, a `half_width`, a unit vector in `directions` for each point of the orbit, and the indices of the points of its ribs in `ribs`. Name the strips shown at a step in its `data-surface-state`, separated by spaces. A strip that is added is grown out of the orbit over 3.6 s, first as its ribs one after another and then as its sheet, and a strip that is removed is shrunk back over 1.4 s. A `libration_point` `[x, y, z]` in the script is drawn as a dot, labeled by a `label` whose `data-at` is an L and a digit, e.g. `L1`. Each arrow of the list `arrows` has an `id` and a point `toward`, and a compass at the upper left of the bundle is drawn with a needle toward each. A `label` whose `data-at` is the id of an arrow is placed past the head of its needle. A `label` whose `data-at` is the id of a strip is placed at the outer end of its rib `data-rib`, counted from 0, on the side `data-side`, 1 or -1, and it is faded in with the sheet. A script with `times`, the fractions of the period at the points of the orbit, increasing from 0, has a moving point. At the state `start`, the point is drawn in an empty ring at the first point of the orbit, at `nudge` an arrow along the orbit is drawn from it, and at `flow` it is moved around the orbit once every 6 s. The ring is filled at the end of the first period, and the elements of the slide with `data-surface-lap` are faded in with it. Under reduced motion, with `?still`, and in a PDF, a step at `flow` shows the point at the start, in a filled ring. A `label` whose `data-at` is `start` is placed at the start, and one whose `data-at` is `nudge` past the tip of the arrow, each moved by `data-dx` and `data-dy`. The text of each label is typeset as that of a label of a backdrop. The camera of a bundle is set and continued across slides as that of a surface, and its heights are not scaled. In PowerPoint, a bundle is not recorded, and each step is shown with its still, as in the PDF. In the editor, a bundle is moved as a figure, turned with a drag, and given a camera in the Surface pane, and its script is written only in a text editor. The test deck `test/pages/bundle.html` is an example to start from.

## Figures

For a figure made with matplotlib, use the styles `beeson-dark.mplstyle` and `beeson-light.mplstyle` in `styles/`. The palette, the typeface, and a transparent background are set in these styles. Export one file per mode and give the images the classes `for-dark` and `for-light`, so that the right one is shown in each mode. Give a white raster figure the class `white-figure`, and it is framed. In a `plate` or a `figure-box`, the white frame is cut to the size of the image, which is scaled down to fit and is not enlarged. Export such a figure at the aspect of its box and at least at its pixel size, e.g. 1680 by 630 px for the plate of a figure slide. Give a black-on-white line plot the class `invert`, and it is inverted in dark mode.

A PDF figure is a page of a PDF shown as an image, e.g. a plot exported from matplotlib or a diagram compiled with LaTeX. In the editor, insert or drop a `.pdf` file, or choose one with Replace…, and the PDF is written into `media/` with a render of its first page for each mode. A render is a PNG of the page, fitted into 3840 by 2160 px. In the render of a mode, black is drawn in the color of the text, `--text` of `theme/beeson.css`, and white in the color of the slide, `--bg`. In the dark mode the lightness is inverted first and the hue is kept, as with the class `invert`. Hence a PDF drawn in black on white is shown in both modes without a second export. The markup is a pair of images, one for each mode, with the path of the PDF in `data-pdf`. The page is written in `data-page`, which is 1 when it is left out. An example is `<img class="for-dark" src="figures/plot-dark.png" data-pdf="figures/plot.pdf" alt="A plot">` and the same image with `for-light` and `figures/plot-light.png`. A PDF figure without `for-dark` and `for-light` is one render in the colors of the PDF, for a figure whose colors are kept, e.g. a photograph. In the PDF section of the Image pane, type another page into Page, or clear Match the Mode for the colors of the PDF, and the PDF is rendered again. In a deck opened from a file, the PDF is asked for once in the file chooser for this, since the page cannot read the files next to it. To write a PDF figure by hand, write its images with the paths of their renders and run `npm run tex`. Each render that is missing or stale is then written with the pdf.js of `theme/vendor/` in Chrome. A render keeps the hash of its PDF, its page, its mode, and the tones of its mode, and `npm run check` fails on a render that is missing or stale, without Chrome. A deck shows the renders as images and does not load pdf.js.

An image, a video, a QR code, or a tycho viewer can be given a width in its `style`. An image or a video with a width is drawn at this width, even past the box of its figure box or plate, and it keeps the aspect of its file. With a height in px as well, it is stretched to both. A tycho viewer with a width and a height in px is drawn in this box, even past the box of its figure box or plate, and its scene is laid out into it, and a figure box around such a viewer takes its size. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected image, and on both images of a figure with an image for each mode. A drag of a grip of a tycho viewer writes its width and its height, since a viewer has no aspect of its own, and a corner keeps the aspect of its box while Constrain Proportions is on. An image may be drawn inside a larger box with `object-fit: contain`. For such an image, a Width or a Height typed into the Arrange pane scales the box at its ratio. The size is written as `width`, `height`, and `object-fit: contain`, which Reset Size takes off again. The old value typed back right after a typed Width or Height gives back the bytes from before. The height is written as well when the box Constrain Proportions of the Arrange pane is off, and you turn the box over for one drag by holding Shift on a corner. On a figure with a caption, a Height typed into the Arrange pane is kept as typed wherever a width of whole pixels gives this height, at any wrap of the caption. Otherwise the nearest height that a width gives is taken, at the smaller width where two widths are equally near. A width under the one at which the figure is lowest is not taken, since under it the caption grows by more lines than the image loses in height. Crop an image with Edit Mask. The crop is written as `object-fit: cover` and `object-position` where the image fills its box, and else as `scale`, a `clip-path` of the form `inset(N%)`, and `object-position`. Give an `img` the class `oval` to clip it to the ellipse of its box. The crop of an oval image is written as `inset(N% round 50%)`, which clips it to the ellipse of the cropped box. In the editor, turn on Oval in Format > Image or in the Image pane, which writes the class on both images of a pair. On an image drawn smaller than its box, e.g. one without a width in a figure box, the width of the drawn image is written on both images as well. The figure is then moved with `translate`, hence the image stays in place and is clipped to its ellipse. A flip with Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical in the Arrange menu is written as a negative `scale`, e.g. `scale: -1 1`.

Math is typeset in SVG with MathJax 4 and its TeX font, i.e. Computer Modern, and the text is set in Latin Modern Roman, all vendored. The macros of a deck are defined in its preamble, as described under LaTeX.

To show a video, put it in a `figure-box` or a `plate`, and add `data-autoplay` to play it while its slide is shown. Give every video a `poster`, since the poster is printed in the PDF and `npm run check` fails without one.

To start a video at a step, as a video set to start on a click in PowerPoint, give it `class="fragment play"` and a `data-fragment-index`, e.g. `<video class="for-dark fragment play" data-fragment-index="1" src="clip-dark.mp4" poster="clip-dark.png" muted></video>`, and the same for the clip of the other mode. Its poster is shown with its slide, and the video is played from its start once its step is shown, without `data-autoplay`. A video that is a plain fragment, or inside one such as a `figure-box` with `class="figure-box fragment"`, appears at its step instead, and with `data-autoplay` it is played from its start at this step. When its step is undone, a video is paused and rewound, to its poster when it is still in view. A video shown at a step is not dimmed at the later steps, since it is still played. In the PDF, each step is printed with the poster of each video shown at it. Give a video `muted` unless its sound is wanted, since a browser may refuse to play sound before a key is pressed, and in the speaker view only a muted video is played, as in reveal.js. In the editor, the effect Play is offered in the Steps pane for a video, and it is written as the class `play`, with `data-autoplay` taken off. With the checkbox Start on a click of the Video pane, the video is made the last step of its slide with this effect, or its step is given the effect. While this checkbox is checked, the checkbox Play while the slide is shown is dimmed and off, and once it is cleared, the video is left a step with the effect Fade In. In a preview of the steps, such a video is played from its start at its step, and it is rewound to its poster at the end of the preview. The demo slide "Starting a video on a click" is an example.

The videos shown at one step are started together from their first frame, once each of them can be played, and a video that can be played earlier is held at its first frame for up to 5 s. Thus two clips side by side are in step. To show them side by side, put them in a `plate` with the class `row` on a figure slide, e.g. `<div class="plate row">`, which gives each figure of the plate an equal share of its width, and give each clip `class="fragment play"` with the same `data-fragment-index`.

The videos of one `figure-box` or `plate` are stacked in one place, except in a plate with the class `row`, and a video is laid over the ones before it. A video under a later one that is shown, in the same mode, is hidden once the later one has faded in, and paused, and it is shown and started again from its start when the later step is undone. To show one clip and then another in its place, give the first `class="fragment play"` and a step, e.g. 1, and the second `class="fragment"`, `data-autoplay`, and the next step, e.g. 2. The poster of the first clip is then shown with the slide, the first clip is played at step 1, and the second clip is faded in over it and played at step 2. The test deck `test/pages/video-play.html` has a slide of each of these cases.

To embed a scene of tycho, the Typed Celestial HTML Observer, call `build_embed` of pytycho, the Python package of tycho, and paste the inline `tycho-viewer` element into a slide. The scene and its data are in two inline scripts of this element. The runtime is vendored once in `theme/vendor/tycho/`, copied by hand from `pytycho/_static/tycho.js` in the wheel of pytycho on PyPI. Its version is written in the license banner at the top of `theme/vendor/tycho/tycho.js`. It reads only scenes written by the pytycho of the same version, and it rejects a scene of an older format. Hence, after a newer `theme/` is copied into a talk, run the script of each scene again with the pytycho of this version. The viewer is set to the mode of the deck, the scene is played only while its slide is shown, and the keys used in the viewer are not passed to the deck. A viewer with `data-keep-theme` keeps the theme written in the file through a flip of the mode, for a scene whose marks are drawn for one background. In a PDF, and in PowerPoint with `--still`, the viewer is replaced by a still of the scene at its first frame, placed where the panels of the viewer are, and every step of the slide is printed with the same still. In PowerPoint without this option, the scene is a recording, as described under PowerPoint. In the editor, a thumbnail of the slide, e.g. in the navigator, shows a still of the scene at its current frame, which is drawn again once the scene is loaded, once its frame is changed, and after a drag in it. Pass `kiosk=True` for a viewer without controls. Run `demo/tycho-scene.py` to write the viewer of the demo deck. This script is also the example to start from.

To start a scene at a step, as a video of the style `play`, give its viewer `class="fragment play"` and a `data-fragment-index`, which `build_embed` writes from its keywords, e.g. `build_embed(document, container, kiosk=True, class_="figure-box fragment play", data_fragment_index=2)`, or put the viewer in a fragment of the style `play`, e.g. a `figure-box` with `class="figure-box fragment play"`. The viewer is then shown with the first frame of its scene, paused, until its step, and its scene is played from its start once its step is shown. A kiosk viewer is started by tycho once its scene is loaded, and a viewer of the style `play` is held at its first frame again before its step. When its step is undone, or its slide is left, the viewer is paused at its first frame. The viewers shown at one step are started together, once the scene of each of them is loaded, e.g. two scenes side by side in a `plate` with the class `row`, each with the same `data-fragment-index`. A viewer shown at a step is not dimmed at the later steps, since it is still played. In a PDF, and in PowerPoint with `--still`, the still of its first frame is on every step of its slide. The viewers of a `figure-box` are not stacked in one place, as videos are, hence a second scene shown in the place of a first one is not one of these cases. A viewer without the style `play` is played by tycho while it is in view, and it is resumed from where it stopped when its slide is shown again. In the editor, the effect Play is offered in the Steps pane for a viewer as well, and it is written on the tag of the viewer. The checkbox Start on a click of the Video pane is in the Arrange pane of a selected scene too. Once this checkbox is cleared, the viewer is not left a step, as a video is, since tycho plays a kiosk viewer that is only a step while it is still hidden. The step is taken off the viewer, or off the figure box of the style `play` around it, and the scene is started with its slide again. In a preview of the steps, a viewer of the style `play` is held at its first frame until its step and played from its start at the step. The test deck `test/pages/viewer-play.html` has a slide of each case.

## TikZ figures

A TikZ figure is a figure written in TikZ or pgfplots and compiled with your TeX installation, i.e. TeX Live or MiKTeX with `latex` and `dvisvgm`. Write its source as a `tikzpicture` alone in `figures/name.tex`, as a paper reads it with `\input`, and put it on a slide as `<div class="tikz" data-figure="name">`, e.g. with the class `figure-box` as well. Give the element its box with a `width` and a `height` in px in its `style`. Then run `npm run tex`, which compiles the source once for each mode at the size of the box and writes both SVG into the output `figures/name.js`. While the edit command runs, it compiles the figure by itself, as described below. Commit the output with the talk, since TeX is not needed to show a deck or to edit its other elements.

For a compile, the source is wrapped in a document of the class `article` with `styles/beeson-tikz.sty` in the mode, then `preamble.tex` of the talk, hence the macros of the preamble can be used in a figure. The packages `amsopn` and `amsthm` are loaded before the preamble, hence the preamble can define an operator with `\DeclareMathOperator` and a kind of theorem with `\theoremstyle`. The packages `amsmath`, `amssymb`, and `bm`, whose commands MathJax knows without a package, are loaded after the preamble, hence a command of these packages cannot be defined in the preamble with `\newcommand`. A source is compiled with `latex`, not with LuaLaTeX, hence it cannot load `fontspec`. A path in a source, such as the CSV file of a pgfplots `table`, is read from the folder of the deck, e.g. `figures/data.csv`. Take each color from the names of the style, which are the tones of the theme in each mode: `ink` for the text, `bg`, `subtle`, `muted`, `line`, `accent`, `violet`, `blue`, `red`, and `data1` to `data8`. A paper loads the same style with `\usepackage{beeson-tikz}`, in the light mode.

The text of a figure is typeset at 10 pt and shown at 36 px, the size of a caption, with lines of 2 px and curves of 3 px. A pgfplots axis is given the width and the height of the box, and any other figure is scaled to the box by its coordinates, with its text and its lines kept at their size. A figure is never enlarged as an image. One that does not fit its box is shown smaller as a whole, and `npm run tex` prints the scale. A source can size a part by the box with the lengths `\figurewidth` and `\figureheight`.

A part is a scope or a pgfplots curve with the key `part`, e.g. `\begin{scope}[part=moon]` or `\addplot+[part=x]`, and a label node at the end of a curve belongs to the part of its curve. Make a part a step with an empty child of the figure, `<span class="fragment" data-part="moon" data-fragment-index="2"></span>`. The part is then hidden until this step, in the show and in the PDF and PowerPoint exports. In the editor, every part is shown, and the step is listed in the Steps pane as Part and the name of the part. While a TikZ figure is selected, each of its parts without a step is listed under With the slide as well, as Part and its name. Choose a step in the menu of its row, or New Step at Start or New Step at End, and the span is written with this step.

An output is stale when its hash differs from a hash of its source, the files that TeX read for it, `preamble.tex`, the style, and its box. Every figure is thus stale after a change of the preamble. `npm run tex` compiles each stale or missing figure and keeps the last good output after an error of TeX, which it prints with the file and the line. `npm run check` fails on a figure that is stale, missing, or without a source, and TeX is not needed for this check. A figure without an output is shown on its slide with the message `figure name is not compiled: run npm run tex`, or `figure name is being compiled` while the edit command compiles it.

In the editor, insert a TikZ figure with Insert > TikZ Figure, or with the same items in the list of the Media button: From Data…, From File…, and Blank. With From Data…, you choose a CSV file, which is written into `figures/` under its name in lowercase. When another file of this name is there, it is written under a free name such as `data-2.csv`, and when the same file is there, this file is used. Blank lines before the first line of the file are dropped, and a file is refused when a `{` in its first line is not closed, since TeX would read it as the start of a group. The source is then written from the first line, as a pgfplots axis with the first column as x and a curve for each other column. The columns are taken by their index, and the first line is skipped, hence the source is compiled whatever the names of the columns are, once pgfplots can read the first line. Each curve is a part, in the next tone of `data1` to `data8`, with the name of its column as its label at its end. The first line of such a source of From Data is a comment, `% From Data: …`. With From File…, you choose a `.tex` file, whose text is written as the source. With From Data… and From File…, the source is written into `figures/` under a free name taken from the file. The figure is put on the slide as a QR code is, in a box of 800 by 600 px. With Blank, the Edit Source sheet is opened as New TikZ Figure on a small figure, with the button Insert in place of Apply. Only at Insert is the source written, as `figure.tex` or under a free name such as `figure-2.tex`, and the figure put on the slide in the same way. With Cancel, neither a file nor a figure is left.

To change a source, double-click the figure, or choose Edit Source… in the Format menu or in the TikZ Figure pane. The Edit Source sheet shows `figures/name.tex` with the numbers of its lines. About a second after you stop typing, the source is compiled as a preview, which is drawn on the slide in place of the output. The errors of TeX are listed under the text, each as Line 6 and its message, or as preamble.tex, line 3 and its message for another file, and the number of each line with an error is marked. Click an error to select its line. With Apply, or Cmd+Return on a Mac and Ctrl+Enter elsewhere, the source is written over the text that the sheet opened with. With Cancel, or Esc, the file is left as it was, and the output is drawn again. When someone else changed the file after the sheet opened, e.g. a guest or a text editor, Apply is refused with the message Someone else changed figures/name.tex since this sheet opened. Use their text, or apply yours over it. With Use Their Text, their text is put into the sheet, and otherwise your text is written over theirs at the next Apply.

The TikZ Figure pane of the selected figure shows its file with Edit Source…, and the state of its compile, i.e. Compiling…, Compiled at a time, Not compiled, or the first error. For a source of From Data, its sections Data, Curves, X Axis, Y Axis, and Labels hold the fields of the source. The CSV file is changed there with Replace…, and each curve is given its column, chosen by its name in the first line of the file, or e.g. Column 3 without a name, its kind, its tone, and its label. Curves are added with Add Curve and removed with Delete Curve, down to one. Each axis is given its label, its Min and Max, and Logarithmic, and the labels of the curves are put At the Curves, in a Legend, or nowhere with None. At a change of a field, the source is written at once over the text that the pane read, as at Apply. When someone else changed the source meanwhile, the change is not written, and the fields show their text. Once the source is edited by hand into a text that the fields would not write again in the same way, the fields are dimmed, and Edit Source… is named in a note above them. They are turned on again once the source is in this shape again. For a source of any other shape, the pane shows the file and the state alone.

A TikZ figure is resized with the grips of its frame or with Width and Height in the Arrange pane, which write `width` and `height` in px into its `style`. Constrain Proportions is off for a TikZ figure by default, as for a shape. Until the figure is compiled again, its output is drawn in the new box at its old size, or smaller when it does not fit. The edit command then compiles it at the new box. Without the edit command, run `npm run tex` after a resize.

The edit command, i.e. `npm run edit` or a launcher, as described under The synchronous editor, is needed for the commands of TikZ figures, since a page cannot run TeX. At its start, the edit command compiles each figure of the deck that is stale or missing, in the background and one at a time, and writes its output. It compiles a figure again by itself within about a second after its source, a file that it read such as a data file, or `preamble.tex` has stopped changing on disk, and after a change of its box in `index.html`. Every page then draws the new output without a reload. A preview of an Edit Source sheet is compiled before the stale figures, and nothing is written for it. After an error of TeX, the last good output is kept, and the error is printed in the window of the edit command. The figure is then not compiled again before its source, its box, a file that it read, or the list of the files of `figures/` is changed. A pass of TeX is stopped after 120 s. In place of Saved, the save state reads e.g. Compiling cr3bp.tex during a compile, and 2 figures are not compiled while outputs are stale or missing. After a compile, it reads e.g. Compiled cr3bp.tex at 22:51 until your next edit. Without the edit command, e.g. in a deck opened from a file, the figures are shown as they are committed. At Insert > TikZ Figure or Edit Source…, you are then told in a message that TikZ figures are compiled by the edit command, and how to start it. When `latex` or `dvisvgm` is not found on the host machine, you are told so instead.

In a session, only the host changes a TikZ figure. The edit command runs TeX with shell escape off, as `npm run tex` does. Every change of a figure and every preview asked for by a guest is refused. A guest is told so in a message at Insert > TikZ Figure and Edit Source…, and in the TikZ Figure pane, before the state of the figure. The fields and Edit Source… of the pane are dimmed for a guest. The guest still sees each figure drawn again after a compile, and its state in the save state and in the TikZ Figure pane.

## QR codes

A QR code is a `div` with the class `qr` and its text in `data-qr`, e.g. `<div class="qr" data-qr="https://example.com/slides.pdf"></div>`. The code is drawn into the div as an inline SVG when the deck is started. It is square at the width of the div, which is 320 px by default. On a closing slide, the code is 200 px wide in the lower right corner, 50 px from the right and the bottom of the slide, and the backdrop is drawn over it. Set another width in the `style` of the div, e.g. `style="width: 400px"`. The encoder is part of the theme, hence nothing is loaded for a code.

The group logo, `brand/logo.png`, is drawn in the middle of the code, as a disc of 30 percent of the width of the modules with a light ring of 0.8 module around it. The modules under the logo and its ring are not drawn. The text is thus encoded as UTF-8 at error correction level H, at which about 30 percent of the code can be restored, up to 1273 bytes. The version is the smallest one with room for the text in which the codewords under the logo are at most two thirds of those that can be restored in an error-correction block, so that a third is left for the errors of a camera. An alignment pattern that the logo reaches is left out as a whole. With `class="no-logo"` on the `body`, which also switches off the logo at the top right of the slides, a code is drawn without the logo at level M, at which about 15 percent can be restored, in the smallest version with room for the text, up to 2331 bytes. A text too long for level H is drawn in the same way. Switch the logo off in a deck without the files of `brand/`.

The modules are drawn dark on a light tile in both modes, since an inverted code is not read in many scanner apps. Neighboring modules are joined, and a corner of a module without a dark neighbor is rounded. The finder and alignment patterns are rounded rings with an eye in the violet of the light mode, which is used in both modes for its contrast on the tile. The tile is the off-white of the text in dark mode and the color of the background in light mode, with rounded corners and a quiet zone of four modules. The colors are the custom properties `--qr-ink`, `--qr-eye`, and `--qr-tile` of `theme/beeson.css`, and the image of the logo is `--qr-logo`. The code is drawn the same in the slides, with `?still`, in the PDF, and in PowerPoint. In the editor, it is moved by a drag as a figure, and it can be given a step in the Steps pane. An empty or too long `data-qr` is shown as a message in place of the code, and `npm run check` fails on it. Keep the address short, since a shorter address is drawn with fewer and larger modules, which are read from farther back in the room. Draw a code that must be read from the back of a room wider than the 200 px of the closing slide, since the distance from which a code is read is proportional to its width. After changing the backdrop of a closing slide, read the code with a phone, since a code is not read by any check. The closing slide of the demo deck is an example.

In the editor, a QR code with a `translate` or a width is kept at its drawn place and size at a change between Closing and another layout. Its new `translate`, and its width where the default width of the new layout differs, are written in the same entry of the history as the change. A code without a `translate` and without a width takes the place and the size of the new layout. On a closing slide, the words of the title text are drawn in front of the code. The code is thus moved past all of them at once with Bring to Front and Send to Back, and Bring Forward and Send Backward are dimmed for it. On a title slide, a code is in the flow, and Insert > QR Code puts it at the upper left corner of the slide. There, its layer is written as a `z-index`.

## Stepped math

A display is revealed in four steps: the lead-in sentence, the display, the sentence in which it is explained, and a step in which a symbol of the display is colored. Wrap the symbol in the TeX in `\class{name}{...}`, and give a later fragment the attribute `data-highlight="name"`. Once this fragment is shown, the symbols of the class are drawn in orange, or in violet, blue, or red with `data-tone` set to `violet`, `blue`, or `red`. Any element of the slide with the class `name` is colored as well, e.g. a `span` around the words that name the symbol. To take the color off a class at a later step, give that fragment `data-unhighlight="name"`, and the class is drawn in the color of the text until a step after it names the class again in `data-highlight`. Use orange for motion and violet for structure, as in the rest of the theme, and blue and red for the terms drawn in Blues and Reds on a surface, and separate several classes with spaces.

In the editor, select a symbol in the TeX of the Formula pane, choose its tone and a step in the pop-up under Color at a Step, and click Color Symbol. To color words, select them, or select their text elements on the slide, choose a step in the pop-up Step of the Text pane, and click the swatch of orange, violet, blue, or red. Format > Text Color > Color at a Step… opens the same pop-up. The words are wrapped in a `span` of a new class named after them, e.g. `hl-equation-boxes`, and words already in such a span keep its class. The pop-up offers the steps after the step of the formula or of the words, and, after a rule, the elements shown with the slide that can be made its new last step. The class is written into `data-highlight` of an element of the chosen step, with `data-tone`, and taken out of the fragment that colored it before. A step that colors another class in another tone is refused, since a step has one color. Each colored symbol or word is listed under the pop-up with its tone and its step, and its button takes the color off, as does Format > Text Color > Remove Color at Step. The class is then taken off the span, a span without another class is unwrapped, and the class is taken out of `data-highlight` once no element of the slide has it. Each color and each removal is one entry of the history.

An earlier fragment is dimmed to 45 percent once a later one is shown, and every fragment is restored at the step added after the last one. Give a fragment without an index the attribute `data-final`, e.g. a QR code, to show it at this step in place of the added one, i.e. once every fragment is restored. Give the display the attribute `data-keep` to hold it at full opacity through the next step, or `data-keep="4"` to hold it through the step of index 4. Give a fragment `data-focus` to hold every fragment of the slide at full opacity from its step on, e.g. an empty `span` after the last element of a comparison, which is then read as a whole. A display is also held at full opacity while a class in it is colored at the current step. The demo slide "Stepping through the equations" is an example of every step.

## LaTeX

Write the LaTeX definitions of a talk into `preamble.tex` next to `index.html`, and its papers into `references.bib`. Both are read into `tex.js` next to the deck by `npm run tex`, at a save in the editor, or by the edit command of a session, and the theme reads only `tex.js`. A talk is thus shown without a toolchain, and nothing is fetched at show time. A line of the preamble or an entry of the bibliography that is not read is printed by `npm run tex` with its reason, and it is listed in the Preamble sheet or the Bibliography sheet of the editor as well. In a session, it is printed in the window of the edit command instead, at its start and each time the list of such lines changes. In an older talk, the macros of `theme/macros.js` are moved into a new `preamble.tex` by `npm run tex`, and `theme/macros.js` is deleted.

In the preamble, `\newcommand`, `\renewcommand`, `\providecommand`, `\DeclareRobustCommand`, `\def`, `\let`, and `\DeclareMathOperator` are read as macros, and `\newenvironment` and `\renewenvironment` as environments. A package of `\usepackage` is read as the extension of MathJax with its commands, e.g. `mathtools`, and a package or a class whose `.sty` or `.cls` file is next to the preamble is read from this file. A file of `\input` or `\include` next to the preamble is read as well.

A theorem is a paragraph with the class `theorem`, its kind in `data-kind`, and an optional name in `data-name`, e.g. `<p class="theorem" data-kind="lemma" data-name="stable manifold">`. Its head, e.g. Lemma 2 (stable manifold), is drawn by the theme in the look of amsthm. In the style `plain`, the head is bold and the words are italic. In the style `definition`, the head is bold and the words are upright, and in the style `remark`, the head is italic. A proof is closed with a square, and its name replaces the word Proof. The kinds theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary, definition, example, remark, and proof are known in every deck without a number. A kind defined with `\newtheorem` in the preamble is numbered through the deck in the order of its slides, in the style of the `\theoremstyle` before it. A kind defined with `\newtheorem*` is not numbered, and a kind with the counter of another kind in brackets, e.g. `\newtheorem{lemma}[theorem]{Lemma}`, shares its numbers. A number within a section is not read, since a deck does not have sections. A theorem on a hidden slide is not numbered. In the editor, choose a kind in Insert > Theorem, or press Ctrl+Cmd+T on a Mac and Alt+Shift+T elsewhere for a theorem of the kind theorem, and change the kind and the name in the Theorem pane of the Format inspector.

A citation is an empty `cite` after the cited words, with the key of an entry of `references.bib` in `data-key`, e.g. `<cite data-key="koon2000"></cite>`, or the keys of several papers separated by commas. On each slide, the papers are numbered from 1 in the order of their first citation, and a paper cited twice keeps its number. The references of the slide are drawn in the line of a footnote, above the footnote of the slide, in a short form such as Koon et al., Chaos 10 (2000). A reference is shown with the step of its first citation, and a reference with a DOI is a link to its paper. Only the entries that the deck cites are written into `tex.js`. In the editor, cite a paper as described under The editor.

The check fails on a macro that is not defined, a citation of a key that is not in `tex.js`, and a theorem of a kind that is not defined. It also fails when `tex.js` is not the one written from `preamble.tex` and `references.bib`, and when a script of the deck changes `window.beesonTex` after `tex.js` is read. Such a change is lost in a session, since each page of a session reads `tex.js` again. Write it into `preamble.tex` or `references.bib` instead. A deck without these three files is not checked for them.

## Code

A code box is a `pre` with a `code` in it, and it is colored with highlight.js in the highlight plugin of reveal.js. Load `theme/vendor/reveal/highlight.js` after `notes.js`, and give the `code` the class of its language, e.g. `language-python`, since the language is guessed in a box without one. With the class `nohighlight`, a box is left uncolored. The tokens are colored from the palette of the theme in each mode, and the code is set in the system monospace at 22 px. At this size, about 24 lines can be shown under a heading, and about 29 on a slide without one. A line can be up to about 65 characters long in the text column, and up to about 115 on a slide with the class `wide`. For a longer box, set a smaller `font-size` in its `style`. In the editor, choose Smaller in the Format menu to write `font-size: 18px` on the `pre`, and Bigger to take it off, or set and clear the box Small in the Code pane.

To step through a box, number its lines with `data-line-numbers` on the `code`, and write one group of lines per step, separated by bars, e.g. `data-line-numbers="1-3|4|5-7"`. The first group is lit when the slide is shown, and each later group is lit at its step, while the other lines are dimmed to 35 percent. The whole box is dimmed at a step outside it after its last group, and it is restored at the step added at the end of the slide. In the print view, each group is printed on a separate page. While the deck is edited, the box is shown plain at the same size, since the text of the page is paired with the file. The demo slide "Code, one group of lines per step" is an example, cut from `demo/tycho-scene.py`.

## Plots

A plot is an animated 2D figure, drawn as SVG from a JSON script in the deck. Put a `div` with the class `plot` in a `figure-box`, or with the classes `plot plate` on a `figure` slide, and give it the id of the script in `data-plot`. Write the script next to it as `<script type="application/json" id="...">`. The script is inline, since fetching a file is blocked for a deck opened with a double click. The demo deck does not have a plot, and the test decks `test/pages/plot-steps.html` and `test/pages/plot-reveals.html` are examples to start from. In the editor, a plot is moved as a figure, and its texts are edited in place, as described at the end of this section. The rest of its script is written only in a text editor. A small plot may be put in a cell of a table as well, e.g. one plot per row beside the words of the row, with its size set in the style sheet of the talk. A cell that holds a plot and no words of its own is not edited as text in the editor, while the other cells of its row are.

The plot is played in a loop while its slide is shown, and it is paused when the slide is left. Under the plot, a button to play and pause, a scrubber with a tick at each event, and the value of the parameter are shown, and the plot is paused when you drag the scrubber. In the PDF, the plot is printed at its last frame, and in PowerPoint it is recorded, as described under PowerPoint. Set another frame with `data-plot-frame`, where a negative frame is counted from the end, and set `data-plot-paused` to show the still in the slides as well. To start the plot at a step, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-play`. The plot is then held at its still frame until the fragment is shown and played from its first frame afterward, and it is printed at its last frame from this step on. Under reduced motion, the plot is not played.

To reveal a plot in steps, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-show` with the ids of panels, marks, or elements, separated by spaces. Each of these is hidden until its fragment is shown. With `data-plot-tab` on a fragment, the plot is switched to the named tab, hence each tab is given a page of its own in the PDF. With `data-plot-once` beside it, the tab is played once from its first frame when the step is reached going forward, and held at its last frame, e.g. on a plot with `data-plot-paused`. Under reduced motion, the tab is shown at its still frame instead. With `data-plot-hide` on a fragment, the panels, marks, or elements of its ids are hidden from its step on. An empty `span` is enough for a fragment without text. To show one JSON script on several slides, give each slide an element of its own, with the ids of its panels in `data-plot-panels` and the names of the tabs it offers in `data-plot-tabs`. A tab named in a `data-plot-show` is offered once its fragment is shown, hence give the panels, marks, elements, and tabs distinct names.

These keys are read from the script.

| Key | Value |
|---|---|
| `panels` | A list of panels, each with an `id`, the axes `x` and `y`, and an optional `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels`. An axis is `{ "range": [a, b], "ticks": [...], "title": "..." }`, and a tick is a number or `{ "at": v, "text": "..." }` with a text of its own. Set `"scale": "asinh"` on an axis to draw a value v at asinh(v/u), where u is the `unit` of the axis, 1 by default. Set `"equal": true` for one scale on both axes, and `"axes": false` to leave the axes out. The `size` of a panel is its share of the plot, 1 by default, and its `margin` is `[top, right, bottom, left]` in pixels. |
| `marks` | A list of the animated marks, each with an `id`, a `panel`, and a `kind`. The data of a mark are given in each tab. |
| `tabs` | A list of named data sets, each with a `name`, a `label` for its button, the `values` of the parameter at the frames, the `data` of the marks by their ids, an optional list of `times`, an optional `active` list, and optional `panels`. In `panels`, the `x`, `y`, `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels` given for a panel id replace those of the panel while the tab is shown. |
| `parameter` | `{ "label": "$A$", "digits": 3 }`, the name of the parameter and the digits of its value. |
| `duration`, `hold`, `fade` | The seconds of a pass over the frames of a tab without `times`, 8 by default, the seconds for which the last frame is held, 1 by default, and the seconds of the crossfade at the start of the next pass, 0.5 by default and at most a pass. In the crossfade, the moving marks of the next pass are faded in while the held last frame is faded out. A text of the held frame is faded out in the first half of the crossfade and a text of the next pass in the second half, so that two texts are not drawn over each other. |
| `layout` | `row` or `column`, the direction of the panels. By default, the panels are in a row in a wide box and in a column in a tall one. |

The data of a mark are given with one entry per value of the tab, or with one entry for a mark that does not move. Between two frames, the positions are interpolated, and a text is taken from the nearest frame.

Without `times`, the frames of a tab are shown at even steps over `duration`. With `times`, each frame is shown at its time in seconds into the pass, from 0 and increasing, hence a tab can be slowed near an event, and a frame given twice with a later time is held still. The scrubber and its ticks are placed by these times.

| Kind | Entry | Drawn as |
|---|---|---|
| `points` | A flat list `[x1, y1, x2, y2, ...]` | A dot at each point, filled, or a ring with `"hollow": true` |
| `trace` | A point `[x, y]` | The whole path in a faint line, left out with `"ghost": false`, and the path up to the current frame in full with a dot at its end |
| `label` | `{ "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, with one position and one text, or a list of each | The text at the position |
| `events` | A list of `{ "frame": n, "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, not one per frame | A ring and its text from the frame on, and a tick on the scrubber in the tone of the mark |

The faint line of a trace runs over the whole path, unless the data of the trace are given as `{ "points": [...], "ghost": n }`, with the list of points in `points`. The faint line then ends at frame n, and the rest of the path is drawn only as it is traced.

Give the data of a trace a list `tones` beside `points`, with one tone per point, to draw each part of the path in the tone of the frame at which the part starts, and the dot at its end in the tone of the nearest frame. Give the mark of a trace a `fade` in seconds to draw its path faint and to fade each part out over these seconds once the dot has passed it, hence only the recent path is seen.

A region is a polygon of `points`, filled at `opacity`. A curve is a polyline of `points` or a `circle` `[cx, cy, r]`, with a `width` and a `dash`. A label is a `text` at `at`, with an `anchor` (`start`, `middle`, or `end`) and an offset `dx`, `dy` in pixels. A mark is drawn with a `size`, a `width`, an `anchor`, `dx`, and `dy` as well. Give each element and mark a `tone`, i.e. a color of the theme without its dashes (`accent`, `violet`, `text`, `subtle`, `muted`, or `data-1` to `data-8`), and the plot is drawn right in both modes. A `class` on an element or a mark is a target of `data-highlight`, as in stepped math, and the element keeps its color when a tab is chosen. In `active`, list the ids of the elements emphasized at each frame. The other ids named in the active list of any tab are dimmed at the same frame. An element or a mark with an id is drawn with this id in `data-plot-id`, e.g. for the selector of a fly.

An element or a mark revealed at a step is shown at once, or after the seconds of its `delay`. A curve with `wipe` is then traced from its first point to its last over the seconds of `wipe`, and a dashed curve keeps its `dash`. A region with `iris` is then grown over the seconds of `iris` as a disk from its point `from`, or from the mean of its points, until the region is filled. Each of them is hidden at once when its step is undone, and shown at once with reduced motion and in the PDF.

A formula in a text of the JSON is written between `$` signs, e.g. `$\delta x$ in km`. Each text is typeset by MathJax as one formula with the macros of `preamble.tex`, as on the slide, and the words outside the `$` signs are set in `\text`, in the font of the formulas. Hence a macro is written between `$` signs, e.g. `$\LL{1}$`. The value of the parameter in the readout changes at each frame, and it is drawn in the font of the text. Each text is typeset once when the deck is loaded, and a label whose text changes at each frame is drawn from these formulas. A formula that MathJax cannot typeset is reported by `npm run check`, as on the slide, and its text is shown as written. In a deck without MathJax, each text is shown as written, with its formulas between their `$` signs. A fault in the script is shown in place of the plot with its path, e.g. `tabs[0].data.m: 3 frames are given, and the tab has 2 values`, and `npm run check` fails on it.

In the editor, click a text of a plot to select it, and click it again, or press Return, to edit its source, e.g. `$\delta x$ in km`. Return, Esc, and a click outside the text end the edit, and the text is typeset again. The texts are the titles of the axes and the panels, the labels, the texts of label and events marks, the names of the tabs, the name of the parameter, and the ticks written as `{ "at": v, "text": "..." }`. A tick given as a number, a tab without a `label`, and the value of the parameter are not texts of the script, hence they are not edited. A label whose text changes at each frame is edited at the frame shown. An edit rewrites only the string of its text in the JSON script, escaped as JSON requires, and it is undone as one edit. All plots drawn from the script are drawn again, the copies included. In a session, the edit is sent as any other edit, and the other pages load the deck again.

## Browsers

You can show a deck in the current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, and in the browsers built on their engines, on a computer, a phone, or a tablet. This holds for both modes, the steps, the speaker view, and the viewer of tycho, whether you open the file with a double click or from a web server. All browsers on iOS are built on the engine of Safari. These are the limits.

- In Safari on a Mac, the paper size of the deck is ignored, and the PDF is printed on the paper chosen in the print dialog. Choose a paper of 16:9, or export with `npm run pdf`. The backdrops are also printed fainter than in the other browsers.
- On an iPhone, fullscreen is not available, since the fullscreen API is missing there.
- On a phone or a tablet, the speaker view is opened with the key `s`, hence you need a keyboard. A deck is opened from a web server, such as the site of the repository, since a double click on a file does not apply there. Printing is not tested.
- In every browser, `\require{bbm}`, `\require{bboldx}`, and `\require{dsfont}` show the error "Extension failed to load", since the fonts of these three extensions of MathJax are not vendored.

## PowerPoint

At some conferences, a speaker is given a laptop that shows only PowerPoint and PDF files. For such a room, run `npm run pptx`, which writes `slides.pptx` next to `slides.pdf`. Each step of the deck is a full-slide image, and a click in PowerPoint is a step of the deck. The text cannot be edited in this file.

A backdrop that is traced or looped in the deck is recorded from the first step of its slide at which it is traced, and the recording is placed on the PowerPoint slide of this step. The recording is over a still of the slide, i.e. its last frame, and under an image of the words of each step, and it is started with the slide. Each later step of the same slide of the deck is one more image of the words, shown on a click, while the same videos are shown at the steps. What is drawn under the backdrop, e.g. the QR code of a closing slide, is part of the recording, as it is drawn at the first recorded step. Hence an element under a recorded backdrop is kept as at the first recorded step, also where it is hidden or dimmed at a later step. A backdrop of the same slide that is traced only from a later step is not recorded, and it is drawn complete in the image of the words of its step and of the steps after it. A recording of loops alone is one period long, i.e. 9 s, and it is looped in PowerPoint. A recording with a trace is as long as the trace, and it is not looped. A backdrop that is drawn complete is left in the image of its step. A tycho scene is recorded once and placed in the box of its viewer as a video, with the first frame of the scene as the poster of the video. A viewer of the fragment style `play` is started on the click of its step, as a video of this style is.

Surfaces and plots are recorded at each step, and each recording is placed in the box of its element as a looped video. The recording of a surface is one turn of its camera, i.e. `data-turn` seconds, 90 by default. A surface that does not turn is left in the image of its step. At a step that changes the state of a surface, a clip of the change is played once from the start of the slide, and the turn of the new state follows it without a jump. A drag that turns a surface in the deck cannot be done in PowerPoint, and the camera starts at its written angle on each PowerPoint slide. The recording of a plot is a pass over its frames and its hold, and it is played once when its step plays the plot once. A paused plot, a plot held until a later step, and a plot under reduced motion are left in the image of their step. The recordings of a step that are the same as at the step before are kept playing through it.

With `npm run pptx -- --still`, the file is written without a recording. The backdrops are then drawn complete, each scene is left as the still of its first frame, each surface and plot as the still of its step, and an H.264 encoder is not needed. The recordings are encoded with the H.264 encoder of WebCodecs, which is built into Google Chrome. They are encoded in the full range of levels, since a dark ground inside a recording in the limited range of video is drawn by PowerPoint one level darker than the slide around it. In a browser without one, the export fails, and the reason is printed together with the option `--still`. A deck without anything to record is exported by such a browser as well.

Each video is placed over its image as a PowerPoint video, and it is started when its step is shown, together with the other videos of the step. The steps with a video are placed on one PowerPoint slide, hence the video goes on playing through them. A video of the fragment style `play` is part of the images of the steps before its own, with its poster, and a video under a later one of its place is left out from the step of the later one on. PowerPoint needs an MP4 file, and the export fails on a video without one. The speaker notes are copied to the PowerPoint notes. Keynote opens a `.pptx`, hence a separate Keynote export is not needed.

## Tooling

For `npm test`, `npm run check`, `npm run pdf`, and `npm run pptx`, Chrome is looked up at its default location on macOS and Windows, and on the `PATH` on Linux. Edge is used on Windows when Chrome is missing. To use another browser build, set `CHROME_PATH` to its executable. For the recordings of `npm run pptx`, an H.264 encoder is needed, which is built into Google Chrome and not into Chromium, and with `--still` you can export from a browser without one.
To run the check in another engine, use `npm run check -- --browser firefox` or `npm run check -- --browser safari`. Firefox is driven with `geckodriver`, which is looked up in `GECKOWEBDRIVER` and on the `PATH`, and `FIREFOX_PATH` selects another Firefox build. Safari is driven with `safaridriver`, which you enable once with `sudo safaridriver --enable`. Quit Safari before a run, since a session cannot be started in a Safari that is already open. Keep the screen unlocked and the window of Safari unminimized during a run, since a hidden page draws no frames. The check then stops at the first page with this reason. A page from `file://` is refused under automation, hence the deck is served from a local port in Safari, and a double click is not covered by this check. The problems in the page are reported in all three browsers, but the console is read only in Chrome, and the phone layout, a pixel ratio of 1.25, and a save of the editor are checked only in Chrome. On Linux, run Firefox under `xvfb-run`, since WebGL in software is available there only in a window.

Besides the problems in the page, `npm run check` fails on four mistakes of writing. It fails on speaker notes of more than 40 words, since notes hold only what the speaker needs and the slide does not show, i.e. numbers, answers to likely questions, and lines to say. It fails on a heading more than 1 px away from the place of the heading on most slides of its layout, i.e. content, narrow, wide, figure, or split, measured in both modes at the first step of each slide. Title, section, and closing slides are left out, and so is the print view. It fails as well on an element copied or moved between two steps. At each step, once its animations have ended, the text elements, formulas, tables, and figures of the slide are compared with the step before, apart from those in an SVG, a canvas, a code box, a plot, a surface, a backdrop, or a TyCHO viewer. A copy is an element hidden at a step while another element with the same text appears, e.g. one sentence or display written once per step with `current-visible`. Formulas are compared by their TeX without the wrappers `\class{...}{}`. Write such an element once, and change its parts with `data-highlight` or a stack. A move is an element shown at both steps whose box moves by more than 1 px, apart from the classes `grow` and `shrink` of reveal.js and a fly. It fails as well on the words of a text element that run off the 1920 by 1080 px of the slide at its last step, measured on the box of the words rather than of the element. Overlaps between elements are not reported. Copies, moves, and words off the slide are measured in the first mode only. In a browser without `checkVisibility` or `getAnimations`, the check prints that these three were not measured.

Give a slide `data-allow` to allow named problems on it, e.g. a heading placed lower on purpose or a quote in the notes, with one or more of `notes`, `heading`, `copy`, `move`, and `outside`, separated by spaces. The check prints each use of the attribute and each allowed problem, and does not fail on them.

Run `npm run tex` after you edit `preamble.tex` or `references.bib` in a text editor, and after you cite a new key, since only the cited entries are written into `tex.js`. For these files it needs only Node.js, from version 22.18 on, or the packages of `npm ci` with an older Node. During a session, the edit command writes `tex.js` itself, as described under The synchronous editor.

For a TikZ figure, `npm run tex` and the edit command look up `latex` and `dvisvgm` on the `PATH`, then in the usual folders of TeX Live and MiKTeX. To use another TeX, set `BEESON_TEXBIN` to the folder of its programs, which is then the only folder searched.

If you copied `theme/` and `brand/` by hand, copy `.agents/`, `.claude/`, `AGENTS.md`, `scripts/`, `src/`, `styles/`, `package.json`, and the launchers `Edit.command`, `Edit.cmd`, and `Edit.sh` as well. You can then run `npm run pdf` and `npm run pptx` without `npm ci`, and `npm run check` as well from Node.js 22.18 on.
