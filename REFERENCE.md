# Reference

Each element of the theme `beeson` is described here in full, with the formats of its files. The start of a talk is given in the README, and the vocabulary of the theme in `AGENTS.md`.

## Slides

Give a section slide `data-run` to show another text in the running head of its section, e.g. its heading without the math. Give a slide `data-visibility="hidden"` to leave it out of the show. Such a slide is removed from the page of the show, and it is kept in the file and in the editor. The next slide is faded in over 400 ms by default. With `data-transition="none"` on a slide, the next one is shown at once, and with `data-auto-animate` on both slides, the elements with the same `data-id` are moved between them. Write the seconds planned for a slide in its `data-timing`, which is read in the pacing of the speaker view and in the times of the scrubber.

In the editor, the running head is written with the field Running Head of the Slide pane, and a hidden slide with Hide Slide in the Slide menu or with the box Hidden in the show. Choose the transition with None, Fade, or Magic Move at the head of the Steps pane, and with Magic Move the matched elements of the two slides are given one `data-id`. At the end of Rehearse Timings, press Keep Timings to write the seconds spent on each slide into its `data-timing`.

## Steps and modes

Give each element its step once the slide is complete: `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. During the talk, a new element is faded in, and the earlier ones are dimmed to 45 percent. The last element is also shown alone at first, and all of them are restored to full opacity at one more step, which is added to every slide with a dimmed element. A slide whose fragments are empty, e.g. the steps of a plot, is not given this step.

A step can be shown by itself after a delay, as a step that starts After Previous in PowerPoint. Give a fragment of the step before it `data-autoslide` with the delay in whole milliseconds. With `data-autoslide` on the last step, the deck is moved on to the next slide after the delay. Press a key during a delay to show the next step at once, and the later delays are still played. When you go back, the steps reached by a delay are skipped up to the last step reached by a key. Press A to pause and resume the timed steps. A slide with a delay on its last step is not given the step that restores the dimmed fragments, and the look of its last step is kept through the transition. Such a slide is marked with `data-moves-on` when the deck is started, and the attribute is not written in the file. In the Steps pane of the editor, choose After Previous as the start of a step and type the delay in seconds, at least 0.1, or step it by 0.1 s with the arrows of the field. The `data-autoslide` is then written on the first fragment of the step before.

You flip the mode with the key `d` or with the button at the upper right. The change is drawn as a circle at the button or at the point of the click, grown on a flip to light and shrunk on a flip to dark. During a flip, a backdrop that is being traced is drawn on in both modes. A viewer of tycho, a surface, a plot, or a video is held still in the old mode for up to 450 ms, until the new mode is shown over it. The mode is kept per browser in local storage. To fix it for a venue, add the URL parameter `?theme=light`. To draw every backdrop complete at once for a screenshot, add `?still`.

## The editor

You can change the words, the formulas, the speaker notes, the steps, and the places of the figures of a deck on the slides themselves, without opening `index.html` in a text editor. The editor is loaded only when the address of the deck carries the parameter `?edit`, and a deck without it does not load any of it. The page is laid out like the normal view of PowerPoint: a toolbar at the top with the name of the file, its state, and the buttons Undo, Redo, Open, Save, Save as, Autosave, and Present, the list of the slides at the left, the slide in the middle, a pane for the notes under it, a pane for the steps at the right, and a status bar at the bottom with the slide number and the buttons Slides, Steps, and Notes.

1. Open `index.html` in a browser, with a double click or from a web server, and press E, which opens the same slide in the editor, or add `?edit` to the end of the address, e.g. `file:///Users/you/talk/index.html?edit`. When the address ends in a slide such as `#/3`, put `?edit` before the `#`.
2. After a double click, a card asks you to choose the file of the deck, since a file on your disk is not readable by a page until you choose it. Choose the same `index.html`, whose folder is named on the card. From a web server, the file is read by the editor itself, and the card is not shown.
3. Click any text and type. Enter starts a new line in the same block, a paste is inserted as plain text, and the arrow keys move within the text. In a text outside a list and a table, Tab and Shift+Tab do not leave the text, and they stop only at the formulas of the block. Press Cmd+B or Cmd+I on a Mac, and Ctrl+B or Ctrl+I elsewhere, to make the selected words bold or italic, which are written as `<strong>` and `<em>`, and press the same keys again to take the format off. Press Esc to leave the text. The block is then selected, as a text box in PowerPoint, the arrow keys nudge it, and a second Esc deselects it, after which the arrow keys and the buttons in the status bar move between the slides again. The keys of reveal.js for the overview, the black screen, fullscreen, and the speaker view are off in the editor. Every step of a slide is shown at once, and the backdrops are drawn still while you edit. On a slide with an element that is hidden at a step, e.g. with `fade-out`, `current-visible`, or `fade-in-then-out`, the steps are cut into layers at each step at which an element is hidden. The slide is then shown one layer at a time, as it is at the last step of the layer, and you choose the layer with the buttons after Shows in the status bar. A text that runs past the edge of the slide, or whose lines are drawn over a picture, a video, or a plot of its slide, e.g. after a change of the layout, is outlined in orange, and the state in the toolbar counts such texts.
4. Press Cmd+Z on a Mac and Ctrl+Z elsewhere to undo the last change, whether it is typing, a formula, a step, or a move, and Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y to redo it. The Undo and Redo buttons in the toolbar do the same. An undo shows the slide of the change and puts the caret where the text changed, and once you have undone every change, the deck is marked as saved again.
5. Move between the slides with the arrow keys, the buttons in the status bar, or a click in the list at the left, which names each slide with its title and its layout. Rest the pointer on a row to see a small copy of its slide beside the list, with every step shown. A backdrop is drawn in the copy once its slide has been shown. Press G and type the number of a slide to jump to it. The Slides button hides the list, and the choice is kept between visits.
6. Click a formula, or reach it with Tab and press Enter, to open its TeX in a field under it, with its rendering under the TeX as you type. Apply, or Cmd+Enter on a Mac and Ctrl+Enter elsewhere, typesets it again, and Esc leaves it as it was. A TeX with an error is refused with the message of MathJax, an empty TeX is refused as well, and the slide is not touched.
7. Press Notes to show the speaker notes of the slide in the pane under it, and type into them. On a slide without notes, you can type into the empty pane, and the notes are saved once they have text. Drag the strip above the pane, or focus it with Tab and press the up and down arrows, to change its height, which is kept between visits.
8. Press Steps to open the pane at the right, which lists the elements of the slide in reading order with the step at which each appears, as the animation pane of PowerPoint and the build order of Keynote. A formula is named in the list by its TeX. Choose the step of an element in its select, or choose Step 1, new, before the others to show it ahead of the other steps, which are each moved one step later. Press One by one to show the elements one after the other and All at once to show them with the slide. Elements with the same step appear together, and the number of the step is shown at the upper left of each stepped element on the slide. A heading and the footnote can be given a step too, and One by one leaves them with the slide. A step is written into the file as a reveal.js fragment, i.e. `class="fragment"` with a `data-fragment-index`. Each group of lines of a code box after the first is listed as a row at its step, and the row is moved with the other steps, while its lines are changed in the Code pane. Choose a row and press Play From, or choose Play From Step in the menu of a row, to play the steps of the slide in place from this step.
9. Drag a figure to move it, and drag a paragraph, a list, a formula, or a table by the handle ⠿ at its upper left, since a click into the text edits it. While you drag, the element snaps to the edges and the centers of the other elements of the slide, of the slide itself, and of its own place, and an orange guide shows the line it is aligned with. Hold Alt to drag freely, and hold Shift to keep the drag on the axis along which you moved farther. The arrow keys nudge the selected element by one slide pixel, or by ten with Shift. Nudges less than a second apart are undone together with one Cmd+Z. While you move the element, its offset is shown in slide pixels at the lower right of its frame. A double click on the handle, or Enter on it, puts the element back. A figure is also reached with Tab, which selects it. The offset is written into the tag of the element as the style `translate`, in slide pixels, and nothing else in the layout of the slide is changed. An element turned with its rotate handle or in the Arrange pane is written with the style `rotate`, in whole degrees from 1 to 359. When you bring an element to the front or send it backward in the Arrange menu, an element out of the flow, e.g. a figure, is moved before or after the others in the file, and an element in the flow, e.g. a paragraph, is given the style `z-index`, with `position: relative` where the theme does not position it, so that the layout of the slide is not changed. A figure is turned at its picture, from the figure and from the picture selected by a second click. The `rotate` is then written on its image or video, on both images of a pair for the two modes, and the caption is left where it is.
10. Press Save, or Cmd+S on a Mac and Ctrl+S elsewhere. In Chrome and Edge, each edit is written into its place in `index.html`, and every other byte of the file is kept, hence a diff of the file shows only your changes. From a web server, the first save asks where to write the file. Open with unsaved changes asks whether to save them first, to open a file without them, or to go on editing. Save as, or Cmd+Shift+S and Ctrl+Shift+S, writes the deck into another file, and the next saves go into it. Firefox, Safari, and the browsers built on them do not have the file pickers of Chrome, hence a save there is a download of a copy of `index.html` with your edits, and you replace the original with it. To choose the place of the copy at each save, set the browser to ask where to save each download: in Safari under Settings, General, File download location, and in Firefox under Settings, General, Downloads. In the dialog, choose `index.html` in the folder of the deck and replace it.
11. Press Autosave to save after each change, as the AutoSave switch of PowerPoint. The file is written 1.5 s after the last change, once it has been chosen, and the switch is kept between visits. In every browser, the unsaved edits are also kept in the storage of the browser, and when the tab is closed or the browser crashes before a save, a card offers them the next time the deck is opened: Restore and save writes them into the file and shows the page again, Download a copy puts the file with them into your downloads, and Discard forgets them.
12. Press Present to play the saved deck in place, in a frame over the editor and in full screen where the browser grants it. Press Esc or `e` to return to the editor at the slide shown last. With unsaved changes, a card asks whether to save them first, to present the saved file without them, or to go on editing.

Select two elements or more and choose Group in the Arrange menu, Cmd+Alt+G on a Mac and Ctrl+G elsewhere, to select and drag them together. Each member is then given the same `data-group`, e.g. `data-group="g1"`, with a name that is not used elsewhere in the deck, and a copy of the group is given a new name. With Ungroup, Cmd+Alt+Shift+G or Ctrl+Shift+G, the attribute is taken off every member of the group. Choose Lock, Cmd+L or Ctrl+L, to keep an element in its place. The element is then given `data-locked`, and it is not moved, resized, or rotated with the pointer, the keys, or the fields, while its text can still be edited. A locked element is framed in gray when it is selected, and the attribute is taken off with Unlock, Cmd+Alt+L or Ctrl+Shift+L. Both attributes are read only in the editor and do not have an effect in the show, hence you can also write them by hand.

Choose Object List in the View menu, Cmd+Shift+L on a Mac and Alt+F10 elsewhere, to list the elements of the current slide under the thumbnails, from front to back, as the object list of Keynote. Each row is named by the kind of its element and the first words of its text, e.g. Text: The lead-in of a display, and the members of a group are listed under a row of the group, which you fold with its arrow. Click a row to select its element, and hold Shift or Cmd, or Ctrl elsewhere, to add the row to the selection or take it off. A selection on the slide is shown in the list as well. Press the padlock of a row to lock or unlock its element, and drag a row up or down to bring its element forward or send it backward, by the layers of Bring Forward and Send Backward. The row of a group is dragged as one element, as one change of the history, and its members keep their order. A row that these commands cannot move, e.g. a paragraph on a slide without a figure or a moved block, is not dragged. The line of a drag stops at the last place that Bring to Front or Send to Back reaches. While a row has the focus, the keys of the four layer commands, Group, Ungroup, Lock, and Unlock act on the rows chosen. Type into the field Search at the bottom of the list to show only the rows whose names contain the words, and press F6 to reach the list from the other panes. With several paragraphs, headings, or lists selected, the Text pane of the inspector formats all of them at once, as one change of the history, and a control whose value differs between them is shown as mixed. While you drag an element, two equal gaps between it and its neighbors are marked with a double arrow each, as in PowerPoint.

Choose View > Rulers to show the rulers, and drag a guide out of a ruler onto the slide. An element snaps to the guides as it snaps to the other elements. Drag a guide on the slide to move it, and drag it back onto a ruler to remove it. The guides of a slide are written into `data-guides` on its `section`, e.g. `data-guides="x480 y270"` in slide pixels, and each change of them is undone with one Cmd+Z. Clear All Guides on Slide, Copy Guides, and Paste Guides are in the View and Slide menus. Guides that an earlier version of the editor kept in the storage of the browser are shown as before, and they are written into the file at the next change of the guides of their slide. The attribute is read only in the editor, like `data-group` and `data-locked`.

To add a slide after the selected one, choose New Slide in the Slide menu, press Ctrl+M, or press Return in the list at the left. The new slide has the layout of the selected slide when it is a content, figure, or split slide, and it is a content slide after a title, section, or closing slide. Choose another layout with Insert > New Slide or with the button Add Slide of the toolbar. In the Slide menu, choose Duplicate Slide, Cmd+D or Ctrl+D, to copy the selected slides with their ids renamed, and Delete Slide, or Backspace in the list, to delete them, as long as one slide is left. Choose Hide Slide, Cmd+Shift+H on a Mac, to write `data-visibility="hidden"`, and choose it again as Show Slide to take it off. Reorder the slides with a drag in the list, or with Move Slide Up and Move Slide Down, Alt+Up and Alt+Down. In the list, the selected slides are also cut, copied, and pasted with the keys of the clipboard. Choose a layout under Layout in the Slide menu to change the class of the slide in place. Its heading, words, figures, footnote, and notes are then moved into the wrappers of the new layout, with their order, their steps, and their places. With Reset Slide, the slide is written again in its own layout, and the places are taken off its elements.

In the outline view of the list at the left, press Shift+Tab on a paragraph or a first-level item to make it the title of a new content slide after its slide, as in PowerPoint. The new slide is written with an `h2` and a list, and the nested items of the item, the items after it in its list, and the paragraphs and lists after the list are moved into it. Press Tab on the title of a slide to make it the last first-level item of the slide before, with the paragraphs and lists of the slide after it, and the slide is then deleted. On the first slide, on a title, section, figure, or closing slide, and on a slide with anything other than a heading, paragraphs, and lists, the title is left in place when you press Tab. Each of the two changes is undone with one Cmd+Z.

Choose View > Check Accessibility, or click the state of accessibility in the status bar, to open the Accessibility pane at the right of the slide. Under Hard-to-read text contrast, each text that is too close in color to what is behind it is listed with its slide, the mode in which it is hard to read, and its contrast ratio. The color behind a text is that of its slide, of a Split panel, or of the fill of a table. Both modes are measured, whichever is shown, with the contrast ratio of WCAG 2 (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines), and a text is listed under 4.5:1, or under 3:1 at 36 px and more. The tones of the theme on a plain slide are above these limits in both modes, and the footnote and the running head are not checked, since they are part of the chrome of the theme. Choose a row to show its slide in its mode, with the text selected. A text over a backdrop, a surface, or a picture is not measured, since the color behind it is not one color. It is listed under Tips as a text to check by eye, and the state of the deck is left Good to go.

Choose Play > Presenter View, or press Alt+Enter, to play the show in a tab of its own with the presenter display in front of you. Click the button Display Options at the right of the clock to choose which of the current slide, the next slide, the presenter notes, the clock, and the timer are shown, as in Keynote. With Play > Customize Presenter Display…, the display is opened without the show and with these options open. Click Customize Presenter Display at the foot of the options to frame each shown part, then drag a frame to move its part and drag its corner to resize it, with a slide kept at its aspect ratio. Use Auto Layout puts the parts back, and Done or Esc ends the customizing. The choice and the layout are kept in the storage of the browser for the deck, the layout as fractions of the display so that it holds at another window size, and neither is written into the file.

In the Insert menu, choose Text Box, Formula, Inline Formula, Table…, Plot, Image…, Video…, QR Code, Code Box, Footnote, Line, or Arrow to put the element at its place in the layout of the current slide. An empty text box, code box, or footnote is written into the file only once you type into it. A new table is put into an empty zone or plate of the slide, whose inner width its columns share, and else where a place of its size is free, or else over the content at the place where it covers the least of the texts and the figures. An image or a video, chosen in the menu or dropped onto the slide, is put into an empty zone or plate of the slide, and else into a new zone. A file dropped onto a picture that the mode of the slide hides, or chosen with its Choose Image for Light Mode… or Choose Image for Dark Mode…, is added as its twin for the other mode. With Caption in the Image pane, a picture gets a caption under it, which you type into. At a save, its file is written into the folder `media` next to the deck in Chrome and Edge, and it is downloaded with the copy of the deck in the other browsers.

Choose Insert > Plot to put a plot of one trace into the first empty zone or plate of the slide, or else into a new zone, which is moved off the texts and the figures of the slide where a place of its size is free, and else to the place where it covers the least of them, and to open its data. To open the data of a selected plot, choose Edit Plot Data… in the Plot pane, or press Cmd+Shift+D on a Mac and Ctrl+Shift+D elsewhere. The data are shown in a window with a sheet for each tab, a sheet for each mark of events, and the sheet Axes with the ranges of the panels. In the sheet of a tab, each mark is a series with two columns, its x and its y. Choose Add Series to add a trace after the series of the focused column, in the first data color that no mark of its panel takes and with points on the diagonal of its panel, and Delete Series to remove the focused series from the marks and from every tab. To move a series, drag the head of one of its columns, or choose Move Series Left or Move Series Right in Format > Plot. Press + next to the sheets to add a copy of the shown tab, and − to delete the shown tab. A tab named by a step of the slide is deleted only after a confirmation that names the step, and its name is taken off the step in the same change of the history. Drag over the cells, click with Shift, or press Shift with an arrow key to select a range. A copy of a range is written as text with tabs, as from a spreadsheet. A paste of such text is written into the cells from the focused one, and rows and series are added where it runs past the sheet. Press Delete to clear a range. A text is cleared, and a number keeps its value and is marked in red, since a plot does not draw a missing value. When the window opens, the first cell has the focus. Tab and Shift+Tab move through the controls of the window without leaving it, and Esc closes it and gives the focus back to the slide. In a cell that you edit, the first Esc only leaves the cell. In the Plot pane, set the title of each panel and whether its axes are drawn, the title, the range, and the scale of each axis, linear or asinh with its unit, and the color of each series, chosen from the accent and the eight data colors of the theme. While you drag a surface to turn it, its azimuth and elevation are shown at the pointer, and the fields of the Surface pane are updated with it. These fields are drawn in the color of the selection until you choose Use Current View, and the view is then written into the file.

A text on the page that cannot be found in the file is skipped, e.g. after a change to the file in another program while the editor is open. The number of skipped texts is shown in the state in the toolbar. Press Which? next to the state to see the list of these texts, with their slides and the reasons.

## Text and tables

Write bold as `strong`, italic as `em`, a strikethrough as `s`, a superscript as `sup`, a subscript as `sub`, and code as `code`. To color words, put them in a `span` with one class out of `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, `tone-red`, `tone-subtle`, and `tone-muted`, which are drawn in `--accent`, `--violet`, `--blue`, `--red`, `--subtle`, and `--muted` in each mode. Such a color is shown from the start of the slide, and a color that is a step is set with `data-highlight`, as described under Stepped math. In the Format menu of the editor, Strikethrough is set with Cmd+Shift+X on a Mac and Alt+Shift+5 elsewhere, Superscript and Subscript are under Baseline, and the colors are under Text Color, where the span is taken off with Text. With Clear Formatting, every format is taken off, apart from a link, a formula, and a span of a colored symbol. With Paragraph Style, a block is written as `p` for Body, `p.small` for Small, `h3` for Subheading, `p.caption` for Caption, and `div.foot` for Footnote.

A heading, a paragraph, a list, a `math` div, or a caption can be given a width in px in its `style`, e.g. `style="width: 720px"`, as a text box of PowerPoint. Its text is then wrapped at this width, even past the text column, and its height is the height of the wrapped text. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected block.

In a table, the width of a column is written in px in the `style` of its cell in the first row, e.g. `<th style="width: 320px">`. This width is the width of the content of the cell, without the padding of 40 px at its right. In the editor, it is written with a drag of the border between two columns, with Distribute Columns Evenly, and with Resize Column to Fit Content. A cell set at the right with Numbers at the Right or Auto-Align is written with `text-align: right` in its `style`, and cells merged with Merge Cells are written with `colspan` and `rowspan`. A copy of a range of cells is written as text with tabs, as from a spreadsheet, and as a table in which each cell keeps its formulas with their delimiters, its bold, italics, and strikethroughs, and its words colored with a class `tone-*`. A paste of such a table into the editor writes them back, and a table pasted from another app is written as plain text.

## Lines

A line or an arrow on a slide is an inline `svg` with the class `line`, a child of the `section`, as large as the box of its line and moved to its place with `translate`. In the `svg`, one `line` is drawn from `x1`, `y1` to `x2`, `y2`, in pixels of the box. Its arrowheads are the `marker` of the `svg`, named in `marker-end` for an arrow and in `marker-start` as well for a double arrow. The line is stroked 3 px wide in `--text`, or in the color of one class `tone-muted`, `tone-accent`, `tone-violet`, `tone-blue`, or `tone-red` on the `svg`, and its arrowheads are filled in the same color. An orange arrow from (200, 300) to (520, 300) on the slide is written as follows.

```html
<svg class="line tone-accent" width="320" height="1" style="translate: 200px 300px"><defs><marker id="line-1" orient="auto-start-reverse"><path d="M-4-2.5 1 0-4 2.5z"></path></marker></defs><line x1="0" y1="0" x2="320" y2="0" marker-end="url(#line-1)"></line></svg>
```

Give each marker an id that is not used elsewhere in the deck, and name it in the `url()` of its own `svg`. In the editor, draw a line with Insert > Line or Insert > Arrow by a drag from one end to the other, with Shift for steps of 45 degrees, or click to insert a line 320 px long. The line is written before the notes of the slide. Move it as any element, drag its ends to change them, and set its color and its arrowheads in Format > Line or in the Line pane. A copy of a line is given a new id, and its `url()` is pointed at it.

## Backdrops

Set the drawing of a `backdrop` with its attribute `data-scene`. Each drawing is computed in the browser by the code in `src/scenes.ts`, and the demo deck shows each of them.

| Name | Drawing |
|---|---|
| `nest` | The Lyapunov families of L1 and L2 with the distant retrograde orbits about the Moon |
| `tubes` | The stable and unstable tubes of orbits at L1 and L2 |
| `wideRibbon` | The tubes of an L1 orbit toward the Earth, after Amlan Sinha's figures in the group deck and in [Phantom Pathways](https://amlan-sinha.github.io/art-of-science/art-1/) |
| a file in `data-strands` | The polylines of a file next to the deck, described below |

Set the window of the drawing with `data-cx` and `data-cy`, the point at the center of the box, and `data-half`, half of the height of the box, all in nondimensional units. The backdrop of a title, section, or closing slide is traced when its slide is shown, and the backdrop of another slide is drawn complete. Add `data-live` to trace it as well. To make a backdrop a step of its slide, e.g. to show the words of a title slide before the logo is traced, give it `class="fragment"` and a `data-fragment-index`. The backdrop is then hidden until its step, traced from this step on, and blanked again when the step is undone. While the pointer is on a backdrop, the strands within 20 slide pixels of it are tinted orange.

Label a point of a named drawing with a `lab` span inside the backdrop, whose `data-at` is `L1`, `L2`, `moon`, or `earth`, and shift the label from the point by `data-dx` and `data-dy` pixels. A label is typeset with MathJax. Write a libration point as `\(\LL{1}\)`, i.e. a calligraphic L with its index, with the macro `\LL` of `theme/macros.js`, and write it in the same way in a heading, a caption, and the notes.

A backdrop can also be drawn from a file of polylines, e.g. the members of families computed elsewhere. Name the file in `data-strands` in place of `data-scene`, with its path relative to the deck. The demo deck draws `demo/broucke-sample.js`, eight members of each of Broucke's families 7 and 11 of the elliptic restricted three-body problem, which is also the example to start from.

The file is a JSON object with a list `families` of records and an optional list `bodies`. Each record is an object with a list `points` of `[x, y]` pairs, and optionally with a list `t` or `theta` of the time or the true anomaly at each point. The strand of a record is traced on this clock, or at a uniform speed along its length without one. A record with `"craft": true` is the path of a spacecraft, whose head is drawn without `data-heads`. The other keys of a record are free, e.g. `family`, `branch`, `frame`, and `e` in the sample, and you select and color the records by them. Eight keys set the look of a strand instead. The key `tone` is `orange`, `violet`, or `text`, for a color of the theme. The key `color` is a CSS color, or a pair of them for the dark and the light mode, drawn in place of the color of the tone. The key `alpha` is the opacity of the strand from 0 to 1. The key `weight` is its width in hairlines, and `head` is the radius of its head in hairlines, with 0 for none. The key `start` is the time on the clock at which the strand is begun, and with `glow` true it is drawn with a soft glow of its color. The key `fade` is the seconds of the clock of the record, from its first point, after which its opacity falls to zero at its last point, as the square of the time left. Each entry of `bodies` has the positions of the primary and the secondary as `m1` and `m2` in an object per frame, and the mass ratio as `mu`, e.g. `{"family": 7, "mu": 0.012155, "pulsating": {"m1": [-0.01215, 0], "m2": [0.98784, 0]}}`. A body entry is matched to every record with the same values for the numbers and strings of the entry. The bodies are drawn as dots only when all the drawn records put them at the same positions, within 10⁻⁴ in the units of the file, hence none are drawn in the inertial frame, where they move, or for records of two mass ratios.

Where the points of a record are too far apart for the turns of its strand, e.g. at a close pass of a body, the strand is drawn as a smooth curve through the points. The curve is the centripetal Catmull–Rom spline, and it is drawn only where it departs from the straight segment between two points by more than 0.1 px.

A deck opened with a double click cannot read a JSON file, since the browser refuses the request. Wrap the JSON in `Beeson.strands(` and `);`, and name the `.js` file in `data-strands`. A plain JSON file can be named only in a deck served from a web server. With a missing or malformed file, the backdrop is left empty, and one warning is printed in the console.

- With `data-strands-select`, you choose the records to draw, e.g. `family=7,frame=pulsating`. A term is `key=value`, `key=a|b` for either value, or `key<number`, `key<=number`, `key>number`, or `key>=number`. The terms of a group are separated by commas, and a record is drawn when it passes every term of one of the groups, which are separated by semicolons.
- With `data-strands-color`, you give each value of a key, e.g. `family`, a pair of colors of the palette, in the order of the first record of each value in the file. The palette is the eight colors `--strand-1` to `--strand-8` in `theme/beeson.css`, defined for each mode, and the pairs are `--strand-1` with `--strand-2`, `--strand-3` with `--strand-4`, and so on.
- With `data-strands-ramp`, you color each strand from the first color of its pair at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn records of the pair, to the second color at the largest.
- With `data-strands-shade`, you draw each strand in a shade of the first color of its pair instead. The shade is lighter at the smallest value of a key, e.g. `e`, among the drawn records of the pair, and darker at the largest. It is used in place of `data-strands-ramp` when both are given.
- With `data-strands-colormap`, you color the strands from a colormap of matplotlib in place of the palette, i.e. `viridis`, `plasma`, or `Blues_r`. A group of strands is a value of the key of `data-strands-color`, or the whole drawing without this attribute. In each group, the strand with the smallest value of the key of `data-strands-ramp` is drawn in the color of the lower end of the colormap, and the strand with the largest value in the color of the upper end. The ends are 0 and 0.85 for viridis and plasma and 0 and 0.683 for Blues_r, short of the yellow or white end, and you can give other ends after the name, e.g. `viridis 0.1 0.7`. In each mode, an end whose color has a contrast below 3:1 against `--bg` is moved toward the other end in steps of 0.01 until it reaches 3:1. Thus viridis is drawn from 0.29 to 0.85 in the dark mode, and from 0 to 0.57 in the light mode. A strand of a colormap is drawn opaque, hence its color on the slide is the color of a matplotlib figure, and each color between the ends keeps 3:1 against `--bg` as drawn. The colors are interpolated between 17 stops of each colormap, sampled from matplotlib and stored in `src/strands.ts`. With a colormap, `data-strands-shade` is ignored after a warning in the console. With a name other than these three, or with malformed ends, the strands are colored from the palette after a warning.
- With `data-stagger`, you start the strands one after another, in the order of the file, with the given number of seconds between two starts, e.g. `0.4`. It works on a named drawing too.
- With `data-heads`, you draw a moving head on every strand, at the size `full` or `small`, or on none with `none`. Without it, a head is drawn only on the path of a spacecraft, and on a record of a file with a `head` of its own. Such a path is a record of a file with `"craft": true`.
- With `data-duration`, you set the seconds in which the whole drawing is traced, from the first strand to the last, 12 by default. The clock of a file runs in these seconds, hence with `data-duration` equal to the last time on the clock, `start` and `t` are in seconds.

Without `data-half`, the window of a backdrop of a file is fitted to its drawing with the fill of `tubes` on the closing slide. In the window, 93 percent of the drawing is held, measured along each strand with every strand counted alike. The drawing is also kept 40 px clear of each side of the box that is inside the slide.

The title slide of the demo deck draws `brand/transfer.js`, the low-thrust transfer of the logo of the group, from a geostationary transfer orbit to a low lunar orbit through the L1 halo orbit. The transfer was computed by DyLAN, the software of the group, and written as polylines by a script beside its demonstration of the transfer, under the oblique view of the logo, fitted to its ink. In the file, every y coordinate is then multiplied by 1.19, hence the ring of tubes around the Earth is round, as in the logo. One head flies the transfer over the 20 s, in proportion to its time of flight, with the thrust arcs in orange and the coasts in violet. The stable and unstable tubes of the halo orbit are traced as hairlines from the orbit outward, from 0.8 s on. Their tips are thus at the end of the thrust at 5.8 s, together with the head, where the coast on the stable arc begins. Each tube is continued beyond the arcs of the demonstration, by 6 TU on the stable and 4 TU on the unstable side, and dimmed to nothing over the continuation. The two arcs on the tubes are drawn at full width and with a glow as they are flown, the orange path glows throughout, and after the flight the drawing rests complete until the slide is changed. The swings of the Earth leg that the logo does not show are cut out of the path. The window puts the drawing in the right part of the slide, beside the title block. In a talk made from the template, this backdrop is kept with the tag of its title slide, and the file is distributed only with the marks, see `brand/NOTICE.md`.

## Surfaces

A `surface` is the graph of a function $z = f(x, y)$ over a rectangle, drawn in 3D with three.js under a camera that turns slowly and can be turned with a drag. Set the function with `data-function`, and its parameters with further data attributes. Each function is defined in `src/surfaces.ts` with its stages, i.e. what is drawn at each step of the slide. Give a fragment of the same slide the attribute `data-surface-stage`, and the surface is moved to this stage when the fragment is shown, and back when it is hidden. At a stage, several sheets may be shown, i.e. layers of the function stacked at different heights, each in its own color ramp. A change of stage is animated. The heights of each sheet are morphed, a sheet that is added is split from the first sheet or grown from a flat sheet, and a sheet that is removed is merged into the first sheet or flattened onto it. The marks are grown and faded in place on the first sheet, drawn over the surface, and dimmed where the surface is in front of them, and a cutting plane is slid to its height. Label a mark with a `lab` span whose `data-at` is the name of the mark. To point at some of the marks, give the fragment that sets the stage the attribute `data-surface-marks` with their names, e.g. `L1 L2 L3`. The other marks and their labels are then dimmed to 18 percent. The marks named in `data-surface-accent` are filled in orange, those named in `data-surface-violet` in violet, and their labels keep the color of the text. A fragment that changes only these attributes and repeats the stage of the step before is drawn as a fade of the marks with a time constant of 0.15 s, and the sheets are left as they are. The demo slide "Color the terms and the marks" is an example of each attribute. In a deck with a surface, load `theme/vendor/three/three.js` before `theme/beeson.js`.

| Function | Attributes | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| `effective-potential` | `data-mu`, the mass ratio $\mu$ of the circular restricted three-body problem, 0.05 by default, and `data-C` on the fragment of the cut, the Jacobi constant $C$, that of L1 by default | `wells` in Blues, `bowl` with the centrifugal dome in Reds above the wells, `sum` with the two merged in Magma_r (Magma reversed) without its darkest 30 %, `extrema` with L1 to L5 marked, and `cut` with the plane at $-C/2$ and the zero-velocity curve |

The camera is set with `data-azimuth` and `data-elevation` in degrees, `data-turn` in seconds per turn (0 for a still camera), and `data-zoom`, and the heights are scaled with `data-zscale`. A surface whose data attributes other than `data-stage` are those of a surface on the slide before is shown under the camera of that surface, and its turn is continued across the change of slide. With `?still`, a stage is drawn at once and the camera is not turned. In a PDF and in PowerPoint, each step is printed with a still of its stage.

## Figures

For a figure made with matplotlib, use the styles `beeson-dark.mplstyle` and `beeson-light.mplstyle` in `styles/`. The palette, the typeface, and a transparent background are set in these styles. Export one file per mode and mark the images `for-dark` and `for-light`, so that the right one is shown in each mode. Give a white raster figure the class `paper`, and it is framed. In a `plate` or a `zone`, the paper is cut to the size of the image, which is scaled down to fit and is not enlarged. Export such a figure at the aspect of its box and at least at its pixel size, e.g. 1680 by 630 px for the plate of a figure slide. Give a black-on-white line plot the class `invert`, and it is inverted in dark mode.

An image, a video, a QR code, or a tycho viewer can be given a width in its `style`. An image or a video with a width is drawn at this width, even past the box of its zone or plate, and it keeps the aspect of its file. With a height in px as well, it is stretched to both. In the editor, the width is written with a drag of a grip of the selected picture, and on both images of a figure with an image for each mode. The height is written as well when the box Constrain Proportions of the Arrange pane is off, and you turn the box over for one drag by holding Shift on a corner. Crop an image with Edit Mask. The crop is written as `object-fit: cover` and `object-position` where the picture fills its box, and else as `scale`, a `clip-path` of the form `inset(N%)`, and `object-position`. A flip with Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical in the Arrange menu is written as a negative `scale`, e.g. `scale: -1 1`.

Math is typeset in SVG with MathJax 4 and its TeX font, i.e. Computer Modern, and the text is set in Latin Modern Roman, all vendored. The macros of a deck are defined in `theme/macros.js`.

To show a video, put it in a `zone` or a `plate`, and add `data-autoplay` to play it while its slide is shown. Give every video a `poster`, since the poster is printed in the PDF and `npm run check` fails without one. The clip of the demo deck is the manifolds example of tycho, exported by `web/scripts/video.mjs` of tycho in the colors of each mode and re-encoded at 30 frames per second with ffmpeg, as described in CONTRIBUTING.md of tycho.

To embed a scene of tycho, the Typed Celestial HTML Observer, call `build_embed` of pytycho, the Python package of tycho, and paste the inline `tycho-viewer` element into a slide. The scene and its data are in two inline blocks of this element. The runtime is vendored once in `theme/vendor/tycho/`, copied by hand from a build of pytycho, since tycho is not on npm. The viewer is set to the mode of the deck, the scene is played only while its slide is shown, and the keys used in the viewer are not passed to the deck. A viewer with `data-keep-theme` keeps the theme written in the file through a flip of the mode, for a scene whose marks are drawn for one background. In a PDF and in PowerPoint, the viewer is replaced by a still of the scene at its first frame, placed where the panels of the viewer are, and every step of the slide is printed with the same still. Pass `kiosk=True` for a viewer without controls. Run `demo/tycho-scene.py` to write the viewer of the demo deck. This script is also the example to start from.

## QR codes

A QR code is a `div` with the class `qr` and its text in `data-qr`, e.g. `<div class="qr" data-qr="https://example.com/slides.pdf"></div>`. The code is drawn into the div as an inline SVG when the deck is started. It is square at the width of the div, which is 320 px by default and 300 px at the lower right of a title or closing slide. Set another width in the `style` of the div, e.g. `style="width: 400px"`. The encoder is in `src/qr.ts` and is bundled into `theme/beeson.js`, hence nothing is loaded for a code. The text is encoded as UTF-8 at error correction level M, at which about 15 percent of the code can be restored, in the smallest version with room for it, up to 2331 bytes.

The modules are drawn dark on a light tile in both modes, since an inverted code is not read in many scanner apps. The tile is the off-white of the text in dark mode and white in light mode, with slightly rounded corners and a quiet zone of four modules, and its colors are the custom properties `--qr-ink` and `--qr-tile` of `theme/beeson.css`. The code is drawn the same in the slides, with `?still`, in the PDF, and in PowerPoint. In the editor, it is moved by a drag as a figure, and it can be given a step in the Steps pane. An empty or too long `data-qr` is shown as a message in place of the code, and `npm run check` fails on it. Keep the address short, since a shorter address is drawn with fewer and larger modules, which are read from farther back in the room. The closing slide of the demo deck is an example.

## Stepped math

A display is revealed in four steps: the lead-in sentence, the display, the sentence in which it is explained, and a step in which a symbol of the display is colored. Mark the symbol in the TeX with `\class{name}{...}`, and give a later fragment the attribute `data-highlight="name"`. Once this fragment is shown, the symbols of the class are drawn in orange, or in violet, blue, or red with `data-tone` set to `violet`, `blue`, or `red`. Any element of the slide with the class `name` is colored as well, e.g. a `span` around the words that name the symbol. To take the color off a class at a later step, give that fragment `data-unhighlight="name"`, and the class is drawn in the color of the text until a step after it names the class again in `data-highlight`. Use orange for motion and violet for structure, as in the rest of the theme, and blue and red for the terms drawn in Blues and Reds on a surface, and separate several classes with spaces.

An earlier fragment is dimmed to 45 percent once a later one is shown, and every fragment is restored at the step added after the last one. Give a fragment without an index the attribute `data-final`, e.g. a QR code, to show it at this step in place of the added one, i.e. once every fragment is restored. Give the display the attribute `data-keep` to hold it at full opacity through the next step, or `data-keep="4"` to hold it through the step of index 4. A display is also held at full opacity while a class in it is colored at the current step. The demo slide "Stepping through the equations" is an example of every step.

## Code

A code box is a `pre` with a `code` in it, and it is colored with highlight.js in the highlight plugin of reveal.js. Load `theme/vendor/reveal/highlight.js` after `notes.js`, and give the `code` the class of its language, e.g. `language-python`, since the language is guessed in a box without one. With the class `nohighlight`, a box is left uncolored. The tokens are colored from the palette of the theme in each mode, and the code is set in the system monospace at 22 px. At this size, about 24 lines can be shown under a heading, and about 29 on a slide without one. A line can be up to about 65 characters long in the text column, and up to about 115 on a slide with the class `wide`. For a longer box, set a smaller `font-size` in its `style`. In the editor, choose Smaller in the Format menu to write `font-size: 18px` on the `pre`, and Bigger to take it off, or set and clear the box Small in the Code pane.

To step through a box, number its lines with `data-line-numbers` on the `code`, and write one group of lines per step, separated by bars, e.g. `data-line-numbers="1-3|4|5-7"`. The first group is lit when the slide is shown, and each later group is lit at its step, while the other lines are dimmed to 35 percent. The whole box is dimmed at a step outside it after its last group, and it is restored at the step added at the end of the slide. In the print view, each group is printed on a separate page. While the deck is edited, the box is shown plain at the same size, since the text of the page is paired with the file. The demo slide "Code, one group of lines per step" is an example, cut from `demo/tycho-scene.py`.

## Plots

A plot is an animated 2D figure, drawn as SVG from a block of JSON in the deck. Put a `div` with the class `plot` in a `zone`, or with the classes `plot plate` on a `figure` slide, and give it the id of the block in `data-plot`. Write the block next to it as `<script type="application/json" id="...">`. The block is inline, since fetching a file is blocked for a deck opened with a double click. The demo deck does not have a plot, and the test decks `test/pages/plot-steps.html` and `test/pages/plot-reveals.html` are examples to start from.

The plot is played in a loop while its slide is shown, and it is paused when the slide is left. Under the plot, a button to play and pause, a scrubber with a tick at each event, and the value of the parameter are shown, and the plot is paused when you drag the scrubber. In the PDF and in PowerPoint, the plot is printed at its last frame. Set another frame with `data-plot-frame`, where a negative frame is counted from the end, and set `data-plot-paused` to show the still in the slides as well. To start the plot at a step, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-play`. The plot is then held at its still frame until the fragment is shown and played from its first frame afterward, and it is printed at its last frame from this step on. Under reduced motion, the plot is not played.

To reveal a plot in steps, give a fragment of its slide the attribute `data-plot-show` with the ids of panels, marks, or elements, separated by spaces. Each of these is hidden until its fragment is shown. With `data-plot-tab` on a fragment, the plot is switched to the named tab, hence each tab is given a page of its own in the PDF. An empty `span` is enough for a fragment without text. To show one block on several slides, give each slide an element of its own, with the ids of its panels in `data-plot-panels` and the names of the tabs it offers in `data-plot-tabs`. A tab named in a `data-plot-show` is offered once its fragment is shown, hence give the panels, marks, elements, and tabs distinct names.

These keys are read from the block.

| Key | Value |
|---|---|
| `panels` | A list of panels, each with an `id`, the axes `x` and `y`, and an optional `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels`. An axis is `{ "range": [a, b], "ticks": [...], "title": "..." }`, and a tick is a number or `{ "at": v, "text": "..." }` with a label in the markup. Set `"scale": "asinh"` on an axis to draw a value v at asinh(v/u), where u is the `unit` of the axis, 1 by default. Set `"equal": true` for one scale on both axes, and `"axes": false` to leave the axes out. The `size` of a panel is its share of the plot, 1 by default, and its `margin` is `[top, right, bottom, left]` in pixels. |
| `marks` | A list of the animated marks, each with an `id`, a `panel`, and a `kind`. The data of a mark are given in each tab. |
| `tabs` | A list of named data sets, each with a `name`, a `label` for its button, the `values` of the parameter at the frames, the `data` of the marks by their ids, an optional list of `times`, an optional `active` list, and optional `panels`. In `panels`, the `x`, `y`, `title`, `regions`, `curves`, and `labels` given for a panel id replace those of the panel while the tab is shown. |
| `parameter` | `{ "label": "$A$", "digits": 3 }`, the name of the parameter and the digits of its value. |
| `duration`, `hold`, `fade` | The seconds of a pass over the frames of a tab without `times`, 8 by default, the seconds for which the last frame is held, 1 by default, and the seconds of the crossfade at the start of the next pass, 0.5 by default and at most a pass. In the crossfade, the moving marks of the next pass are faded in while the held last frame is faded out. A text of the held frame is faded out in the first half of the crossfade and a text of the next pass in the second half, so that two texts are not drawn over each other. |
| `layout` | `row` or `column`, the direction of the panels. By default, the panels are in a row in a wide box and in a column in a tall one. |

The data of a mark are given with one entry per value of the tab, or with one entry for a mark that does not move. Between two frames, the positions are interpolated, and a text is taken from the nearest frame.

Without `times`, the frames of a tab are shown at even steps over `duration`. With `times`, each frame is shown at its time in seconds into the pass, from 0 and increasing, hence a tab can be slowed near an event, and a frame given twice with a later time is held still. The scrubber and its ticks are placed by these times.

| Kind | Entry | Drawing |
|---|---|---|
| `points` | A flat list `[x1, y1, x2, y2, ...]` | A dot at each point, filled, or a ring with `"hollow": true` |
| `trace` | A point `[x, y]` | The whole path in a faint line, left out with `"ghost": false`, and the path up to the current frame in full with a dot at its end |
| `label` | `{ "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, with one position and one text, or a list of each | The text at the position |
| `events` | A list of `{ "frame": n, "at": [x, y], "text": "..." }`, not one per frame | A ring and its text from the frame on, and a tick on the scrubber in the tone of the mark |

The faint line of a trace runs over the whole path, unless the data of the trace are given as `{ "points": [...], "ghost": n }`, with the list of points in `points`. The faint line then ends at frame n, and the rest of the path is drawn only as it is traced.

A region is a polygon of `points`, filled at `opacity`. A curve is a polyline of `points` or a `circle` `[cx, cy, r]`, with a `width` and a `dash`. A label is a `text` at `at`, with an `anchor` (`start`, `middle`, or `end`) and an offset `dx`, `dy` in pixels. A mark is drawn with a `size`, a `width`, an `anchor`, `dx`, and `dy` as well. Give each element and mark a `tone`, i.e. a color of the theme without its dashes (`accent`, `violet`, `text`, `subtle`, `muted`, or `data-1` to `data-8`), and the plot is drawn right in both modes. A `class` on an element or a mark is a target of `data-highlight`, as in stepped math, and the element keeps its color when a tab is chosen. In `active`, list the ids of the elements emphasized at each frame. The other ids named in the active list of any tab are dimmed at the same frame.

Text in the JSON is set in a small markup rather than with MathJax, so that a plot is drawn the same in the slides, the PDF, and PowerPoint. Math is written between `$` signs, with italic letters, `_` and `^`, braces, `\mathrm{}`, `\text{}`, `\mathcal{}` for the script capitals of Unicode, Greek letters, and a few symbols such as `\to`, and a hyphen is set as a minus sign there. A fault in the block is shown in place of the plot with its path, e.g. `tabs[0].data.m: 3 frames are given, and the tab has 2 values`, and `npm run check` fails on it.

## Browsers

You can show a deck in the current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, and in the browsers built on their engines, on a computer, a phone, or a tablet. This holds for both modes, the steps, the speaker view, and the viewer of tycho, whether you open the file with a double click or from a web server. All browsers on iOS are built on the engine of Safari. These are the limits.

- In Safari on a Mac, the paper size of the deck is ignored, and the PDF is printed on the paper chosen in the print dialog. Choose a paper of 16:9, or export with `npm run pdf`. The backdrops are also printed fainter than in the other browsers.
- On an iPhone, fullscreen is not available, since the fullscreen API is missing there.
- On a phone or a tablet, the speaker view is opened with the key `s`, hence you need a keyboard. A deck is opened from a web server, such as the site of the repository, since a double click on a file does not apply there. Printing is not tested.
- In every browser, `\require{bbm}`, `\require{bboldx}`, and `\require{dsfont}` show the error "Extension failed to load", since the fonts of these three extensions of MathJax are not vendored.

## PowerPoint

At some conferences, a speaker is given a laptop that shows only PowerPoint and PDF files. For such a room, run `npm run pptx`, which writes `slides.pptx` next to `slides.pdf`. Each step of the deck is a full-slide image, and a click in PowerPoint is a step of the deck. The text cannot be edited in this file, and the backdrops are static.

Each video is placed over its image as a PowerPoint video, and it is started when its step is shown. The steps with a video are placed on one PowerPoint slide, hence the video goes on playing through them. PowerPoint needs an MP4 file, and the export fails on a video without one. The speaker notes are copied to the PowerPoint notes. Keynote opens a `.pptx`, hence a separate Keynote export is not needed.

## Tooling

For `npm test`, `npm run check`, `npm run pdf`, and `npm run pptx`, Chrome is looked up at its default location on macOS and Windows, and on the `PATH` on Linux. Edge is used on Windows when Chrome is missing. To use another browser build, set `CHROME_PATH` to its executable.
To run the check in another engine, use `npm run check -- --browser firefox` or `npm run check -- --browser safari`. Firefox is driven with `geckodriver`, which is looked up in `GECKOWEBDRIVER` and on the `PATH`, and `FIREFOX_PATH` selects another Firefox build. Safari is driven with `safaridriver`, which you enable once with `sudo safaridriver --enable`. Quit Safari before a run, since a session cannot be started in a Safari that is already open. A page from `file://` is refused under automation, hence the deck is served from a local port in Safari, and a double click is not covered by this check. The problems in the page are reported in all three browsers, but the console is read only in Chrome, and the phone layout, a pixel ratio of 1.25, and a save of the editor are checked only in Chrome. On Linux, run Firefox under `xvfb-run`, since WebGL in software is available there only in a window.

If you copied `theme/` and `brand/` by hand, copy `.agents/`, `.claude/`, `AGENTS.md`, `scripts/`, and `package.json` as well. You can then run `npm run check`, `npm run pdf`, and `npm run pptx` without `npm ci`.
